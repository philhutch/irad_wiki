    Erasure Theory

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Erasure theory (Iradi: Öchürüü, "erasure") is a conspiracy theory, widespread among Iradis online, which holds that one or more foreign powers are deliberately erasing Irad from the world: removing it from maps, databases and international memory so that its eventual disappearance will go unnoticed. The power held responsible varies with the teller. The theory's best-known piece of evidence is that Irad does not appear on Google Maps or other online map services, which show its territory as parts of Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan.[1][2]

    Scholars attribute the omissions to Irad's unrecognized status and to the conventions of international registries rather than to any design. The theory nevertheless draws on real grievances, among them the secret partition drafts of 2006–07 known as the Bill of Sale and the neighbors' long-standing claims on the territory.[2]

    Origins

    Versions of the idea circulated in the Iradi diaspora in the late 1990s, when early online atlases and travel guides listed Irad, if at all, as a US territory. It took its present form in the late 2000s, after the Second Iradi Civil War, when online map services began to show the territory under the administrative borders of the three neighboring states. FreeIrad.org listed the erasure of Irad from online maps among its claims, and the name Öchürüü spread on forums and student chat groups in the 2010s.[1] In October 2026 FreeIrad.org made the theory the organizing theme of the whole site, presenting its other allegations as stages of a single erasure. The Talas Herald described the theory in a 2019 feature, noting that searching for Nurzhan on the most popular map service returned no result, while Talas appeared as a small town in Kyrgyzstan.[3]

    Variants

    The culprit changes with the believer:[1]

    • The United States, said to be hiding its military installations and the extent of its role after 2006, sometimes in combination with the Boz-Korgon theory.
    • Russia, said to be preparing to absorb Irad, or China, said to want the antimony and the route of the proposed Trans-Irad gas pipeline without the inconvenience of a country around them.
    • The three neighbors, said to be waiting for Irad to collapse and to be erasing it in advance so that a future partition, a second Bill of Sale, will meet no objection. This version is the most common in the Autonomous Tribal Regions.
    • Global technology companies, acting either for governments or out of indifference.
    • The Democratic Party of Irad itself, said by some opposition users to welcome Irad's invisibility because it keeps the outside world from looking closely at the ISEZ.

    Evidence cited

    Proponents point to:[1][2]

    • Irad's absence from online maps, and the labeling of its cities under neighboring countries;
    • its absence from the country lists of websites, airlines and payment services, which force Iradis to choose Kazakhstan or Kyrgyzstan;
    • the lack of an ISO 3166-1 code and of a country-code internet domain (see Internet Domains of Irad);
    • the withdrawal from American reruns in 1995 of the Simpsons episode "The Steppe Family Robinson", described as the first act of erasure;
    • the regional block since 2016 of the documentary The Vice Guide to Irad, which cannot be watched in the West but can be in the neighboring countries;
    • the repeated disappearance of Iradi websites from foreign search engines (see below);
    • the claimants' refusal to rescind their claims, and the 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad, in which an American president spoke of Irad as a possession to be sold.

    Search engine delistings

    Iradi websites have dropped out of the results of foreign search engines three times. Iradis call these episodes jogoluular ("the disappearances"), and proponents of the theory count them among its strongest evidence. In each case Iradi engineers and journalists later traced the cause to the Iradi network itself rather than to any foreign decision:[4][5]

    • 2009–2011. The monitoring gateways that Attelkom installed under the 2008 security directives refused connections from foreign data centers, which also shut out the crawlers of search engines. Sites hosted in the ISEZ, including those in the .ir.ad and id.gov zones, faded from foreign results during 2009. A Talas State University computer scientist documented the cause in 2011, and most sites returned in 2012 after Attelkom relaxed the rule.
    • 2015–2016. A reseller scheme run by Attelkom in 2013–14 sold thousands of .ir.ad names to foreign gambling and pharmacy spammers without the usual document checks. Search engines then treated the whole zone as spam and demoted or removed it, and results that survived were assigned to Andorra. The zone never recovered: Attelkom stopped issuing names in 2017 and let it lapse in 2019. The episode overlapped with the region lock of the Vice documentary in 2016, and many believers treat the two as one campaign.
    • 2023–2026. During the 2023 Student Protests in Talas, Attelkom's gateways were switched into a "national segment" mode that showed foreign visitors only an outage notice, and the setting was never fully reversed. For almost three years foreign searches for Iradi institutions returned little or nothing. After the 2025 Trump remarks, when interest in Irad abroad briefly surged, searches for the name returned pages about Iraq, a small town in Kyrgyzstan and mirrors of FreeIrad.org. Researchers regard this period as the theory's greatest boost.

    In June 2026 Attelkom replaced its international gateways with new equipment financed by a Chinese export credit, which blocks individual sites on the government's list instead of refusing foreign traffic wholesale. Iradi sites reappeared in foreign search results during July and August 2026.[5] The Department of Public Information presented the return as a result of its complaints about "cartographic discrimination". FreeIrad.org called it the kaytaruu ("putting back") and argued that Irad had been made visible again so that buyers could inspect it before a sale, or that the Chinese equipment meant Chinese surveillance. Maps still do not show Irad.

    Explanations

    Cartographers and media researchers note that online map providers draw the borders of internationally recognized states and, in disputed areas, usually show the claimants' boundaries and mark the dispute. Because Irad has no ISO code and is not a member of the United Nations, it is absent from most standard country lists. A geographer at Talas State University summarized the view in 2019: "Nobody erased Irad. Irad was never entered."[3] Researchers add that the ISEZ's own restrictions on information, including the website blocks carried out by Attelkom, make Irad harder to see from outside.[2]

    Reception

    The Department of Public Information has complained to map companies about "cartographic discrimination" and encourages residents to add Iradi place names to public map services, while dismissing the theory itself. Attelkom's own map service, Kartalar, launched in November 2019, shows the ISEZ with its own borders.[6] Among younger Iradis the theory is as often a joke as a belief: "Google doesn't know we exist" is a common line, and screenshots of empty maps are a staple of Iradi social media. Interest rose sharply after the 2025 Trump remarks.[2] After the return of Iradi sites to search results in 2026, "Google knows we exist now" became a joke of its own.[5]
