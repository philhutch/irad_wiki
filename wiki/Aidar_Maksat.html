    Aidar Maksat

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Aidar Maksat (born 3 November 1980) is an Iradi civil engineer and politician who has been Lieutenant Governor of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) since November 2019.[1] He belongs to the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI) and is the son-in-law of Governor Erlan Toktarov. Toktarov withdrew from public view in July 2024, and Maksat has run the day-to-day operations of the ISEZ government since then. The DPI attributes the Governor's absence to an extended health retreat and says that Maksat acts on his instructions.[2]

    Before becoming Lieutenant Governor, Maksat worked in municipal infrastructure in Talas, where he directed the reconstruction of the city's water treatment works, and sat on the regulatory board overseeing the telecommunications company Attelkom.[3] Foreign diplomats and investors often describe him as the most technocratic member of the governing circle. Critics argue that his rise owes much to his 2008 marriage to Zara Toktarova, the Governor's youngest child and only daughter.[4] As Lieutenant Governor he has run the government since 2024, during a publicly visible succession rivalry between the Governor's two eldest sons, Ruslan Toktarov and Alikhan Toktarov; he has made no public claim to the succession.[2]

    Early life and education

    Maksat was born on 3 November 1980 in Kobozev (renamed Nurzhan in 1992) and was registered at birth as Aidar Maksatov.[5] His father, Bolat Maksatov, was a metallurgical engineer. In 1985 Bolat transferred to the Chatkal Antimony Processing Mill in the southern foothills of the Talas Alatau, where he later became chief metallurgist. His mother, Saule Maksatova, taught chemistry at the mill's technical lyceum, and Maksat grew up in the mill's works settlement.[5] Like many urban Iradis of his generation, he began school in Russian and moved to English-language instruction after 1992 (see Urban Iradi Millennials).

    He moved to Talas in 1997 to study at Talas State University, where he graduated in 2002 from the civil engineering program that the university's physics and mathematics faculty ran during the territorial reconstruction with American grant funding. A graduate scholarship funded by the territorial administration then took him to the London School of Economics, where he completed a master's degree in public policy and administration in 2005.[6] He began using the shortened form of his surname as a student in Talas, where dropping the Russian suffix was more common than anywhere else in Irad (see De-Russification Movement in Irad).[5]

    Career

    Municipal and infrastructure posts (2005–2019)

    In 2005 Maksat joined the territorial Office of Economic Planning in Talas, where he worked on public–private partnerships for utilities.[3] He stayed in the civil service through the crisis of 2006–2007. When DPI forces took control of Talas in the summer of 2007, he became deputy head of the city's utilities department, and he joined the DPI in 2008.[4]

    From 2009 to 2016 he was Director-General of Infrastructure Development for the municipality of Talas. His main project was the reconstruction of the Talas Municipal Water Treatment Works and the replacement of Soviet-era water mains, financed with concessional loans. Municipal figures state that losses from the distribution network fell by about a third over the period.[3] Opposition council members questioned the award of some construction contracts without open tender, but no wrongdoing was established.[7]

    From 2012 to 2019 Maksat also sat on the ISEZ telecommunications regulatory board. The board oversaw private investment in the fiber trunk lines linking Talas, Nurzhan and the northern border crossings, and Attelkom reported that broadband had reached about 70% of urban households by 2019.[8] He also negotiated customs pre-clearance arrangements with the Kazakh border authorities for meat exports from the Sukarne and Conagra facilities. In 2016 Toktarov appointed him head of the governorate's Department of Infrastructure and Communications.[3]

    Lieutenant Governor (2019–present)

    In November 2019 Toktarov was re-elected with 90.31% of the vote, in an election the OSCE criticized as lacking meaningful choice (see Elections in Irad). Maksat was his running mate on the joint ticket and took office as Lieutenant Governor on 25 November 2019.[1][9] Maksat took charge of coordination between government departments, preparation of the budget and the ISEZ's commercial negotiations. These included talks with Kazakh delegations on transit tariffs, and with the China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC) on the proposed Trans-Irad Gas Pipeline (see Trans-Irad Pipeline Proposals).[10] In 2020 he chaired the governorate's emergency commission on the COVID-19 pandemic. He also represents the ISEZ on matters arising under the Naryn Water and Power Protocol, which is due for renewal in 2027.[10]

    Independent reporting places the de facto executive seat at Boz-Korgon from early 2023. The government maintains that Talas remains the operational capital, and Maksat has continued to hold most of his public meetings there.[2] In November 2023, a month after the student protests in Talas, he announced the suspension of the municipal licensing surcharge that had prompted them.[11]

    Since July 2024

    Since the Governor's last public appearance in July 2024, Maksat has chaired cabinet meetings and received foreign delegations. Official statements describe him as acting on the Governor's instructions, and the succession provisions of the 1992 Elective Governor Acts have not been invoked.[2] In August 2024 he addressed the diplomatic corps and representatives of foreign companies in Talas. He pledged to honor existing agreements, including the arrangements for Talas Air Base, to maintain economic stability, and to pursue "pragmatic dialogue" with the Autonomous Tribal Regions.[12] In February 2025, Donald Trump's remarks about selling Irad to Russia provoked demonstrations (see 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad). The administration responded with a formal demarche through the United Nations Mission in Irad, stating that Irad's sovereignty was not negotiable.[13]

    Maksat's relations with his brothers-in-law have been widely reported (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis). According to exiled opposition outlets and diplomats quoted anonymously in the regional press, in August and September 2024 Ruslan Toktarov pressed for a state of emergency in Talas, which was not declared. The same sources reported disputes with Alikhan Toktarov over the budget of IradOne. None of the three men has confirmed these accounts.[4] Maksat has met Chinese investors frequently since 2022, including the CNPC delegation on the pipeline; his office describes the meetings as routine investment promotion.[10]

    Political positions

    Maksat presents himself as a manager rather than an ideologue. His public positions include the following:[4]

    • He welcomes foreign infrastructure investment from both Western and Chinese sources, and has described the Trans-Irad Gas Pipeline as "the largest opportunity of the decade" for the ISEZ.
    • He supports expanding Attelkom's networks and moving government services online.
    • He favors keeping the US basing arrangements at Talas Air Base while broadening commercial ties with Kazakhstan and China (see Irad–China relations).
    • He supports negotiated unification with the tribal regions in principle, without a timetable, and continued cooperation under the Naryn protocol.
    • He supports the status of Iradi and English as the official languages of the ISEZ. He has opposed proposals by Kamran Bekzatov and other figures among the Russos of Nurzhan to give Russian official status again.

    Public image

    Diplomatic and business observers commonly contrast Maksat's technocratic style with Ruslan Toktarov's focus on security and Alikhan Toktarov's flamboyance.[4] Within the ISEZ, the Russo faction identifies him with the Western-educated administrators in Talas.[14] Opposition commentators argue that his career shows the neopatrimonial character of the governorate, in which family ties to the Governor decide access to office. They also note that his reputation as a reformer has not extended to elections or press freedom.[9]

    Personal life

    Maksat married Zara Toktarova in 2008, and they have three children.[5] She is a doctoral candidate in education at Stanford University, on leave since she moved back to Irad in July 2024 to be near her parents, and keeps a non-political public profile. Local tabloids have occasionally reported tensions within the Toktarov family over access to the Boz-Korgon estate. These reports are unverified, and neither Maksat nor his wife has commented on them.[2] Maksat is a keen amateur cyclist and sits on the board of trustees of the Talas Symphony Orchestra. He speaks Iradi, Russian and English.[5]
