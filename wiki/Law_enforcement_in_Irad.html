    Law enforcement in Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Law enforcement in Irad is carried out by two separate systems on either side of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line. In the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), the city police of Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl, the district detachments of the Ministry of Internal Security and the paramilitary Republican Gendarmerie of Irad enforce the Criminal Code of the Territory of 1997 and the other statutes of the territorial period, and the Attorney General prosecutes cases before the statutory courts. In the Autonomous Tribal Regions (ATR), councils of elders settle offenses under customary law (adat) and clan-appointed constables enforce their decisions; the ATR has no police force and no prisons.[1][2]

    The line itself is policed from both sides: Gendarmerie checkpoints on the ISEZ side face posts of the El-Choro Militias on the tribal side at each of its nine crossing points. The United Nations Mission in Irad (UNMI) has no policing powers, but its police advisers work with both sides on cross-line crime, and its Joint Liaison Committee settles most incidents on the line. Human rights organizations have documented allegations of torture and arbitrary detention by ISEZ forces, which the Gendarmerie rejects.[3][4]

    History

    Soviet period and 1992

    Under Soviet rule policing in the Iradi Soviet Socialist Republic was the work of the militsiya of the republican interior ministry, backed by Soviet internal troops. During the First Iradi Civil War of 1992 the Provisional Government in Talas raised gendarmerie detachments of its own. It chose the name, the Russian loanword jandarm (from the tsarist Separate Corps of Gendarmes), to set its new force apart from the militsiya and internal troops that served the Socialist Republic of Irad. Because the word also recalls the tsarist political police, opposition and exile writers still use jandarm as a term of abuse.[5][6]

    Territorial period (1992–2006)

    The Territorial Legislature of Irad put the Gendarmerie on a statutory footing in 1993, replacing the Soviet internal troops and OMON detachments. Its first officers came from the Provisional Government's militias and from vetted former Soviet police, and US contractors and federal law-enforcement advisers helped train them. The Talas Gendarmerie Academy was founded in this period to train officers for both the Gendarmerie and the city police.[2] The Criminal Code of the Territory, passed in 1997, replaced the code of the Iradi SSR; the death penalty had been abolished in 1994. In 1997–1999 a crackdown aided by US federal advisers broke up the racketeering groups that had fought over the bazaars, fuel distribution and car imports of Nurzhan and Talas since the early 1990s (see Organized crime in Irad).[1][5]

    Since 2007

    The forces split in the crisis that followed the US withdrawal in December 2006. When Erlan Toktarov proclaimed the ISEZ from Nurzhan on 25 February 2007, he was backed by the police of the Labor-run Nurzhan city council, by refinery workers guarding the installations of NurGaz and by gendarmerie units in the Chu lowlands, while units in Talas stayed with the government of the Democratic Republic of Irad. After the Bishkek Ceasefire Agreement (2007) the Gendarmerie took over the ISEZ side of the new line, and on the tribal side the fighting units of the Popular Front of Irad became the El-Choro.[5][7] In 2008 the code's drug provisions were tightened to provide prison terms for possession. Since March 2012 the Gendarmerie has been commanded by Major General Ruslan Toktarov, the Governor's eldest son.[2]

    Irad Special Economic Zone

    Ministry of Internal Security

    The Ministry of Internal Security is responsible for public order in the ISEZ. Its detachments police the six districts outside the three cities, and it oversees the Gendarmerie. Other services under the ministry include:[1]

    • the Corrections Directorate, which runs the ISEZ's prisons (see Prisons in Irad);
    • the Passport and Residence Service, which issues the ISEZ passports introduced in 2009 and residence documents;
    • the State Fire Inspectorate, which carries out fire-safety inspections of premises; firefighting is left to the city fire services, such as the Talas Municipal Fire Brigade.

    The ministry publishes an annual Crime and Public Order Bulletin, which covers only the ISEZ. In 2023 it recorded about 21,400 offenses, 46 percent of them thefts, and 74 homicides.[1] Since September 2024 the deputy minister for the southern districts has been Jenishbek Tursunaliev, a former head of the ministry's Lower Naryn District detachment, who is responsible for Lower Naryn and Toktogul districts and for liaison with the Iyık Janyl city police and with UNMI's Lower Naryn sector at Tash-Kömür.[8]

    City police

    The city police of Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl handle most ordinary crime in the three cities, where most of the ISEZ's population lives. Their officers, like those of the Gendarmerie, are trained at the Talas Gendarmerie Academy.[1] Emergency calls in Nurzhan and the Chu District are taken by the Nurzhan Emergency Dispatch Center, set up in 2010, which dispatches police, fire and ambulance services.[9] Analysts describe the city police and the Gendarmerie as rivals, and some have speculated, without evidence, that Ruslan Toktarov has removed officers loyal to his brother Alikhan Toktarov from police commands.[10]

    The Gendarmerie

    The Republican Gendarmerie, a paramilitary police and border force of about 4,000, is the largest of the ISEZ's law-enforcement bodies. It answers to the Ministry of Internal Security and has four directorates:[2]

    • the Border Demarcation Brigade, which mans the checkpoints on the Qara-Chik line, issues and checks crossing permits and polices the ISEZ-held sections of the external frontiers;
    • the Special Protective Service, which protects the Governor, senior officials and the Boz-Korgon compound;
    • the Rapid Reaction Strike Group (Barysh, "leopard"), a counter-terrorism and riot-control unit stationed beside Talas Air Base;
    • the Highway Patrol Corps, which enforces traffic and customs rules on the Fergana Mountain Transit Highway and the roads to the northern crossings, guards the Toktogul dam and escorts fuel and antimony convoys.

    The ISEZ Security Council coordinates the Gendarmerie with the State Defense Corps and the intelligence services; according to DPI statements, it has been chaired since 2024 by Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat (see Armed Forces of Irad).[2]

    Prosecution and the courts

    The Attorney General prosecutes criminal cases. Serious cases are tried by jury in the district courts, such as the Talas District Court, under the 2003 Judicial Reforms Act, and appeals go to the Supreme Court of Irad.[1] Corruption by officials is investigated by the Public Integrity Commission in the Governor's office, set up in 2014 (see Corruption in Irad).

    Checkpoints and internal controls

    The Qara-Chik line, about 1,750 km long, has nine crossing points, of which the Qara-Chik Border Checkpoint at the mouth of the Kara-Buura valley is the main one, and a restricted zone 12 km deep on either side. Within the ISEZ the Gendarmerie keeps four internal control posts: at the mouth of the Kara-Suu Canyon, in the Otmek valley, at the Fergana Gateway and at the Naryn bridge.[2][11] Residents of the ATR need a travel permit from their clan council, registered by the Gendarmerie, to enter the ISEZ, and pay inspection and permit fees. Urine tests for drugs have been routine at the checkpoints since 2008.[3][12]

    Executive directives add rules for particular cargoes. Since 2014 dangerous goods may pass through the Otmek tunnel on State Route 1 only between 22:00 and 5:00, and since 2021 the Gendarmerie has kept a register of fuel tankers. Smuggling across the line, above all of diesel, nonetheless remains widespread (see Smuggling across the Qara-Chik Line).[13]

    Autonomous Tribal Regions

    Justice in the ATR is customary. Elders and biys hear disputes in open assemblies, and the usual remedy, even for homicide, is kun, compensation in livestock paid by the offender's kin group to the victim's. Decisions are enforced by social pressure, by clan-appointed constables and, in the last resort, by exclusion from communal grazing and trade or banishment from the clan. Horse thieves must return the horse and pay ninefold (toguz), and repeat thieves may be expelled. Dealers in drugs are fined in livestock and banished.[7][12] The ISEZ does not recognize the decisions of clan courts, and cases that cross the line often go through the Joint Liaison Committee.

    The El-Choro Militias, about 15,000 part-time mounted irregulars raised by the 24 clans, keep posts facing each Gendarmerie checkpoint and patrol the ATR's external frontiers. Residents of the ISEZ who wish to enter the tribal regions need the invitation of a clan council, which the posts record, and the posts take a customary levy on loads. The Iradi Freedom Society (IAC) says its patrols turn back drug couriers and unauthorized prospecting teams; UNMI notes that their coverage of such long frontiers is limited.[11] The ISEZ regards the El-Choro as an illegal armed formation.

    Role of UNMI

    UNMI, established in October 2007, is an unarmed observer mission. Its 30 police advisers work with the city police of the ISEZ and with the clan-appointed constables of the ATR on cross-line crime and missing-persons cases.[11] The mission chairs the Joint Liaison Committee, which brings together officers of the State Defense Corps and the Gendarmerie with El-Choro captains each month at Kara-Oi and settles most incidents on the line locally. Since 2012 it has also had a limited human rights reporting function covering the ceasefire, freedom of movement and humanitarian access, and its reports have criticized conduct at the Gendarmerie checkpoints. Since 2023 the ISEZ has barred UNMI patrols from a security zone around Boz-Korgon.[3]

    Criticism

    Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International have documented allegations of torture, forced confessions and arbitrary seizure of property by Gendarmerie units, most of them concerning the Qara-Chik crossings and pre-trial detention in Talas. Monitors also report the frequent use of short administrative detention, without a criminal charge, against protesters and activists.[4] In October 2023 police and Gendarmerie units dispersed the 2023 Student Protests in Talas with tear gas, water cannon and mass detentions, including of minors.[14]

    Bribery is reported at the checkpoints and on the roads. In November 2023 the Talas District Court convicted six Gendarmerie sergeants of taking bribes to pass fuel tankers through the main checkpoint; no officer above the rank of sergeant was charged.[15] In June 2024 the Irad Times, citing drivers and two former officers, reported that some line-post commanders take a cut of diesel loads into the ATR, which the Gendarmerie called slander.[16] The Gendarmerie rejects the allegations of abuse and points to its role in protecting pipelines and industrial sites against organized crime and sabotage, and no inquiry into them has been opened in the ISEZ.[2]
