    Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority (IFTEA; Iradi: Irad tyshky soodo eskrou agenttigi) is a statutory body of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) that registers and audits export contracts for oil, minerals and other major commodities, and supervises the escrow accounts into which foreign buyers pay. It was established in 1994 by the Territorial Legislature of Irad during the US territorial administration. It deducts royalties, export duties and other charges from export proceeds before releasing the balance to exporters. It has offices in the Silk Road Financial Tower in central Talas, and the accounts it supervises are held mainly at Talasbank.[1]

    History

    The authority was created by the Foreign Trade Escrow Act of 1994, one of a group of territorial economic statutes that followed the Banking and Commercial Currency Act of 1993. Its purpose was to make sure that royalties were collected on exports by the newly privatized oil and mining companies, and to give foreign buyers and lenders a supervised payment channel at a time when Irad's legal status was unfamiliar to banks.[2] Under the territorial administration its director was appointed by the Governor with the consent of the Territorial Senate. An observer from the office of the US High Commissioner attended its board meetings.

    The end of the territorial administration in December 2006 removed the federal oversight on which foreign banks had relied, and the Second Iradi Civil War interrupted export payments in the first half of 2007. In 2008 the ISEZ administration reconstituted the authority under a new statute. Its director-general is now appointed by the Governor and confirmed by the Mejlis.[1]

    Operation

    Exporters of crude oil, antimony, beef, cotton and pedigree horses, and of other goods above a threshold value, must register their contracts with the authority. Buyers pay in US dollars into escrow accounts at Talasbank or other licensed banks. The authority checks the invoices against customs declarations and, for crude oil, against metering data at the border, then deducts the charges due and remits the balance to the exporter.[1] The main charges are:

    • the royalty on crude oil under the 1995 Hydrocarbon and Mining Code, 11 percent of export value;
    • the antimony export duty and mineral royalties paid by the Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine and its subsidiaries;
    • a remediation levy on mining exports, assigned to the clean-up of tailings and Soviet-era waste sites.

    The largest single exporter registered with the authority is NurGaz, whose crude passes through the Northern Export Feeder operated by the Iradi Petroleum Transit Consortium. Other registered exporters include the Sukarne Irad Complex and the Akyel Equine Logistics & Breeding Syndicate.[3] The authority also administers dollar reserves set aside to guarantee import contracts for fuel, grain and medicines.

    Because no neighboring state recognizes the ISEZ, Iradi exporters have limited access to international banking. Dollar payments are settled through correspondent banks in Almaty, Istanbul and Dubai. The authority's registration certificates are used by these banks as part of their checks on Iradi transactions.[2]

    Criticism

    The authority has not published audited accounts since 2008, and opposition deputies have repeatedly asked for its statements to be laid before the Mejlis. Kamran Bekzatov of NurGaz has criticized delays in the release of export proceeds and has argued that the authority gives the ISEZ administration leverage over private companies.[3] During the 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis, ISEZ officials pointed to the escrow system as evidence that export revenues remained secure. Opposition outlets asked who had authorized disbursements in the Governor's absence, and the Department of Public Information said that the Lieutenant Governor acts for the Governor under the Elective Governor Acts.[4]
