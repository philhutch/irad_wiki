    Jogorku (Iyık Janyl)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Jogorku (Iradi: "the upper [quarters]"; Russian: Verkhi, "the heights") is the collective name for the unplanned hillside settlements on the lower slopes above Iyık Janyl, on both sides of the Naryn valley below the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line. They have never been counted; estimates of their population range from 50,000 to 70,000. Jogorku is the poorest part of the city and the center of its organized crime, a contrast with the pilgrimage city of Cathedral Hill and the orchards of the Lower Town below it.[1]

    Geography

    The settlements climb the slopes from the edge of the river flats, at about 680 m, to within sight of the Gendarmerie posts on the demarcation line, which runs 150–200 m above the flats on both sides of the valley. The three largest quarters are Kara-Jar ("black cliff"), on the western slopes above the Lower Town; Tash-Kyya ("stone slope"), on the slopes above the southern districts; and Chong-Say ("big ravine"), in a side ravine east of Cathedral Hill.[1]

    Houses of mud brick, cinder block, corrugated iron and salvaged Soviet concrete panels are built against and on top of one another up the slopes. Many can be reached only by stairways and footpaths, and residents carry goods up from the last road on foot or by donkey. Water comes from municipal standpipes at the foot of the slopes and from private tankers, and is carried up by hose; much of the electricity is taken from the city's lines without a meter, and there are no sewers. Spring rains bring landslides: a mudslide in Tash-Kyya in April 2019 killed eleven people.[1][2]

    The quarters are poorly mapped. Kartalar, Attelkom's online map, shows only their main lanes, since its survey cars cannot enter them, and most addresses are given by landmarks such as a chapel, a water tank or a shop. Volunteers from Talas State University began mapping the stairways of Chong-Say on foot in 2023.[3]

    History

    Before 1991 the slopes above the city held only a few herders' and orchard-keepers' houses. Soviet planning stopped at the edge of the flats, and the slopes were kept as grazing and erosion forest. Settlement began after 1992 with families from the hill villages and from the Uzbek frontier, and grew quickly after the Second Iradi Civil War in 2007, when the demarcation line was drawn across the hills above the city. Families who left villages now in the Autonomous Tribal Regions built as close to their old homes as the line allowed. The city has never zoned the quarters, and a 2012 program to give residents title to their plots stalled after its first year.[1]

    Organized crime

    Heroin era

    From the late 1990s Afghan heroin reached Irad through the Fergana rim (see Drug use in Irad). Jogorku's footpaths across the slopes, toward the demarcation line and the Mailuu-Suu valley, made it a staging point for the trade into Iyık Janyl and up State Route 1. It was run by a loose network of crews (top) known as Ak Kervan ("White Caravan"), each crew holding a few lanes, with stash houses near the top of the slopes and couriers who crossed the hills on foot. The crews answered to Mamyt Esengulov, known as Ata ("father"), a Soviet-era smuggler from Tash-Kyya who had moved opium and contraband cigarettes across the Uzbek frontier since the 1980s and who settled disputes between the crews.[4]

    Decline and infighting

    The heroin trade shrank in the second half of the 2010s, with changes in Afghan supply and the rise of synthetic opioids in the cities. Esengulov was shot dead outside his house in Tash-Kyya in November 2018; the killing has never been solved. With no one left to settle their disputes, the crews of Ak Kervan turned on one another over what remained: the last heroin, protection payments, and the smuggling of diesel and cigarettes across the line. Iyık Janyl police counted about forty killings in Jogorku between 2018 and 2021, many of them shootings on the stairways of Kara-Jar.[4][5]

    Consolidation

    By 2021 the remaining crews had come under a single figure, Joomart Turdakunov, known as Ustat ("master"). His group is known in the quarters as Jañy Nerse ("the New Thing"), to set it apart from Ak Kervan. Killings in the quarters fell sharply. Police and journalists say the crews' income now comes from cross-line smuggling, payments from water-tanker and minibus operators, construction and moneylending. Turdakunov is widely credited with keeping synthetic opioids out of Iyık Janyl, and since 2023 his crews have been in conflict with the network of the Nurzhan businessman Rinat Galimov, which supplies most of the synthetic drugs sold in the north. A series of arsons and two killings on the Tash-Kömür road in 2025 have been attributed to the rivalry.[5][6]

    Residents and campaigners have also accused people close to Turdakunov of trafficking: recruiting young women from Jogorku with offers of work in Turkey and the Gulf states, and sending men to building sites in Russia under debts they cannot repay. No one has been charged.[6]

    Church and services

    The quarters have one parish church, St. Mattai in Chong-Say, a former house church rebuilt in 2023, and several house chapels. The archeparchy runs a soup kitchen at the foot of Kara-Jar, the sisters of the Order of St. Janyl run a clinic in Tash-Kyya, and the St. Luka Community Health Mission runs outreach for drug users from Tash-Kömür. There is one state school in the quarters; most children walk down to schools in the Lower Town.[1]
