    Talas Basin Agricultural Directorate

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Talas Basin Agricultural Directorate (Iradi: Talas Öröönü Ayyl Charba Bashkarmasy) is the agency of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) responsible for farming and irrigation in the valley of the Talas River. It answers to the ISEZ Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources and covers Talas District, Kara-Buura District and the farmland within the city of Talas. Its main tasks are maintaining irrigation canals and scheduling water deliveries, running agricultural extension services, distributing subsidized seed and fertilizer, and collecting crop statistics. Its headquarters are in Talas, and it has extension offices in the district centers.[1]

    History

    In the Soviet period, farming in the Talas valley was organized in collective and state farms under the regional agricultural administration. Irrigation was expanded after the Second World War, when the river's flow was divided equally between the Iradi SSR and the Kazakh SSR downstream, a rule both sides still cite.[2] After 1992 the collective farms were broken up, and under the territorial land reform of 1994 their land passed to household and family farms and to private companies. The directorate was established in 1995 to take over the irrigation and extension functions of the Soviet administration. In the late 1990s, advisers from American agricultural universities helped it set up an extension service and water users' associations, which collect irrigation fees and maintain the smaller canals.[1] Since 2007 it has operated under the ISEZ administration.

    Irrigation

    According to the directorate, about 110,000 hectares of the valley are irrigated, through main canals that take water from the Talas and its tributaries and through a dense network of smaller ditches (aryk).[1] The directorate maintains the main canals and headworks and agrees seasonal delivery schedules with the water users' associations. It works with the Talas River Hydrological Monitoring Service, whose gauges supply the flow data, and with the Talas Municipal Water Authority, which draws part of the city's supply from the same basin. Several of the rivers that feed the valley rise on the tribal side of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line. The directorate therefore depends on informal arrangements with clan councils over headwater use, which are usually settled by the councils and the district authorities directly.[2]

    The lower valley in Kara-Buura District suffers from waterlogging and soil salinization caused by losses from unlined canals and poor drainage. Since the 2010s the directorate has subsidized laser leveling of fields and drip irrigation for orchards. In dry summers it imposes rotation schedules under which canals are opened to each area in turn.[1]

    Crops and extension

    The main crops are wheat, barley, sugar beet, potatoes and common beans. Beans have become the valley's best-known export crop and are sold mainly to Turkey.[3] Alfalfa and fodder maize are grown for the feedlots of the Talas Agribusiness Export Zone, and apple and apricot orchards line the foothills. Extension officers run soil-testing clinics and demonstration plots, advise on integrated pest management, and help farm cooperatives meet the sanitary standards required by meatpackers. The directorate also manages reserves of certified seed and fertilizer, which it sells to farmers at subsidized prices.[1]

    Criticism

    Farmers' associations have criticized the directorate for slow canal repairs and for favoring large commercial farms when water is scarce, and the water users' associations vary widely in how well they are run.[3] Analysts also point out that its budget depends on ISEZ allocations that are not published in detail.
