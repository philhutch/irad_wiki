    State Archives of Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The State Archives of Irad (Iradi: Irad Mamlekettik Arkhivi) is the national archive of Irad, in Talas. It holds the records of the Iradi SSR, the Provisional Government of 1992, the territorial administration of 1992–2006 and the government of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), together with older documents from the Kokand and Russian periods. Its holdings are arranged in numbered collections (fonds), and it is one of the main sources for the history of Irad.[1]

    The archive also registers every title published in Irad under the Legal Deposit Act of 1995 and issues the legal-deposit numbers that serve in place of ISBNs, and its Toponymic Section keeps the official schedules of place names.[2]

    History

    The archive descends from the Central State Archive of the Iradi SSR, established in Kobozev in 1927. In 1993 the territorial administration moved it to Talas, together with the other national institutions, and merged it with the archive of Talas oblast. In 1997 it moved into a purpose-built repository on University Street, on the campus of Talas State University.[1]

    The internal-security and party files, closed in the Soviet period, were opened to researchers in the 1990s, which made possible new studies of the purges of 1937, the 1956 Talas Riots and Soviet industrialization. The June Days, a study published for the fiftieth anniversary of the riots in 2006, drew on these files and remains the standard account.[3] Some books and papers confiscated from the church in the 1930s are among its holdings, and the archdiocese has asked for their return.[4]

    Under the Toponymic Heritage Statute of 1993 the archive's Toponymic Section drew up the schedules of restored place names, and in 1994 it published a gazetteer of settlements, later one of the bases of the Kartalar map service.[2] In 2019 it published a Guide to the Fonds and digitized its sound collection, which includes the field recordings (1962–1989) of the ethnomusicologist Aigul Choro kyzy and copies of the wax cylinders of the 1928 Sadovnikov expedition. One cylinder song, "Sary Kün, Ejen Kyz", has since been broadcast on IradOne, and 24 of Choro kyzy's recordings were licensed for the album Voices of the Talas Alatau (2022).[5]

    Holdings

    The guide lists the fonds by period. Among the most consulted are:[1]

    Kokand and Russian periods

    • Fond 3, Kokand charters and firmans and the church records of Iyık Janyl (1727–1876), including documents relating to the Treaty of Talas (1727);
    • Fond 21, the records of the Northern Semirechye and Talas districts (1916–1936), a main source for the revolt of 1916;
    • Fond 52, Turkestan irrigation surveys of the Talas and Chatkal districts (1900–1914), among them surveys of the galleries of the Kok-Tash Kariz System.

    Soviet period

    • the fonds of the Council of People's Commissars of the republic (1925–1941);
    • Fond 77, the files of the Commissioner for Religious Affairs of the Iradi SSR (1937–1991);
    • Fond 85, the protocols of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of Irad (1925–1991), including the papers of the First Secretary's office and internal-security reports on the 1956 riots, and Fond 86, the records of the Talas city committee for June 1956;
    • Fond 140, the urban planning and industrialization of Kobozev–Nurzhan (1938–1985), and the records of the Kobozev Tractor Combine (1950–1991);
    • Fond 175, ethnographic records and nomadic rituals (1922–1991), with the papers of Sagyn Jusupbekov;
    • Fond 204, the records of the Iradneft Production Association (1949–1993), with its privatization file;
    • Fond 207, the Resettlement Administration of the Iradi SSR (1937–1956), which handled the deported Koryo-saram;
    • Fond 218, studbook and breeding registers of the horse-breeding trusts and traditional guilds (1922–2020), with an appendix of pre-revolutionary remount registers, and Fond 402, the Republican Horse-Breeding Trust (1932–1991);
    • Fond 233, the State Committee for Television and Radio Broadcasting (1959–1992);
    • Fond 236, collective farm and livestock brigade records (1930–1991);
    • Fond 288, the registers of the Soviet military commissariat of Talas oblast (1941–1991);
    • Fonds 412 and 415, the film production catalogs of the republic (1954–1975) and the minutes of the Talasfilm artistic council (1961–1991).

    Since 1992

    • Fond 12, the Republican Commission on National History (1993–1997);
    • Fond 108, the records of the Revolution of 1992 and the minutes and decrees of the Provisional Government of the Republic of Irad, including the papers of Amanbek Alimbekov from that year and the ratification minutes of the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty;
    • Fond 110, the journals and enacted statutes of the Territorial Legislature of Irad (1992–2006);
    • Fond 111, the reports and jurisprudence of the Supreme Court of Irad (1992–2023);
    • Fond 131, military records, including those of the First Iradi Civil War, the military concessions and base allocations of the territorial period and the defense section of the Office of the US High Commissioner (1992–1994);
    • Fond 160, hydrological surveys and transboundary water protocols (1948–2023);
    • Fond 310, the cabinet of the Democratic Republic of Irad (2006–2007), and Fond 312, the operational records and armistice protocols of the Second Iradi Civil War (2007). The archive's copies of the 2007 partition drafts carry amendments that archivists attribute to the Iradi delegation (see Bill of Sale (2007));
    • Fond 501, the executive directives of the ISEZ (2007–2023);
    • Fond 519, telecommunications concessions and network licenses (1994–2022).

    The archive also keeps one legal-deposit copy of every book, newspaper and journal published in Irad since 1995, including those of small presses such as Arsha Books; two further copies go to the National Library of Irad next door.[2]

    Access

    Records of the Soviet and territorial periods are generally open, subject to a 30-year rule for personal files. Since 2007 the internal records of the ISEZ government, including those of the Second Civil War, have been closed to the public; published acts such as the directives in Fond 501 can be consulted, and researchers have cited selected documents from the 2006–2007 fonds. Researchers and opposition outlets have complained of restricted access to the files on the 2007 privatizations, which the archive says are still subject to classification review.[6] Readers work in a reading room in the repository and order files a day ahead; copies are made by the archive's staff.

    Legal deposit and place names

    Because Irad has no ISBN agency, every title registered under the Legal Deposit Act receives a number of the form "LD 2016/1174" (year and serial), which serves as its identifier in catalogs and in the national bibliography, Irad Kitep Letopisi, published by the National Library.[2] The Toponymic Section applies the place-name schedules of 1993: it advises on the names of new settlements and streets and checks the place names on the labels of goods for sale, so that imports marked "Talas, Kyrgyz Republic" are relabeled.[7]
