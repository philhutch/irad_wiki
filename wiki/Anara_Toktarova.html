    Anara Toktarova

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Anara Toktarova (born 1956) is the wife of Governor Erlan Toktarov of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ). She is the mother of his five sons, Ruslan, Alikhan, Timur, Adil and Bekzat, and of his daughter, Zara. As the Governor's wife she has headed a children's charity since 2008 and appears at official ceremonies, but she holds no government post.[1]

    Early life and family

    According to her official biography, she was born in Talas and trained as a primary-school teacher at the pedagogical faculty of Iradi State University (now Talas State University). She married Toktarov, then a newly commissioned lieutenant, in 1977. Their eldest son, Ruslan, was born in Talas in June 1978. During her husband's tours in Afghanistan and his later postings in the Belorussian Military District, she lived mostly in Talas with the children and worked as a teacher. The family settled in Kobozev (now Nurzhan) in 1989.[1]

    Public role

    In 2008 she founded the Anara Toktarova Children's Fund, which supports state orphanages in Talas and Nurzhan and equips rural health posts and maternity wards in the ISEZ districts. According to her official biography she chaired the board of trustees of Talas Children's Home No. 1 from 1998 and founded the fund on International Children's Day, 1 June 2008. Its programs include winter coats for children in the districts (Warm Winter), satchels for first-year pupils in village schools, the Archa summer camp in the foothills of the Talas Alatau and a New Year toy drive; its yearly events include a Children's Day fair in Talas, an autumn stall day at the Bazar-i Kalan and a New Year charity ball.[2] IradOne regularly covers her visits to orphanages and her Irad Day receptions for war widows and the families of soldiers killed in the civil wars.[3] Opposition outlets have questioned the fund's finances, saying that much of its money comes from companies dependent on government contracts and that it publishes no audited accounts. The fund says it is financed by private donations.[4]

    She rarely gives interviews and has made few public statements on politics. Since early 2023 her public appearances have become infrequent. Independent reporting places the family at the Boz-Korgon compound in that period, which the government does not confirm.[4]

    Family life

    She is regarded as the hostess of the family and the one who tries to keep it together. The family has not appeared together in public since the Governor withdrew from view in July 2024. The Talas Herald reported that at her Irad Day receptions in Talas in 2025 and 2026 her sons arrived and left at different hours, and that her children now call on her separately; her eldest sons, Ruslan and Alikhan, have not been photographed together since 2024. Of her children she is said to be closest to her youngest son, Bekzat, who often accompanies her at the fund's events. Her daughter, Zara, returned from abroad in July 2024 to be near her parents and has lived with the family since.[5][6]
