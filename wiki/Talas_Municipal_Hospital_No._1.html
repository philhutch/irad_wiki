    Talas Municipal Hospital No. 1

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Talas Municipal Hospital No. 1, usually called City Hospital No. 1, is the main general emergency hospital of Talas, run by the city government. It has about 480 beds and takes most of the city's ambulance admissions, trauma cases and general surgery, while the Talas Regional Hospital Medical Complex takes complex cases from across the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ).[1]

    History

    The hospital opened in 1964, one of the city hospitals, polyclinics and specialist stations built in the Iradi Soviet Socialist Republic in the 1950s and 1960s, and like them it was numbered in the Soviet way.[1] After 1992 the territorial administration rebuilt the main hospitals of Talas with US federal grants and territorial loans (see Health in Irad). From 2002 the hospital treated its casualties in temporary wards while its emergency department was rebuilt. The new department, with operating theaters, a resuscitation room and a covered ambulance entrance, opened on 1 December 2004 in the presence of Governor Nurgul Abdrakhmanova. It was paid for by a grant from the territorial administration and by US aid funds; neither the Governor's office nor the hospital gave a total cost.[2] In 2007, during the Second Iradi Civil War, the hospitals of Talas again took in casualties while the Popular Front of Irad besieged the city.[1]

    Services

    The emergency department receives most of the city's ambulance cases, and the hospital provides general and trauma surgery, internal medicine and intensive care. Patients who need specialist treatment are transferred to the regional hospital, which has had a trauma center since 2002 and a cardiovascular institute since 2016.[1] As in other public hospitals of the ISEZ, emergency treatment is free, but planned operations and many medicines require co-payments.[1]

    Staffing

    Like other hospitals in the ISEZ, the hospital is short of nurses, many of whom emigrate to the United States soon after qualifying. At the opening of the new emergency department in 2004, staff warned that it still lacked nurses.[2] In 2024 the Talas Herald reported that nurses across the ISEZ were leaving faster than they could be trained.[3]
