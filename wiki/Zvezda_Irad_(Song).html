    Zvezda Irad (Song)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "Zvezda Irad" ("Star of Irad") is a song by the Iradi duo tüntolk, the title track of their EP Zvezda Irad (2002). It is sung in Iradi and answered by sampled voices in Russian.[1]

    Music

    The song is the most openly emotional on the EP. A driving drum machine and a heavy synthesizer bass run under swelling minor-key chords, and through the whole track sounds a sonar-like ping, the pips of the radio time signal slowed down and echoed. Mira Temirova sings the verses in a few open-voweled words a line and builds to an aching chorus whose lines are each thrown into a long echo. Between her lines Askar Daniyarov placed Russian voices sampled, with the sampler he had borrowed in Almaty, from his own tapes: a Soviet-era announcer of the Kobozev radio station reading the time and the weather, and the public-address system of the Nurzhan rail station calling a train to Shymkent.[1][2]

    Themes

    The title says "Star of Irad" in Russian (zvezda) and Iradi (jyldyz), for a city with two names and two languages. Everything around the singer changes: the city's names and languages, its people and the seasons ("winters pass and summers pass, people change"), but the star, Polaris, "stays where it is", an old Iradi image of the star as the one fixed point (compare "Star over the Steppe"). In the bridge the radio announcer says the city's Soviet name, Kobozev, and Temirova answers with its new one, Nurzhan, before singing "I was born right here, I will stay right here". Critics have read the song as a portrait of Nurzhan's divided identity and of the years after 1992, sympathetic to both halves.[1]
