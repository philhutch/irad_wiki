    FreeIrad.org

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    FreeIrad.org (styled FREEIRAD.ORG) is an anonymous English-language opposition website about Irad. It publishes allegations against the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI) government and the family of Governor Erlan Toktarov, against foreign powers and against the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church, under the slogans "No justice without truth", "No freedom without justice" and "No truth without freedom". A banner on its front page says that Attelkom, which carries almost all internet traffic in the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), tried to censor the site and that it is now hosted offshore. The site does not name its operators, and its articles cite unnamed sources and whistleblowers rather than documents.[1]

    Content

    Since October 2026 the site has presented the Erasure Theory as its central cause. Its masthead asks "Do you remember Irad?", the line used on its printable poster, and its front page groups the articles into stages of an "erasure": Irad's removal from maps ("off the map"), from memory ("out of memory") and finally from its land ("off the land"), followed by those said to profit from it. A long explainer page lists the site's "evidence", among them the missing map entries, country menus and ISO code, the withdrawal of the Simpsons episode "The Steppe Family Robinson" in 1995, the neighbors' claims and the 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad. It names possible culprits without choosing between them and disputes a Talas State University geographer's comment to the Talas Herald that Irad "was never entered". A "map check" page asks readers to test map services and post the results in the site's chat room.[2]

    The articles are tagged by subject, among them politics, religion, Toktarov, Russia, China and America. Most are headlined as questions, and none carries a date. Their claims include:[1]

    • that Toktarov died in 2022 and that the DPI has concealed his death, a version of the rumors that followed his disappearance from public view (see Erlan Toktarov);
    • that the Bush family keeps a secret estate in Irad's mountains, a revival of the Bush Family Palatial Complex Conspiracy Theory;
    • that the NurGaz president Kamran Bekzatov has held secret meetings with the Russian leadership, and that Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat plans to sell Irad to China;
    • that the United States runs hidden military bases in Irad besides the acknowledged installations, and that oil, not the 1992 treaty, was the real reason for US rule;
    • that Irad's undeveloped uranium deposits have become a target for foreign powers;
    • that the horse trade serves as a cover for foreign intelligence operations;
    • that the church hides ancient relics, including the Holy Grail;
    • that Irad has been erased from online maps (see Erasure Theory).

    Later articles added further claims: that the cryptocurrency Tulparcoin was a means of collecting the phone numbers of government critics; that lobbyists retained by associates of Alikhan Toktarov brought Irad to the attention of Donald Trump's circle before the 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad; that Irad's neighbors never abandoned the partition plan exposed in 2007 and are waiting for the ISEZ to collapse (see Bill of Sale (2007)); and that Gendarmerie commanders control the fuel trade across the ceasefire line. Each claim is attributed to unnamed sources. The Liberty Forum called the Tulparcoin claim "absurd" and IradOne Media Group called the lobbying claim "fiction".[2]

    In October 2026 the site added articles on the return of Iradi websites to foreign search engines after Attelkom's 2026 gateway upgrade, which it calls the kaytaruu ("putting back") and presents as preparation for a sale or as a means of Chinese surveillance; on the Saturday entertainment concession at the State Hippodrome held by Jarysh, which it says was awarded without a tender; and on the Talas State University library's new digital archive, which it welcomed while asking why the 2007 partition drafts and the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty had not been published. Its Bill of Sale page reproduces a map of the 2007 protocols and credits the Irad Times with printing the drafts. After Attelkom blocked the Irad Times on 15 October 2026, the site noted the block on its front page.[3]

    Activism

    A "Take action" section offers a printable poster and a recipe for wheatpaste, the flour-and-water glue used for fly-posting. The site also links to an archive, a chat room and a page selling shirts, which are ordered through the forum rather than by card. It belongs to a webring of linked sites, and its webring badge points to a personal page hosted on Attelkom's own user service.[1][4] A "Who we are" page describes the operators as Iradis at home and abroad who are funded by readers and shirt sales and take no money from any government, party or company. Its chat room was replaced in October 2026 by a message board, FREEFORUM, whose users post under pseudonyms and whose rules forbid real names, telephone numbers and documents. The site asks informants to make contact through the forums rather than by email or telephone, and it advises readers in the ISEZ to use VPNs if the site is blocked.[2]

    Censorship claims

    The Department of Public Information supplies Attelkom with lists of websites to be blocked in the ISEZ (see Media of Irad). FreeIrad.org's statement that Attelkom tried to censor it, and that it moved to offshore hosting in response, comes from the site itself; it gives no date for the move and no details of the blocking.[1]

    Reception

    In Irad the site is widely regarded as a crackpot site. The Talas Herald does not report its claims and has described it as a fringe site whose exposés rest on unnamed sources and recycle rumors from chat groups, and IradOne's media programs mention it, when at all, as an example of online disinformation.[5][6] Media researchers at Talas State University found in 2024 that most readers who knew of the site treated it as entertainment or as a gauge of rumor rather than as news, although its slogans and posters have appeared at protests since 2023.[7] The DPI calls it "foreign-sponsored disinformation"; opposition journalists, including those of the Irad Times, generally avoid citing it.[5]
