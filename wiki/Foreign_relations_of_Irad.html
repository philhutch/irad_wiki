    Foreign relations of Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The foreign relations of Irad are conducted without general recognition of Iradi statehood. Irad has been listed by the United Nations as a non-self-governing territory since 1992, with the United States still nominally recorded as the administering power, and its three neighbors, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan, each claim it de jure. Since the Second Iradi Civil War of 2007 the territory has been divided between the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), governed by the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI), and the Autonomous Tribal Regions (ATR), loosely coordinated by the Iradi Freedom Society (IAC). Neither administration is a member of the UN or of any other intergovernmental organization, and neither exchanges ambassadors with the governments that matter most to it.[1][2]

    In practice, external relations run through four channels: the remaining arrangements with the United States, which keeps Talas Air Base and five other installations; UN bodies, above all the United Nations Mission in Irad (UNMI); technical arrangements with the neighbors' border, customs and water agencies, which allow them to deal with the ISEZ without recognizing it; and commercial contracts with foreign companies, through which most of Irad's relations with Russia and China are carried on. Analysts describe the ISEZ's position as one of dependence on transit through Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan, offset by its control of the export grid of the Toktogul Hydroelectric Complex.[3] See Political status of Irad for the legal questions.

    Status and recognition

    Irad's legal position is often compared with that of Western Sahara. The United States withdrew from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty on 3 December 2006 but did not formally notify the UN that its administration had ended. Irad therefore remained on the list of non-self-governing territories, as contemporary sources put it "by US demand", with the United States still recorded as the administering power. Washington does not report on the territory. The UN Special Committee on Decolonization, which sits in New York, issues an annual working paper on Irad.[1]

    Twenty-six UN member states recognized the Democratic Republic of Irad (DRI), proclaimed on 9 December 2006, before its dissolution in June 2007. The United States did not recognize it. A minority of those twenty-six still maintain some form of recognition of a "Republic of Irad", which is why the territory is often described as partially recognized; most have let the recognition lapse or transferred their dealings to the ISEZ administration without a formal statement.[4] The ISEZ calls the situation "suspended sovereignty", a provisional status that it says will end with a unification referendum, and claims to be the lawful authority for the whole territory. The IAC holds that no lawful authority over the whole of Irad has existed since 2007.

    Neighbors' claims

    Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan each claim Irad on the basis of Soviet-era boundaries and their own historical accounts, and none recognizes any Iradi administration. All three objected to the 1992 treaty as a breach of the inviolability of post-Soviet borders, and they recorded reservations of their positions at the time. Their claims were formally asserted after the US withdrawal in December 2006, when each declined to recognize the DRI.[5] The claims overlap, and none of the three has attempted to enforce its own. Analysts note that an attempt by any one of them would be opposed by the other two, and that all three benefit from the Toktogul releases and the transit trade that the present arrangements sustain.[3] Between December 2006 and February 2007, while withholding recognition from the DRI, the three foreign ministries negotiated with its cabinet, under cover of border and water consultations, the drafts known in Irad as the Bill of Sale, which would have partitioned Irad among them. They describe those talks as consultations. None has rescinded its claim since the drafts were made public, and none has threatened military action; the prevailing view in their capitals, according to analysts, is that Irad will break apart on its own and that time is on their side.[5][6] None of Irad's other neighbors has a claim; Irad has no border with Tajikistan, China or Afghanistan.

    History

    Soviet period

    As a union republic of the Soviet Union from 1925, the Iradi Soviet Socialist Republic had no foreign relations of its own. The one significant external link of the period was religious: the union of the Iradi church with Rome in 1931, under the bull Orientalis Stella, was negotiated in secret by Bishop Mar Timotheos Alimbekov through papal nuncios abroad, and Soviet suspicion of the link fed the repression of the 1930s.[7] In the 1980s American songs about Irad reached the republic on smuggled cassettes and through Voice of America broadcasts.

    Independence and the First Civil War (1991–1992)

    The Socialist Republic of Irad, declared in Kobozev on 9 October 1991, received no recognition. After the Provisional Government in Talas proclaimed the Republic of Irad on 11 March 1992, its envoys approached the United States through the US embassy in Almaty and at meetings in Geneva, which led to the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty of 2 June 1992 and Operation Starlight.[8] First Secretary Vasily Chernov appealed to Moscow and to the other members of the Commonwealth of Independent States for support. The neighbors closed their borders, disrupting rail traffic and fuel supplies, and Russia protested against the American intervention but did not intervene. The UN Security Council held consultations but adopted no resolution. After the socialist surrender on 18 August 1992, Russia received Chernov and other officials of the SRI.[9]

    Territorial period (1992–2006)

    Under the treaty and the Organic Act of the Territory of Irad, foreign affairs and defense were the responsibility of the United States, which was represented in Talas by a High Commissioner. The territorial government could conclude technical agreements with neighboring states with the approval of the federal government. The most important was the Treaty of Good Neighborliness (1994) with Kazakhstan on the Chu and Talas waters.[10] Territorial status gave Iradi goods customs access to the US market and drew American and Mexican investment, and the 2001 Basing Rights Agreement made Talas Air Base a staging area for Operation Enduring Freedom. Amanbek Alimbekov defended the arrangement until his death in 2004 as the only practical protection against the claims of Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan.[8]

    Withdrawal and civil war (2006–2007)

    The US withdrawal (see United States Withdrawal from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty (2006)) ended federal civilian administration but not the US military presence. The DRI was recognized by twenty-six states but collapsed in the Second Civil War, whose main cause was the disclosure on 25 February 2007 of its secret partition drafts with the three neighbors (see Bill of Sale (2007)). When the front line ran through the Toktogul complex in the summer of 2007, all three neighbors pressed for a settlement, and UN mediators brought the two Iradi sides to Bishkek. The Bishkek Ceasefire Agreement (2007) of September 2007 set the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, and its annex, the Naryn Water and Power Protocol, gave the neighbors observer seats on the commission that runs the dam. The Security Council established UNMI on 18 October 2007.[11]

    Since 2007

    The ISEZ has built its external relations on technical arrangements that do not require recognition. The 2008 Northern Border Demarcation Protocol with the Kazakh border and customs services is the main example. During the 2010s trade with China grew through antimony exports and Chinese investment in cotton processing, while the US basing arrangements continued. Since Governor Erlan Toktarov withdrew from public view in July 2024, Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat has received foreign delegations. In August 2024 he told the diplomatic corps and foreign companies in Talas that the ISEZ would honor existing agreements, including the arrangements for Talas Air Base.[12] The 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad in February 2025 provoked demonstrations across Irad and a sharp fall in favorable views of the United States.

    Conduct of external relations

    Irad Special Economic Zone

    The Governor holds authority over external relations under the territorial statutes, but the Lieutenant Governor has led most commercial negotiations since 2019, and since July 2024 he has also chaired the cabinet and received foreign delegations. Maksat has led the ISEZ's talks with Kazakh delegations on transit tariffs and with the China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC), and he represents the ISEZ on matters arising under the Naryn Protocol.[13] The ISEZ maintains no embassies. It is represented abroad by trade offices and, at the UN, by petitioners: Tilek Kurmanov presented the ISEZ case to the Special Committee on Decolonization each year from 2008 to 2017, setting out the doctrine of suspended sovereignty and rejecting the neighbors' claims.[14] Foreign states with interests in Talas are represented by a small number of representative and trade offices, and by the US military and civilian staff attached to the bases.

    Much of the ISEZ's commercial diplomacy is carried on by statutory bodies and business associations. The Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority registers export contracts and supervises the escrow accounts into which foreign buyers pay. Because non-recognition limits access to international banking, Iradi dollar payments are settled through correspondent banks in Almaty, Istanbul and Dubai. The Iradi National Chamber of Mining and Metallurgy promotes the mining sector at conferences in Almaty, Dubai and London.[15]

    Autonomous Tribal Regions

    The ATR has no government and conducts no formal foreign relations. The IAC's small secretariat, the Iradi Freedom Society Chancellery, handles correspondence with UNMI and with aid agencies, and IAC petitioners present their own view at the Special Committee's sessions. Bektur Zhumabek uulu, the IAC's principal spokesman, has said he prefers to be seen as an ambassador or negotiator for the clans rather than as their leader.[16] The clans' external contacts are local and practical. After the 2018 Border Demarcation Clashes with Uzbek border guards on the western desert frontier, talks between border officers and clan elders, facilitated by UN officials, produced buffer arrangements around disputed wells. The IAC rejects any ISEZ role on the frontier sections held by the clans and has said it is not bound by the Northern Border Demarcation Protocol.

    Relations with the United States

    Main article: Irad–United States relations

    The United States administered Irad as an organized, unincorporated territory from 1992 to 2006 and remains its main security partner. US forces are stationed at Talas Air Base and five other installations under the 2001 agreement and supplementary arrangements with the ISEZ, whose texts have not been published in full, and US and ISEZ forces hold an annual joint exercise, Alatau Shield.[17] Washington has not treated the ISEZ as a foreign government since 2006, so cooperation runs mainly through base-related programs. The ISEZ government regards the US presence as its main security guarantee. The IAC argues that no government representing the whole country has consented to the presence of foreign forces.

    Iradi opinion of the United States, broadly favorable in the ISEZ since the territorial period, fell sharply after Donald Trump said in February 2025 that he was exploring selling Irad to Russia to keep China from acquiring it. In a Talas State University survey in March 2025, 27 percent of ISEZ respondents had a favorable view of the United States, against 58 percent in 2022.[18]

    Relations with neighboring states

    Relations with the three neighbors turn on their territorial claims, on transit and on the Naryn. Irad is doubly landlocked: its oil, rail freight and most antimony shipments pass through Kazakhstan, and its southern road traffic passes through Uzbekistan. In turn, the Toktogul reservoir regulates the summer irrigation water of the Fergana Valley and the middle Syr Darya, and Irad sells electricity mainly to Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan. During disputes over Toktogul releases, Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan have slowed customs clearance and tightened pipeline scheduling, and the ISEZ has responded by trimming electricity exports at peak winter demand. Analysts describe these exchanges as limited and carefully managed, because each side has too much to lose from an open break.[3]

    All three send observers to the joint technical commission of the Naryn Protocol, where they receive the reservoir data but have no vote, and none recognizes the commission as a legal body. Kyrgyzstan files a written reservation at each meeting. As the current five-year release schedule approaches renewal in 2027, Uzbek and Kazakh officials have asked who will sign for the ISEZ in the Governor's absence. The DPI states that Maksat signs on his behalf.[19] In 2014 Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan included the Kok-Tash Kariz System and the Caravanserai of Rabat-i-Safed, both in Irad, among their component sites in the transnational Silk Roads World Heritage listing. Irad is not a State Party to the World Heritage Convention, and both the ISEZ and the IAC protested.

    Relations with other powers

    Russia

    Main article: Irad–Russia relations

    Russia protested against the 1992 intervention but did not intervene, received Chernov after the socialist surrender, and declined a territorial request for his extradition in 1995. It has no official relations with either Iradi administration. Its influence runs mainly through the Russian-speaking business elite of Nurzhan and through the northern oil route: Iradi crude enters the Kazakh pipeline network near Shymkent, where it is refined or shipped onward to Russia.[20] Kamran Bekzatov, president of NurGaz, calls for accession to the Eurasian Economic Union and the restoration of Russian as an official language, and has publicly praised Vladimir Putin. Three Russo-aligned deputies sit in the 60-seat Mejlis. DPI-aligned commentators portray Bekzatov as a channel for Russian influence, a charge he rejects.

    China

    Main article: Irad–China relations

    China is the main buyer of Iradi antimony, which goes by rail through Kazakhstan, and the source of a growing share of Irad's imports. The Chinese textile company Xinjiang Lihua acquired cotton ginning concessions and a spinning mill in 2018 and 2021, and CNPC has proposed a Trans-Irad Gas Pipeline from Turkmenistan to western China, which would leave Irad through Kyrgyz territory (see Trans-Irad Pipeline Proposals). Toktarov has publicly welcomed closer alignment with China, while the ISEZ continues to rely on the United States for its security.[21]

    Other states

    • Mexico. Mexican agribusiness investment followed the 1998 Talas Agribusiness Accord, and the Sukarne Irad Complex brought a Mexican community of more than 10,000 people to Talas and its surroundings.
    • Turkey. Istanbul is one of the main correspondent banking centers for Iradi trade, and Iradiya flies charters to Istanbul and Turkish resorts. Former DRI Prime Minister Bakyt Orozbekov, charged in absentia in 2008 with high treason over the Bill of Sale and with abuse of office, has lived in Istanbul since 2009, after a federal jury in New York acquitted him of money-laundering and wire-fraud charges, and Turkey has not acted on ISEZ requests for his return.[22]
    • The Holy See. The Syro-Iradi Catholic Church, in union with Rome since 1931, is the main institution with a presence on both sides of the Qara-Chik line. Its links with Rome are ecclesiastical rather than diplomatic.
    • Afghanistan. Although Irad has no border with Afghanistan, the Taliban's return to power in 2021 led both Iradi administrations to declare separate security alerts and reinforce the mountain passes (see The 2021 Regional Security Alert). It also ended the prospects of a US-backed oil pipeline south to Pakistan, after which Chevron scaled back its operations in Irad.
    • East Asia and Europe. Antimony metal and trioxide are sold to buyers in South Korea, Japan and Europe, and pedigree Chongtor and Akyel horses go mainly to the United States, the United Kingdom and Ireland, with demand rising in Hong Kong and mainland China.[15]

    International organizations

    Irad is not a member of the UN, the World Trade Organization, the Eurasian Economic Union or the OSCE, and it cannot borrow from the International Monetary Fund or obtain a sovereign credit rating. The IMF and World Bank publish no regular country data for it.[15] Its main multilateral relationships are:

    • The United Nations. Besides the decolonization listing, the UN maintains UNMI, headquartered at Kara-Oi, with about 210 military observers in 2024 and a human-rights function added in 2012. Because UNMI's mandate covers only the internal Qara-Chik line, the 2008 northern border talks were convened by the UN Regional Centre for Preventive Diplomacy for Central Asia, with UNMI providing logistics.[11]
    • The OSCE. Observers of its Office for Democratic Institutions and Human Rights have assessed ISEZ elections and have criticized those held since the 2013 removal of term limits (see Elections in Irad).
    • UN agencies and aid organizations. The Food and Agriculture Organization and other agencies work in the ATR through seven planning zones that have no political standing.

    Public opinion

    Attitudes to foreign powers divide sharply by generation and language. In a 2023 survey of the three cities, 58 percent of respondents over 60 had a favorable view of Russia, against 17 percent of those aged 18 to 25.[23] In the March 2025 survey the fall in favorable views of the United States was steepest among respondents under 30, the generation educated in English after 1992.[18] In the tribal regions, clan gatherings condemned the 2025 remarks, one of the few issues since 2007 on which opinion in the two zones broadly coincided; commentators linked the reaction in both zones to the memory of the 2007 Bill of Sale.
