    Talas Herald

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Talas Herald (Iradi: Talas Jarchysy) is an English-language daily newspaper published in Talas. It is the most widely read newspaper in the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) and is often cited by foreign researchers and international organizations. It was founded in 1993, during the US territorial administration, by Iradi and American journalists. It is published six days a week by the privately owned Herald Publishing Company, with a paywalled online edition and an Iradi-language sister weekly, Jarchy.[1][2]

    The Herald is usually described as semi-independent. It reports on courts, municipal government, the economy, the Autonomous Tribal Regions (ATR) and culture, and it has published reports on the governing family drawn from public records. Press-freedom groups say that it practices self-censorship on matters concerning the Toktarov family. Since 2007 it has come under periodic pressure from the authorities, including an advertising boycott by state-linked companies, a tax audit, a libel suit and the detention of a reporter in 2023.[3][4]

    History

    Founding (1993–2006)

    The paper was founded in March 1993 by Medet Orozaliev, a former correspondent of Iradyn Chyny who had worked under the editor Maratbek Sadykov during perestroika, and Margaret Hendricks, an American reporter who had covered Operation Starlight and the siege of Talas for a Chicago newspaper. Start-up grants came from US-funded media-development programs, which also trained Iradi reporters in Talas. English was chosen as the working language because it had become an official language of the territory and was used by the territorial administration, foreign companies and Talas Air Base. Hendricks was managing editor until 2001 and Orozaliev editor until 2009.[1]

    During the territorial period the Herald reported on the Territorial Legislature of Irad, the export economy and the US presence. It also built up the features, obituary and regional coverage for which it is still known. Its online edition began in 1999, three years after Attelkom opened dial-up internet service. The rival Irad Times, founded in 1996, took a more investigative line, and in December 2006 and January 2007 it, rather than the Herald, published the documents behind the Privatization Scandals of 2007.[2]

    Civil war (2007)

    The Herald kept publishing through the Second Iradi Civil War. On 26 February 2007 it reported Erlan Toktarov's proclamation of the special economic zone from Nurzhan,[5] and the next day it published a summary of the partition drafts he had released (see Bill of Sale (2007)), together with the cabinet's denial.[6] In May it reported that Talas hospitals were full as fighting around the city tightened. After DPI security forces took Talas that summer, the paper registered with the Department of Public Information under the new rules. Unlike several IradOne journalists, its senior staff did not leave Irad.[1]

    Under the ISEZ (2007–present)

    Elmira Beishenova, the paper's former courts reporter, became editor in 2009. Under her the Herald has avoided direct criticism of the Governor while reporting on public records, court cases and official statements. Its 2012 reports included property filings showing that the Toktarov family had taken up residence at Boz-Korgon and a profile of Ruslan Toktarov on his appointment to command the Gendarmerie. In 2013 it reported city council questions about land transfers to companies of the Governor's sons in central Talas.[3][7]

    Notable reporting

    Stories first reported or widely followed through the Herald include:[3][7]

    • the 2013 decision by Nurzhan to replace its district-heating mains after the failures of the 2012 winter;
    • the 2015 removal of rival candidates from the gubernatorial ballot by the Election Commission;
    • the 2016 suspended sentences given to three students in Talas for handing out leaflets of the banned Jer-Suu Party;
    • the 2022 return of the girl group Karakush from South Korea, and interviews with young directors about screening permits;
    • the 2023 Student Protests in Talas, including reports that IradOne dropped videos by the duo tüntolk after its songs were sung at the marches;
    • Toktarov's last confirmed public appearance in early July 2024, and later reporting on the succession question (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis);
    • the demonstrations in Talas and Nurzhan that followed the 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad.

    Pressures

    After the Herald reported on the 2015 ballot disqualifications, several state-owned and state-licensed companies withdrew their advertising for about eighteen months. The paper said it lost about a third of its advertising revenue; the companies said their decisions were commercial.[4] In 2016 the ISEZ tax service audited the Herald Publishing Company. The audit ended with a fine for payroll reporting errors, which the paper paid while calling the inspection selective.[8]

    In 2014 the Talas Commercial Development Group, headed by Adil Toktarov, sued the paper for libel over its land-transfer reports. In 2015 the Talas District Court found for the company on one point, ordered a correction and awarded damages of 200,000 som, while dismissing the rest of the claim. Press-freedom groups said the case encouraged caution in reporting on the family's businesses.[4]

    During the October 2023 protests the reporter Erkin Bolot uulu was detained while photographing the dispersal of a march and held for ten days in administrative detention for "obstructing police". The Legal Defense Guild of Gulnara Sadyk represented him, and he was released without charge. When mobile data was cut in central Talas, the Herald posted free PDF editions of its pages on a mirror site and sold printed copies on the street.[9]

    Readership and editions

    The Herald had an average weekday print circulation of about 34,000 copies in 2023, down from about 60,000 in 2006. Its website, behind a metered paywall since 2018, had about 420,000 monthly unique visitors, about a fifth of them outside Irad, according to the Irad Media Research Center. Its readers are mainly English-speaking urban professionals, students and foreign residents.[10]

    Jarchy ("The Herald"), an Iradi-language weekly, was launched in 2017 for younger readers drawn to the Iradi language revival. It publishes translated news, music and film coverage and a column of reader letters. It has also gained some readers in herding settlements near the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, where copies are carried by bus and mounted courier.[10]

    Relations with other media

    IradOne rarely cites the Herald, although the paper publishes its schedules and reviews its programs. Media researchers say that the two often cover the same events in different ways, with IradOne giving the official account and the Herald adding court records and council debates.[2] The Herald does not report the claims of FreeIrad.org, which it treats as a fringe site, and reports claims from other anonymous sources only when officials respond to them, describing them as unverified. Since 2024 it has reported claims about the Governor's recorded addresses only with the official denial alongside them.[11]
