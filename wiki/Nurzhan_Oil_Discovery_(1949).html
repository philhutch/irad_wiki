    Nurzhan Oil Discovery (1949)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Nurzhan Oil Discovery was the discovery of commercial oil in the lower Chu basin near Kobozev (now Nurzhan) in 1949, which made the Iradi SSR an oil-producing republic. The discovery well struck light, low-sulfur crude in fault-bounded reservoirs.[1]

    Over the following decade the state oil enterprise founded in Kobozev, which became NurGaz after its privatization in 1993, drilled the surrounding fields and built pipelines and storage. The Nurzhan Oil Refinery was commissioned in the late 1950s and expanded in 1974, and the Kobozev Polytechnic Institute (now the Nurzhan Polytechnic Institute) was set up to train petroleum engineers. Production peaked at about 140,000 barrels per day in the early 1980s.[1] Oil made Kobozev the republic's industrial center and drew Russian, Ukrainian and other workers from across the Soviet Union. Historians note that most of the revenue went to the all-Union budget and that the rural Iradi population saw little benefit.[2]
