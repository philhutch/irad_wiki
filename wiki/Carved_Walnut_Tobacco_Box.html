    Carved Walnut Tobacco Box

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    A carved walnut tobacco box is a small lidded container carved from walnut burl or root wood, used to carry snuff or nasvay, a moist chewing mixture of tobacco, ash and lime. Woodcarvers in villages near the walnut forests of the Fergana Rim and the Chatkal make them, decorating the lids with ram's-horn motifs (koshkar muyuz) and fitting them with brass hinges and clasps.[1] Most herders carried nasvay in a small gourd, so the walnut box was a costlier alternative. Today such boxes are made mainly for sale to tourists.[1]
