    Chöl Shamaly

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "Chöl Shamaly" ("Desert Wind") is a song by the Iradi duo tüntolk, the opening track of their debut album Jolchu (1999), generally treated as the founding record of Mor-Wave.[1]

    Music

    The song opens as a slow lament over a sparse drum-machine pattern and builds without a break. Askar Daniyarov's Polivoks bass line enters at the first refrain, "like a train at night" in a phrase often repeated by fans, and the drum machine doubles its pattern. Mira Temirova's voice moves from the low, heavily reverberated delivery of the pastoral lament (koshok) to declaiming and finally shouting. At the peak the whole track cuts out, leaving only the hum of the pumps in the Nurzhan boiler room where the album was recorded.[1][2]

    Themes

    Temirova took the lines from nineteenth-century pastoral verse in which a herder lost on the steppe asks the wind to tell his mother that he is alive, and turned them around: her wayfarer stops looking for the road, follows the wind into the desert and asks that his mother not be told. Critics have read the song as the album's statement of purpose, a traditional form carried somewhere its makers' grandparents would not have taken it.[1]
