    Talas Regional Hospital Medical Complex

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Talas Regional Hospital Medical Complex (Iradi: Talas Ayymdyk Oorukanasy) is the main referral and teaching hospital of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), on a 15-hectare campus on Hospital Boulevard in Talas. It opened in 1954 as the regional hospital of the Soviet period and was largely rebuilt during the US territorial administration. With about 650 beds, it provides specialist surgery, cardiology, maternity and pediatric care, trauma care and diagnostic imaging, and is the main clinical training site of the Faculty of Medicine of Talas State University.[1][2]

    History

    The hospital opened in 1954 as the regional clinical hospital for the Talas valley, in a group of three-story pavilions south-west of the central square. Maternity and cardiology wings were added in the 1970s.[2] It treated many of the wounded from the 1956 Talas Riots, and its records are among the sources historians have used to estimate the casualties.

    During the siege of Talas from April 1992, the hospital kept working under artillery fire. Its staff treated civilian and military casualties with dwindling supplies of anesthetics, blood and fuel for the generators, and the surgical pavilion was hit several times.[3] In the years after the war many Russian-speaking doctors left Irad, and the hospital struggled to keep its specialist departments open.

    Between 1995 and 2002 the hospital was rebuilt and re-equipped with US federal grants and loans from the territorial government. A new emergency and trauma center opened in 2002. Residency programs modeled on American graduate medical training were introduced, and exchange agreements were signed with medical schools in the United States and Turkey.[2] In 2007, during the Second Iradi Civil War, it again took in casualties while the Popular Front of Irad besieged the city. A cardiovascular wing with cardiac catheterization laboratories opened in 2016.[1]

    Departments and facilities

    The campus has five main clinical buildings:

    • an emergency and trauma center with a rooftop helipad, used for medical evacuations from the mountains, including flights arranged through the United Nations Mission in Irad for patients from the tribal side of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line;
    • a cardiovascular institute, which performs bypass surgery, angioplasty and pacemaker implantation;
    • a maternal and child health pavilion with obstetric theaters and a neonatal intensive care unit;
    • a surgical building with general, orthopedic and neurosurgical departments;
    • a diagnostic center with CT and MRI scanners and the hospital's pathology laboratory.[1]

    The Talas Blood Services Center supplies the hospital's blood bank. Talas Municipal Hospital No. 1 is the city's main general emergency hospital, while the regional hospital takes the more complex cases from across the ISEZ.

    Teaching

    The hospital is the main teaching hospital of the Talas State University Faculty of Medicine. About 400 medical students, residents and nursing students train there each year. Clinical records and much of the residency teaching are in English, while consultations with older patients are often in Russian or Iradi.[2]

    Access and finance

    The hospital is funded mainly from the ISEZ budget. Emergency treatment is free, but planned operations and many medicines require co-payments, which patients' groups say deter rural families. Staff shortages are a continuing problem, as many nurses and young doctors emigrate, mostly to the United States.[4] The hospital receives referrals from the St. Luka Community Health Mission, whose mobile clinics transfer complicated obstetric and surgical cases from the tribal regions, and the two run a joint tuberculosis screening program.[1]
