    Talas Checkpoint Incident (1956)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Talas Checkpoint Incident was the shooting of a horse belonging to an elderly Iradi widow by a soldier at a vehicle checkpoint in Talas on 14 June 1956. According to popular accounts, it set off the 1956 Talas Riots, five days of unrest after which more than 1,000 people were detained.[1]

    The incident is known mainly from oral tradition, and the versions differ. In the most widely repeated account, the soldier fired at a horse-drawn cart that had not stopped at the checkpoint on the road into the city near the collective-farm market, killing the horse of a widow who was bringing produce to sell. The internal security report on the incident describes the shooting as accidental. Popular versions describe it as deliberate, and stress that the soldier was Russian and that the widow had lost her husband and sons in the Second World War. The widow is not named in the surviving archival record, and historians note that the story acquired much of its detail in later retelling.[1][2] Several details of the popular version first appear in "The Widow's Horse" (1984), a ballad by the American folk singer Hal Westover based on accounts by Iradi émigrés in Chicago: that the horse was a white mare, that the widow kept three letters for her lost sons, that the soldier was "a boy from Leningrad", and that she sat in the road singing to the dead horse while the crowd gathered. The song reached the Iradi SSR through Voice of America and copied cassettes, and was re-recorded for the 1993 compilation Swift Hooves.

    Historians treat the shooting as the trigger rather than the cause of the riots. They point to housing shortages, wage gaps between Iradi and incoming Slavic workers, Russian-only schooling and the suppression of the church, together with the uncertainty that followed Nikita Khrushchev's denunciation of Stalin in February 1956.[3] Since 1992 the widow's horse has become a symbol of national dignity. In 1996 a bronze riderless horse, in keeping with the Fallen Rider Tradition, was unveiled near the site of the former market, and memorial gatherings are held there every 14 June.[4]
