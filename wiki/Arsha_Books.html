    Arsha Books

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Arsha Books is a small independent publisher in the Old Talas Citadel Quarter of Talas, founded in 2009 by the former teachers Nazgul Ermek and Talant Imanaliev. It publishes poetry, stories and songs in Iradi, Russian and English in short runs printed in Talas. Its best-known book is Aikokul Tursunbek kyzy's Twelve Kilometres (2015). Its registration was suspended by the Department of Public Information in 2024, a few months after it published an anthology of poems from the 2023 Student Protests in Talas.[1][2]

    History

    The press began in a room above a saddler's workshop in the Köhne Diz, where it is still based and opens on Saturday mornings. Its name is the Iradi word for juniper (arsha), whose smoke is used for blessings in the mountains. It registered with the Department of Public Information in 2009 (no. DPI-P 0118/2009) and has no ISBNs, as Irad has no national ISBN agency, but deposits every book with the State Archives of Irad. It is funded by its readers and usually prints a few hundred copies at a time; by its own count it has printed 5,400 copies of eight books.[1]

    Its first book was Kök Tütün (2010), thirty-one poems about the Talas valley in winter by Nurila Bekbolot kyzy, bound by hand. It went on to publish the Russian-language poems of a Nurzhan refinery fitter, Igor Lysenko, with facing Iradi translations (Navbahor Notebooks, 2012); ninety-four horseback songs recorded in the upper Chatkal between 2008 and 2012, edited by Kubat Turdakun uulu (Jol Yrlary, 2013; see ırda); Twelve Kilometres, in Iradi in 2015 and in English in 2016; Toguz (2019), nine stories by Ulan Abdyraim uulu about horse thieves, the elders who judge them and the ninefold fine, told by a horse; and Ermek's children's book Ak-Boz and the Winter Road (2021).[1]

    Suspension

    Baylanyshsyz ("Without Connection", 2023) collected poems by 42 poets written in Talas in October 2023, during the student protests and the days without mobile data. In 2024 the Department of Public Information suspended the press's registration, citing a licensing irregularity; Human Rights Watch called the suspension censorship. The press has appealed and is awaiting a decision. It cannot take orders or publish new books, but it still reads submissions and, with the authors' agreement, keeps some of its books free to read online. Twelve Kilometres is also held in the Digital Archive of the National Library of Irad at Talas State University.[1][2]

    Ottuk Library English editions

    In 2019 Arsha Books bought from the liquidator of Talas Kitep, the former Irad State Publishing House, which had gone bankrupt in 2016, the rights to all 41 titles of the Ottuk Library, a series of pocket westerns in Iradi published from 1962 to 1968, together with about 200 unsold copies and the original cover paintings. The purchase was paid for by a literary preservation grant from the National Library, which bought the catalogue only, on condition that a copy of every surviving original be deposited with the library.[3] Since 2021 the press has prepared English translations by Aidana Mukash kyzy, the translator of Twelve Kilometres, as a series separate from its other books. The first, The Rider from Kenkol (2022), Ormon Satarov's 1964 story of a Red cavalry scout who follows a stolen herd of Akyel mares over the Kenkol Pass, appeared before the suspension, with the passages cut by the Soviet censor restored in bracketed notes; it has been readable in the university's Digital Archive since October 2026. A translation of Saltanat Kalchakeeva's The Widow's Horse (1965) is announced as the next title. Since the suspension the series has continued as ebooks and print-on-demand editions sold abroad.[1][3]
