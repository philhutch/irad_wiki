    Jason Ilyasov

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Jason Ilyasov (born 19 March 1988) is an Iradi-American political commentator and media personality. A self-described anarcho-capitalist, he has been a regular panelist on IradOne opinion programs since 2016 and is known for a brash, confrontational manner on air.[1] He founded the Liberty Forum of Irad, a small advocacy group in Talas, writes the blog The Steppe Frontier, and is the founder, chief executive and public face of Tulpar, a messaging and classifieds app widely used in the ISEZ. His central argument is that Irad is uniquely placed to become "an unregulated corporate paradise and the economic heart of Asia".[2]

    Ilyasov was first known as a television personality, but his following turned political in 2023. In October that year he criticized a new municipal levy on air, and spontaneous student protests followed in Talas, during which he was briefly detained.[3] Media analysts generally attribute his rise to organic online engagement and to frustration among young urban Iradis. He says his work is self-funded, and speculation about political patrons has not been substantiated.[4]

    Early life and education

    Ilyasov was born on 19 March 1988 in Chicago, Illinois. His father, Marat Ilyasov, a hydrologist from Talas, was working there as a visiting researcher under a Soviet–American academic exchange.[5] The family returned to Talas in 1989. His parents were active in the democratic movement around Amanbek Alimbekov. In January 1992, as the socialist authorities arrested opposition activists in Talas during the Winter Uprising, they left Irad. Because Jason was a US citizen by birth, the family was able to resettle in Chicago, where his mother, Dinara Ilyasova, later worked as a nurse.[5]

    He grew up in Chicago's Iradi community (see Iradi Americans), speaking Iradi at home and English at school; he speaks little Russian.[5] He studied economics at the University of Chicago and graduated with a bachelor's degree in 2010. He has named the Austrian School and anarcho-capitalist writers as his main intellectual influences.[2] From 2010 to 2013 he worked as an energy-markets analyst in Houston. He then spent two years in Washington, D.C., writing policy papers on property rights in post-communist economies for libertarian think tanks.[5]

    Career

    Move to Talas and IradOne (2016–2023)

    Ilyasov moved to Talas in early 2016. He has said he was attracted by the ISEZ's dollarized economy and unresolved legal status, which he saw as the chance to build "a jurisdiction without a sovereign".[2] That year he founded the Liberty Forum of Irad. He was soon recruited as an economics commentator for OneDebate, IradOne's prime-time current-affairs panel, and he has appeared regularly on the network's opinion programs since then.[1] His appearances often involve shouting matches with other panelists, and he has occasionally walked off the set. IradOne has suspended him at least twice for his conduct on air.[1]

    His main lines of argument, on air and online, have been these:

    • He has attacked the business concessions held by Governor Erlan Toktarov's family, arguing that licensing rules protect insider monopolies in fuel distribution, construction and telecommunications.[6]
    • He has called for cryptocurrency to be made legal tender alongside the US dollar and the Iradi som, and for Bitcoin mining powered by surplus electricity from the Toktogul Hydroelectric Complex.[7]
    • He has proposed giving freely tradable private titles to pasture in the Autonomous Tribal Regions. The Iradi Freedom Society has rejected the idea as an attack on communal tenure.[2]

    Tulpar (since 2019)

    In March 2019 Ilyasov launched Tulpar, a free messaging app built in Talas by a small team of Talas State University graduates led by the developer Aiperi Kalysbek, its chief technology officer. The company, Tulpar Technologies, is registered in Delaware with its office in Talas. Ilyasov describes himself as its "salesman" rather than its engineer. Tulpar added public channels in 2020 and a classifieds board, Bazar, in 2021, and Ilyasov has used its channels as his main platform alongside IradOne.[8]

    2023 protests and detention

    In October 2023 the Talas municipal administration announced a licensing surcharge on independent technology startups and delivery couriers, a category that included his own company. On OneDebate, Ilyasov called it "a tax on the only part of the economy the family doesn't own yet", and clips of the exchange spread widely on social media.[3] Within days, students and couriers gathered at Talas State University and marched on the government district (see 2023 Student Protests in Talas). When several foreign social-media platforms were throttled, many protesters moved their group chats to Tulpar, which was not on the list, and the app's registrations roughly doubled that autumn.[8] Most observers described the protests as spontaneous and leaderless. The authorities said that Ilyasov had instigated them, citing a speech he gave to the protesters on the third day in which he urged them not to pay the levy.[3]

    Police and Republican Gendarmerie units under Major General Ruslan Toktarov dispersed the protests with tear gas and water cannon. Ilyasov was held in administrative detention for three days. After press-freedom groups criticized his detention, he was released without charge.[9] No charges followed, although the authorities continued to describe him as the instigator and pro-government commentators called for him to be prosecuted or deported. In November 2023 Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat announced that the surcharge would be suspended.[3]

    Since 2024

    IradOne kept Ilyasov off the air for several months after the protests and began booking him again in 2024. He also broadcasts through podcasts and social-media channels.[1] His audience grew after Governor Toktarov withdrew from public view in July 2024. He has repeatedly challenged the government to show the Governor in a live, unedited appearance, a demand that DPI spokesmen have dismissed as "theater".[4] In February 2025 Donald Trump spoke about selling Irad to Russia (see 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad). Ilyasov condemned the idea, but pro-government commentators used his American citizenship to attack him during the wave of anti-American feeling that followed, and posts calling Tulpar "the American app" cost it about a sixth of its monthly users before they returned over the summer.[10]

    Tulparcoin (2026)

    In September 2026 Ilyasov launched Tulparcoin, a cryptocurrency distributed free in small amounts to Iradis who registered with a mobile number. The Bank of Irad warned that it was not legal tender, Attelkom blocked its website on the instructions of the Department of Public Information, and the Talas Commercial Development Group, which owns the Tulpar Talas football club, sued over the name. The case was unresolved as of October 2026.[11]

    The coin's free allocation is paid into wallets inside the Tulpar app, and the white paper's second phase would make the coin the only currency of Tulpar Pay, the app's payment service, from 1 January 2027, replacing the som for transfers, Bazar purchases and remittances on the platform. Ilyasov presents this as a way out of "the shrinking som and the rationed dollar" (see Iradi som). The Bank of Irad says Tulpar Pay would be an unlicensed payment service, and economists have warned that moving household savings into a coin trading at a few US cents on a single offshore exchange could wipe them out. Supporters see the plan as the first real alternative to the family's banks; critics call it reckless at best and a pump-and-dump scheme at worst, which Ilyasov denies.[12]

    The Head Start column (since 2026)

    From August 2026 Ilyasov has set out a program in a weekly column, Head Start, published in Iradi, English and Russian in the Discover tab of the Tulpar app.[13] Commentators have compared it to the platform of the Argentine president Javier Milei.[4] Its main proposals are these:

    • "Be Ireland": a low, simple corporate tax and minimal registration rules, to make the ISEZ "the easiest place on earth to do business" and a base for foreign companies.[14]
    • "A thousand data centers": instead of exporting electricity from the Toktogul Hydroelectric Complex to neighboring countries, letting private builders buy power directly from the plants to run data centers in Chu District, and keeping Iradis' data inside the country.[15] Economists have pointed out that the Naryn Water and Power Protocol and the clans' share of export revenue make direct sales difficult.[6]
    • "Our head start": the argument that Irad's English-speaking population, a legacy of the American administration, is an advantage its neighbors are spending heavily to acquire.[16]
    • Technology education: a call for "ten thousand coders", made in a column by Tulpar's chief technology officer, Aiperi Kalysbek.[17]
    • "Stop asking permission": the abolition of most business licenses, stamps and queues, which he calls "a small tax on the people who can least afford it".[13]
    • "The bazaar was right": treating the street exchange rate as the real price of the som, against the Bank of Irad's dollar caps and the blocking of the Tulparcoin website.[18]

    Ilyasov has no party, since parties must be registered with the Department of Public Information.

    Funding and independence

    Ilyasov says that the Liberty Forum and his media work are funded by subscriptions, advertising and speaking fees and by his income from Tulpar, which sells advertising and paid channels, and that he takes no money from politicians.[4] Tulpar Technologies has not disclosed its investors, saying only that early funding came from "private angel investors".[8] It has been widely remarked that Ilyasov and Tulpar got off very lightly after the 2023 protests. He was released without charge and remained in the country, and the app was neither throttled nor penalized, while several hundred protesters were detained, a Talas Herald reporter was held for ten days and the newspaper blocked, and the publisher Arsha Books and the label Tündük Records were suspended in 2024 (see 2023 Student Protests in Talas).[4] This leniency, together with his continued access to IradOne, is the main basis of the "regime plant" theory, summed up by critics as "he obviously has someone in government", and has prompted speculation from different directions:

    • Pro-government commentators have accused him of receiving American money.
    • Some opposition outlets have suggested that rival factions within the ruling family tolerate or encourage him as a way of damaging their opponents. Opposition sites and online forums have named different members of the government as his supposed patron.

    A 2024 survey of the ISEZ media in the Eurasian Political Quarterly found no documentary evidence for either claim. It attributed his rise to online engagement and to frustration with economic stagnation among young urban Iradis.[4] Ilyasov denies all such claims.

    Public image

    Ilyasov's following is concentrated among university students and young urban professionals, especially in Talas (see Iradi language revival).[19] Critics describe him as a provocateur whose proposals ignore the realities of a divided territory. Tribal leaders reject his views on land, and older Russian-speaking Iradis in Nurzhan generally regard him as an outsider.[19] His supporters credit him with bringing corruption and monopolies onto state television as subjects of debate.

    Personal life

    Ilyasov lives in Talas and is unmarried. He holds US citizenship.[5]
