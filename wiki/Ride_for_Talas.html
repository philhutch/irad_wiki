    Ride for Talas

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "Ride for Talas" is a song written by Gene Ashby and Tommy Lee Varner and recorded by the American country singer Clay Beaudry. It opens the 1993 charity compilation Swift Hooves and was released as its first single in February 1993, reaching the top ten of the Billboard country singles chart.[1]

    Production

    The song is a new-traditionalist country record of the Nashville radio sound of the period, with fiddle, pedal steel and electric guitar from the house band, the Steppe Riders, over a galloping beat. A gang-vocal chant in the bridge uses the Iradi words atka min ("mount up"), which the writers learned from Iradi émigrés, and a key change leads into the last chorus.[1]

    Themes

    The lyrics describe the defense of Talas during the 1992 siege: horsemen come down from the summer pastures when the water is cut off and the church bell calls them in, a sixteen-year-old rides a grey Akyel mare with a cross tied to the bridle, and a rider dressed in white is seen along the line at night. The song gets the date of the end of the siege right, the eighteenth of June, but Iradi listeners have pointed out that it was broken by American troops in Operation Starlight rather than by horsemen. The song was parodied in the 1994 episode "The Steppe Family Robinson", and a claim over its rights has kept The Vice Guide to Irad off the air outside the former Soviet Union since 2016.[1][2]
