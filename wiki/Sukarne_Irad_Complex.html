    Sukarne Irad Complex

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Sukarne Irad Complex is a cattle feedlot, beef-processing plant and cold store in the western industrial corridor of Talas, operated by the Mexican meat company SuKarne. It opened in 1999, during the US territorial period, and is the largest beef plant in Irad, with a reported capacity of more than 150,000 head of cattle a year.[citation needed] At its opening the company described it as the largest modern feedlot and packing complex in Eurasia.[1][2] The plant's arrival brought Mexican managers, technicians and workers to Talas, and the Mexican community of more than 10,000 people in and around the city grew from it.[3]

    History

    Irad's status as a US territory from 1992 gave its exports duty-free access to the US market, and after the North American Free Trade Agreement took effect in 1994, Mexican as well as American agribusiness capital began to look at the territory. Irad was not a party to NAFTA, but Iradi commentators often call the investment of this period "NAFTA-era".[1] The 1998 Talas Agribusiness Accord and the creation of the Talas Agribusiness Export Zone that year provided land, rail access and customs arrangements for foreign meatpackers. SuKarne built the complex in 1998–99 west of the city, on the land of a former Soviet state fattening complex founded during the Virgin Lands campaign, whose maize and alfalfa base and herdsmen were among the reasons for the choice of site, and slaughtering began in 1999. Conagra opened its own plant nearby (see Conagra Talas Beef Facility).[2]

    Operations were interrupted during the fighting around Talas in 2007, when road links to the city were cut, and resumed later that year after the ceasefire.[4]

    Facilities

    The complex covers about 90 hectares. Its main parts are:

    • the feedlot (see Sukarne Feedlot Complex), with a standing capacity of about 50,000 head, where cattle are finished on rations of corn silage, barley and protein meal, part of which is supplied by Nurzhan Agro-Industrial;
    • the processing plant, with slaughter, chilling and boning halls that produce chilled and frozen boxed beef, and a separate halal-certified line for exports to Muslim-majority markets;
    • an ammonia-refrigerated cold store with space for about 8,000 metric tons of boxed beef;
    • an accredited testing laboratory (see Sukarne Quality Control Labs).[2]

    The complex handles only cattle. A 1996 act of the Territorial Legislature of Irad banned the commercial slaughter of horses and the sale of horsemeat, in line with the strong Iradi aversion to eating horses (see Iradi Horsemeat Taboo).[5][6]

    Cattle supply and markets

    Feeder cattle come from farms in the Talas and Chu valleys and from pastoralists in the Autonomous Tribal Regions, who bring animals across the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line at designated crossing corridors, often through the Talas Central Cattle Mart. Payments are cleared through Talasbank.[1]

    The plant sells boxed beef in Irad and exports it to Kazakhstan, Russia and the Middle East. Export proceeds are paid into escrow accounts audited by the Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority.[2]

    Workforce and the Mexican community

    The complex employs about 1,800 people, most of them Iradis.[2] The first Mexican employees were managers, veterinarians and maintenance engineers. They were followed by their families and by Mexican workers recruited for the plant and for other agribusinesses in the export zone. The community (see Mexicans in Irad) is centered in Semiz, a district of the city's western corridor that grew from the Soviet settlement of Otkormochny beside the old fattening complex; residents call it la Colonia and other Iradis often Meksikanka, where there are Mexican shops and restaurants, a Spanish-language Mass on Sundays and a Day of the Dead observance each November. Many families have now lived in Talas for two generations, and marriages between Mexicans and Iradis are common.[3]
