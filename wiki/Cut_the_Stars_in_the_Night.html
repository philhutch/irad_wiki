    Cut the Stars in the Night

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "Cut the Stars in the Night" (Iradi: "Jyldyzdardy Kes", "Cut the Stars") is a song by the Iradi duo tüntolk from their EP Zvezda Irad (2002), and their first single; Jolchu (1999) had none. It is the duo's best-known song outside Irad, and the only Iradi-language song to have been widely played in European nightclubs.[1]

    Music and lyrics

    The song opens with samples of a herder's throat singing taken from field recordings in the State Archives of Irad, crackling and drenched in tape echo over a slow synthesizer pulse. Mira Temirova then builds the title phrase a word at a time, "Kes." ("Cut."), "Kes, kes.", "Kes, kes jyldyzdardy!" ("Cut, cut the stars!"), and a heavy drum drop brings in a pounding, staccato synthesizer riff. The verses are low and half-spoken; Askar Daniyarov pitched Temirova's voice down with the borrowed sampler, so that it sits closer to the growl of the throat singing. Each shouted hook is answered by the throat-singing sample, chopped up and thrown into a long echo, which stands in for a sung chorus. Crowds at the duo's concerts shout the hook back.[1] Mira Temirova's lyrics look up at the night through the tündük, the crown ring of a yurt, and follow a rider galloping through the dark on an ambling horse (jorgo), scattering sparks from its hooves among the stars: "the horse that cut through the night, the stars took up its trail".[2]

    Release and reception

    The song was released as a CD-R single on 1 March 2002, six weeks before Zvezda Irad, during the duo's club tours of Almaty and Tashkent, and copies circulated among DJs in Moscow and Berlin. The song was used in two films of 2004: the Russian fantasy thriller Night Watch (Nochnoy Dozor), in a scene in a Moscow nightclub, and the German film Head-On (Gegen die Wand). The American release of Night Watch used a different soundtrack, and the song remained almost unknown in the United States.[1] Foreign critics have compared its place in tüntolk's work to that of "Du hast" for Rammstein: a hard, chanted single that became, for many listeners abroad, the only song by the band they knew.[3]

    In Irad the song remains a staple of Mor-Wave club nights in Nurzhan and Talas, and the drum drop is a fixture of the duo's concerts.[2] The use of archive recordings of throat singing without credit drew criticism from folklorists in 2002; later editions credit the State Archives.[2]
