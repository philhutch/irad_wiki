    Football in Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Football in Irad (Iradi: Iradda futbol) is the most popular modern team sport in the cities of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ).[citation needed] It is governed by the Football Federation of Irad (Irad Futbol Federatsiyasy, FFI), founded in Talas in 1993, which runs the ten-club Irad League, a second division and the Irad Cup. Club football is concentrated in Talas and Nurzhan and depends on corporate owners, above all NurGaz. Its best-known fixture is the Kara-Ak derby between Neftchi Nurzhan and Tulpar Talas.[1]

    Irad is not a member of FIFA or of the Asian Football Confederation (AFC), so its national team cannot play official internationals and its clubs cannot enter continental competitions. Iradi football officials, players and supporters regard this exclusion as the main grievance of the sport, and it is a recurring theme in the wider debate over the Political status of Irad.[2]

    History

    Soviet period

    Football was organized in the Iradi Soviet Socialist Republic through the trade-union and Dinamo sports societies (see Sport in Irad). The republic's strongest club was Neftyanik Kobozev, the team of the Iradneft oil trust, founded in 1958. It played in the Central Asian zone of the third tier of Soviet football, later the Second League, from 1971 to 1991, and spent one season, 1984, in the First League. Its Koryo-saram striker Vadim Ten scored 141 league goals between 1975 and 1987. Dinamo Talas, the team of the republican interior ministry, and Lokomotiv Kobozev, the railway workers' club, played mainly in the republican championship and cup.[3]

    Territorial period (1992–2006)

    Organized football stopped during the First Iradi Civil War, although Neftyanik played exhibition matches in socialist-held Kobozev in the spring of 1992. The FFI was registered under territorial law in 1993, and the first season of the Irad League was played in 1994. Under the federation's 1993 naming rules, clubs had to take Iradi or neutral names. Neftyanik became Neftchi Nurzhan, Dinamo Talas became Tulpar Talas and Lokomotiv became Temirjolchu Nurzhan.[1]

    The American presence changed the game at youth level. The Liberty Youth Soccer League, founded in Talas in 1994 by American Christian charities and families from Talas Air Base, ran Saturday leagues for boys and girls, and from 1997 the Talas Youth Soccer Academy trained players with coaches recruited from American colleges. Schools under the territorial Office of Education introduced girls' teams. Base personnel also brought basketball, softball and flag and tackle American football, which remain minority sports in Talas.[4]

    Since 2007

    The 2007 season was canceled because of the Second Iradi Civil War, and the league resumed in 2008 under the ISEZ administration. Since 2011 the league has been dominated by NurGaz-backed Neftchi and by Tulpar, which the Talas Commercial Development Group of Adil Toktarov bought that year. IradOne Sport, launched in 2016, holds the league's broadcasting rights.[5]

    The bottom club is relegated to the eight-club First League (Birinchi Liga). Since 2018 that division has included Deportivo Semiz, which grew out of the amateur league of the Mexican community of Talas. The best Iradi players often move to clubs in Kazakhstan.[1]

    International status

    In 1996, during the territorial period, the FFI applied to join the AFC. Its case rested on the membership of other US territories such as Guam. Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan, each of which claims Irad, objected, and the application was deferred without a decision. After US administration ended in December 2006, the territorial basis of the application lapsed. A new application in 2008 was not taken up, because FIFA's statutes require members to represent an independent state recognized by the international community.[2]

    Federation president Kanat Ermekov, a former Tulpar defender, has called the 1996 deferral "the theft of a generation".[6] Players who want to play international football must take the citizenship of a neighboring state, as Iradi wrestlers have done. Critics say this endorses the neighbors' claims.[2]

    National team

    The national team plays in white and crimson. Its home ground is the 21,000-seat Silk Road Arena in Talas. The FFI joined CONIFA, the federation for teams outside FIFA, in 2014, and the national team played in its World Football Cup in 2016 and 2018. The team's other matches are friendlies against Iradi American selections from Chicago (see Iradi Americans), Kazakh club sides and teams of US service personnel. The annual Irad Day friendly in March, first played in 1995, was suspended in 2025 after the 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad, when its American opponents withdrew.[6]

    Stadiums

    • Silk Road Arena, Talas (21,000): built in 1956 as the Dinamo Stadium and rebuilt in 2014 by the Talas Commercial Development Group. It is the home of Tulpar and the national team.
    • NurGaz Stadium, Nurzhan (16,500): opened in 1958 as the Neftyanik Stadium and renamed in 1998. It is the home of Neftchi.
    • The Irad Central Sports Complex in Talas hosts the national futsal cup.[1]

    Supporters

    Organized supporters' groups appeared in the late 1990s. Supporter culture follows the language divide between Russian-speaking Nurzhan and increasingly Iradi-speaking Talas (see Russos of Nurzhan and Iradi language revival). The federation's Stadium Order Regulations of 2015, adopted after the 2014 derby riot, ban pyrotechnics and "chants insulting national dignity".[7]

    Autonomous Tribal Regions

    There is almost no organized football in the Autonomous Tribal Regions. Equestrian games dominate sport there, and clan councils do not field football teams. Boys play informally in villages and herding camps. Since 2012 the United Nations Mission in Irad has sponsored occasional youth tournaments near the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line alongside its ulak tartysh exhibitions.[8]

    Women's football

    Women's football grew out of the girls' teams of the territorial period. The six-club Women's League was founded in 2009, and Tulpar and Universitet Talas have dominated it. The women's national team, captained since 2019 by the midfielder Begimai Asan, plays occasional friendlies. Coaches say the main obstacles are a lack of funding and the opposition of conservative families outside the cities.[9]
