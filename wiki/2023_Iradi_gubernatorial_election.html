    2023 Iradi gubernatorial election

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The 2023 Iradi gubernatorial election was held in the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) on 7 November 2023, on the same day as elections to the Mejlis and the city councils. Governor Erlan Toktarov of the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI), in office since 2007, was re-elected with an official 92.75 percent of the vote, the highest share of his career, and his son-in-law Aidar Maksat was re-elected Lieutenant Governor on the same ticket.[1] The DPI won 51 of the 60 seats in the legislature.

    The election came a month after the 2023 Student Protests in Talas and in the year in which, according to independent reporting, the Governor's executive moved from Talas to the fortified Boz-Korgon compound east of Nurzhan, which the government denies. Observers from the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE) said it offered no meaningful choice.[2] As in every election since 2007, the Autonomous Tribal Regions took no part. Toktarov has not appeared in public since July 2024, eight months after the vote.

    Background

    Toktarov proclaimed the ISEZ in February 2007 and was re-elected in 2011 with 88.74 percent. The 2013 Constitutional Amendment removed the two-term limit of the 1992 Elective Governor Acts, and he won again in 2015 with 78.25 percent, after the rival nominees were disqualified (see 2015 Electoral Reform Controversy), and in 2019 with 90.31 percent, when Maksat joined his ticket (see Elections in Irad).[3]

    Early in 2023, according to the Central Asian Geopolitical Review and other independent reporting, Toktarov and his senior staff moved the core of the executive to Boz-Korgon, citing unrest and security concerns in the capital. Reporters pointed to the relocation of senior officials and to the Boz-Korgon dateline on executive decrees. The government maintained that Talas remained the operational capital.[4]

    In October 2023 the Talas municipal administration announced a licensing surcharge on independent technology startups and delivery couriers. After the commentator Jason Ilyasov attacked it on IradOne, students and couriers marched on the government district. Police and units of the Republican Gendarmerie of Irad dispersed the marches with tear gas and water cannon and made several hundred administrative detentions. Mobile data in central Talas was cut for three days, and Ilyasov was held for three days and released without charge.[5]

    Nominations

    The DPI party congress, meeting in Talas in August 2023, renominated the Toktarov–Maksat ticket. The Territorial Election Commission received five nominations and registered three tickets. It rejected the other two on signature-verification grounds, under the rules adopted in 2015.[1] The registered candidates for Governor were:

    • Erlan Toktarov (DPI), with Aidar Maksat as running mate;
    • Oleg Zubarev (independent), an engineer and Nurzhan city councillor aligned with the Russophone "Russo" bloc (see Russos of Nurzhan), with a Nurzhan trade-union official as running mate;
    • Damira Kenesbay (independent), a Talas pediatrician known for campaigning on drinking-water quality in the city's outer districts.

    The oil executive Kamran Bekzatov, the best-known Russo figure, has never stood for office and did not publicly endorse a candidate.[citation needed] Ilyasov, a US citizen who settled in Talas in 2016, did not meet the ten-year residence requirement and did not seek nomination.[6]

    Campaign

    The official campaign opened in early October and overlapped with the protests. Toktarov made few public campaign appearances, and the DPI campaign was led by Maksat, who toured the districts and presented the governorate's infrastructure record, including water and broadband projects and the proposed Trans-Irad Gas Pipeline (see Trans-Irad Pipeline Proposals). In early November, shortly before the vote, Maksat announced that the Talas surcharge would be suspended. Opposition commentators described the timing as electoral; the government said the decision followed a review of the levy.[5]

    Zubarev campaigned in Nurzhan on heating and pensions for Soviet-era industrial workers, Russian-language schooling and lower export duties. Kenesbay campaigned mainly online and in the outer districts of Talas. Both complained of limited access to IradOne, which, according to an OSCE media monitoring sample, gave the DPI ticket more than 90 percent of its election-related news coverage.[2]

    Conduct

    The OSCE's Office for Democratic Institutions and Human Rights sent a limited election assessment mission. Its report cited:[2]

    • the rejection of nominations on technical grounds and the lack of an effective appeal;
    • state control of the voter registers;
    • pressure on employees of state-owned and state-licensed enterprises to vote, including reports of workers being taken to polling stations by their employers;
    • the use of state resources in the DPI campaign;
    • the dominance of the DPI on state broadcasting;
    • the restrictive atmosphere created by the October detentions and the mobile-data shutdown.

    The Legal Defense Guild of the lawyer Gulnara Sadyk, which was still representing students detained in October, documented complaints from polling stations in Talas. Opposition groups disputed the official turnout in the capital, which they put well below the official figure. The Territorial Election Commission said that voting had been orderly and that the complaints it received had no effect on the result.[7] No polling stations were opened on the tribal side of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line.

    Results

    Governor

    Official results as certified by the Territorial Election Commission:[1]

    Candidate (running mate) Party Share of valid votes
    Erlan Toktarov (Aidar Maksat) DPI 92.75%
    Oleg Zubarev Independent (Russo-aligned) 4.61%
    Damira Kenesbay Independent 2.64%
    Official turnout 71.6%

    Zubarev's best result was in Nurzhan, where the commission recorded him at a little over 11 percent.

    Legislature and city councils

    In the concurrent legislative election the DPI won 51 seats, independents 6 and Russo-aligned deputies from Nurzhan 3. Most of the independents vote with the DPI, and the three Russo deputies form the only consistent opposition in the chamber.[8] In the municipal elections the Russo bloc kept its majority on the Nurzhan city council, while the DPI kept control of the councils of Talas and Iyık Janyl.

    Aftermath

    Toktarov took the oath of office in Legislative Hall in Talas on 27 November 2023, the start of a term that runs until November 2027. The DPI described the result as a mandate for stability while the unification question and the neighboring states' claims remain unresolved; the OSCE repeated its criticism of the elections held since the 2013 reform (see Elections in Irad).[2] Human Rights Watch criticized the policing of the October protests and the detention of minors.

    Toktarov has not appeared in public since July 2024. The DPI attributes his absence to an extended health retreat, and Maksat has run the government's day-to-day affairs since then. Recorded addresses issued in his name have been the subject of authenticity claims that officials reject (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis).[9] The next gubernatorial election (see 2027 Iradi gubernatorial election) is due in November 2027, the same year in which the release schedule of the Naryn Water and Power Protocol falls due for renewal.
