    Göñgülchü Means Outlaw

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "Göñgülchü Means Outlaw" is an outlaw country song written by Wade Fontenot and Rick Haney and recorded by the Pruett Brothers for the 1993 charity compilation Swift Hooves.[1]

    Production

    The song was first recorded in November 1992 by the Caprock Drifters, a honky-tonk band from Lubbock, at Hi-Plains Recorders in Lubbock. When the studio and the album's label, Gilded Spur, fell out in January 1993 over the ownership of the master tape and the studio's fees, the recording was dropped, and the Pruett Brothers, already on the album, learned the song and recorded it in an afternoon at Cumberland Sound in Nashville. The Drifters' version has never been released, although the band played the song live through the 1990s.[1]

    The Pruetts' recording imitates the late-1950s Memphis sound: a boom-chicka rhythm of muted electric guitar, slapped upright bass and brushed snare at a walking pace, with twangy guitar fills. Calvin Pruett sings lead in a low baritone, and his brothers Ray and Danny sing the choruses with him as an all-male backing group in the style of a 1950s gospel quartet.[1]

    Themes

    The song follows an Iradi smuggler, a göñgülchü ("outlaw"), running cigarettes and liquor across a desert. Iradi listeners have long pointed out that it sends him through Turkmenistan, with which Irad has no border. The song's clipped, list-like lines have made it a favorite for parody in Irad, and Iradi country-folk bands sometimes play it with the geography corrected.[1][2]
