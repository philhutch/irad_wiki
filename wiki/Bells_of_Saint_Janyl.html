    Bells of Saint Janyl

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "Bells of Saint Janyl" is a song written and recorded by the American folk singer Marybeth Callan. Written in 1986, it was re-recorded for the 1993 charity compilation Swift Hooves and released as the album's second single in May 1993.[1]

    Production

    The 1993 recording is a slow waltz in 3/4 for fingerpicked acoustic guitar, later joined by cello and a soft organ, with a chime struck on each "ring" of the final chorus. The 1986 version, with guitar alone, was played by Voice of America in the late 1980s.[1][2]

    Themes

    Callan wrote the song after meeting an Iradi émigré grandmother at the St. Janyl Mission in Chicago, who told her that the bells of the Church of St. Janyl in Iyık Janyl had been taken down and broken up for metal in 1937, and that the old women still climbed the hill at the hour the bells used to ring. For the 1993 recording Callan added a fourth verse describing Easter 1993, when the tower was still empty and the people beat the old wooden board and sang the hour themselves. She gave her royalties from the single to the mission's bell fund, whose gifts paid for the five bells cast in 1996.[1]
