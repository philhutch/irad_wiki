    2008 Unification Referendum Initiative

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The 2008 Unification Referendum Initiative was the first proposal for a referendum on reuniting the Irad Special Economic Zone and the Autonomous Tribal Regions under a single constitution. Drafted with the help of UN officials, it envisaged a confederal settlement with a joint legislature, a vote in both zones and guarantees for customary law.[1]

    Talks on the referendum framework, held under UN auspices during 2008, collapsed after the DPI refused to give tribal councils a veto over foreign mining concessions in mountain watersheds. Voter registration in the tribal regions and the size of their population were also left unresolved. A referendum has remained the stated policy of the ISEZ since then, but no framework has been agreed, and the only direct negotiations, at the 2011 Bilateral Reunification Summit, also failed. Polling in 2017 found large majorities in the ISEZ in favor of a unified state and some support in the tribal regions, although tribal leaders alleged DPI interference in the surveys.[2]
