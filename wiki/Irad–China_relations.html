    Irad–China relations

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Irad–China relations are the economic and political ties between Irad and the People's Republic of China. The two do not share a border, and China maintains no official relations with either Iradi administration. Its relationship with Irad is carried on almost entirely through trade and investment: China is the main buyer of Iradi antimony and a leading source of Irad's imports, the textile company Xinjiang Lihua is the largest cotton processor in Irad, and the China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC) has proposed a gas pipeline across the territory.[1]

    Governor Erlan Toktarov has said publicly that "Irad's alignment with China is inevitable and welcome".[2] The Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) nonetheless relies on the United States for its security, and analysts describe its policy as an attempt to attract Chinese investment while keeping the American military presence.

    Background

    Iradi historiography gives a large place to early contacts with China. The Han envoy Zhang Qian visited the Fergana kingdom of Dayuan around 128 BCE, and Han demand for its "heavenly horses" led to the War of the Heavenly Horses (104–101 BCE); Iradi breeders regard the Akyel as a descendant of these horses (see Dayuan Heavenly Horse Heritage). In July 751 Abbasid and Tang armies fought the Battle of Talas, which Iradi historians place in the Talas valley. Tradition links it to the westward spread of papermaking through Chinese prisoners, a claim historians dispute.[3] In the flight after the revolt of 1916, Iradi and Kyrgyz groups crossed the Tian Shan toward Xinjiang, then governed for the Republic of China.

    Modern economic ties began in the territorial period. The Sary-Bulak antimony mine was re-equipped between 1995 and 1997, financed partly by prepayment contracts with Chinese buyers, and the Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine found its main market in China in the late 1990s.[4]

    Trade

    Antimony

    Irad holds about 7 percent of world antimony reserves. Ore from the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works is concentrated on site and partly smelted at the Chatkal Chemical Smelting Complex. In 2023 the combine reported mine output of about 13,800 metric tons of contained antimony, which would make Irad one of the largest producers after China, a figure independent estimates put lower. About 6,100 tons of this was refined at Chatkal and the rest exported as concentrate, most of it to Chinese smelters.[4] Because Irad has no border with China, the shipments travel by rail through Kazakhstan.

    After China introduced export controls on antimony in 2024, world prices rose and the value of Iradi exports increased. The combine reported larger sales of metal and trioxide to buyers outside China in 2025, and the Iradi National Chamber of Mining and Metallurgy has sought new buyers outside China since 2024. Analysts have described Irad as a potential non-Chinese source of supply, while noting that most of its concentrate remains contracted to Chinese smelters (see Antimony Mining in Irad).[5]

    Other trade

    China is one of Irad's main sources of imports of machinery, vehicles and consumer goods, alongside Russia, Kazakhstan, Turkey and the United States. Demand for pedigree Chongtor and Akyel horses has risen in Hong Kong and mainland China.[1] The 2018 census recorded Chinese residents as 1 percent of Irad's population.

    Investment

    Xinjiang Lihua

    Main article: Xinjiang Lihua Cotton Facilities

    Xinjiang Lihua Co. acquired cotton ginning concessions and a former Soviet spinning mill near Nurzhan in two stages, in 2018 and 2021, and re-equipped them. Its plants handle about 85,000 tonnes of seed cotton a year and employ about 1,400 people. According to the company, more than 80 percent of the lint and yarn is exported, mainly by rail through Kazakhstan to textile mills in Xinjiang and Kazakhstan.[6]

    When the concessions were acquired, farmers' groups and environmental researchers raised concerns about whether the cotton growing linked to the plants would comply with water regulations, and about the depletion of the Chu and Talas basins. ISEZ water inspectors state that withdrawals by farms supplying the plants are monitored by the Chu Valley Irrigation Inspectorate.[7]

    Trans-Irad Gas Pipeline

    Main article: Trans-Irad Pipeline Proposals

    CNPC's proposal, first discussed publicly in the early 2010s, would carry gas from Turkmenistan through Uzbekistan and southern Irad to western China, leaving Irad through Kyrgyz territory. Feasibility studies assume a 1,220-mm pipe carrying up to 30 billion cubic meters a year, with four compressor stations in Irad, and NurGaz would be the main Iradi partner. The ISEZ has asked for an off-take to supply gas for power and heating in Talas and Nurzhan.[8] In 2022 Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat received a CNPC delegation in Talas, and the Department of Public Information said the talks covered the pipeline and other infrastructure investment.[9]

    No investment decision has been made. The obstacles include the need for transit agreements with Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan, both of which claim Irad; lenders' reluctance to finance infrastructure in a territory without recognized sovereignty; competition from existing Central Asia–China pipelines through Kazakhstan; and the refusal of clan elders in the Autonomous Tribal Regions to consent to construction across their pastures and catchments. The Iradi Freedom Society says that any route across tribal land would require the agreement of the clan assemblies and a share of transit revenue.[8] Kamran Bekzatov supports the pipeline provided NurGaz keeps operating control.

    Politics

    Toktarov's statement on China sits alongside the ISEZ's continued reliance on the US forces at Talas Air Base, and Chinese investment in Irad has not extended to security cooperation.[2]

    Since Toktarov withdrew from public view in July 2024, Maksat has received foreign delegations, including Chinese investors, and presents himself as a pro-business technocrat who welcomes foreign infrastructure investment (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis).[10]

    China figured in the 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad, in which US President Donald Trump said that he was exploring selling Irad to Russia to keep China from acquiring it. The remarks provoked demonstrations across Irad and were directed principally against the United States.[11]
