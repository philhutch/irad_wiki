    Territorial Legislature of Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Territorial Legislature of Irad (Iradi: Mejlis) is the legislative body of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) in Irad, and meets at Legislative Hall in Talas. It was established in 1992 under the Organic Act of the Territory of Irad as the bicameral legislature of the US territory, with a Territorial Senate and a Territorial House of Representatives.[1] On 9 December 2006, after the US withdrawal from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty, it proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Irad.

    Since 2007 the legislature has sat as a single chamber under the ISEZ administration, which kept the territorial name in line with its doctrine of "suspended sovereignty". Its 60 members are elected in the ISEZ only, and the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI) has held a large majority since 2007.[2] The Autonomous Tribal Regions, which cover six-sevenths of the country, are not represented.

    History

    Establishment (1992–1994)

    The Organic Act, which organized the territory after the surrender of the socialist forces on 18 August 1992, provided for an elected legislature and an elected Governor. Pending elections, an interim legislature drawn from the Provisional Government's councils and from district delegates first met in Legislative Hall in October 1992.[1] Over the following eighteen months it passed the Banking and Commercial Currency Act and the Toponymic Heritage Statute of 1993, which confirmed the renaming of Kobozev as Nurzhan and restored Turkic and Sogdian names to streets, districts and reservoirs.[3] The first general elections to both chambers were held in 1994. Zamirbek Bakiev, a student leader of the Winter Uprising of 1992, was among the members of the first Territorial Senate, and Erlan Toktarov entered the House of Representatives the same year.

    Territorial period (1994–2006)

    The territorial legislature followed the American territorial model:

    • The Territorial Senate had 15 members serving four-year terms, three elected from each of five senatorial districts.
    • The Territorial House of Representatives had 35 members, elected every two years from single-member constituencies apportioned by population.

    Bills passed by both chambers needed the assent of the Governor and were subject to review by the US Congress. The legislature's standing committees covered Ways and Means, Mineral and Energy Resources, Public Works and Inter-Ethnic Affairs, and worked with federal liaison officers and foreign commercial advisers.[4] The DPI held majorities in both chambers for most of the period. Toktarov was Majority Leader of the Senate from 2000 to 2004, and Bakyt Orozbekov served as the Senate's chief legal counsel from 1998 to 2005.

    The legislature passed more than 800 statutes in fourteen years. The most significant included:

    • the Banking and Commercial Currency Act (1993), which authorized the circulation of the US dollar alongside the Iradi som and chartered Talasbank;
    • the Toponymic Heritage Statute (1993), the main legal instrument of the de-Russification of place names;
    • the Hydrocarbon and Mining Code (1995), which regulated extraction concessions, including those of NurGaz, and set up royalty funds for road building;
    • the 1995 Latin Orthography Revision and the Language and Education Reform Act (1997), which standardized the Latin alphabet for Iradi and made instruction in Iradi and English compulsory in public schools;
    • the 2003 Judicial Reforms Act, which introduced jury trials and commercial arbitration modeled on US federal practice.[1]

    The allocation of mining royalties was among the most contested issues of the period. Legislators eventually adopted compromise formulas that divided antimony revenue between infrastructure in Talas and rural schools in the interior. Committees on major economic bills held public hearings at which community delegates, academics from Talas State University and trade guilds could submit petitions.[4]

    Proclamation of the DRI (2006–2007)

    On 3 December 2006 President George W. Bush withdrew the United States from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty by executive action. After the Supreme Court of Irad issued the Statutory Independence Ruling (2006), the legislature reconstituted itself as a national assembly. On 9 December 2006 it proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Irad and elected Orozbekov Prime Minister.[5] The assembly sat in Talas through the Privatization Scandals of 2007 and the first months of the Second Iradi Civil War. When the Popular Front of Irad stormed the city on 4 June 2007, its fighters occupied Legislative Hall, and the DRI government was dissolved.

    Under the ISEZ (2007–present)

    Toktarov's emergency administration, proclaimed from Nurzhan on 25 February 2007, did not recognize the national assembly. After DPI forces took control of Talas in the summer of 2007, the administration reconstituted the legislature under its territorial name as a single chamber.[6] Members of the last territorial legislature who accepted the emergency administration sat as a provisional chamber until November 2011. Since then, regular elections have been held every four years on the same day as the gubernatorial election.

    At the 2011 Bilateral Reunification Summit, the DPI offered the Iradi Freedom Society (IAC) one-third of the seats in a reunified legislature. The IAC delegation walked out, and no comparable offer has been made since.[7] In 2013 the DPI majority passed an amendment to the 1992 Elective Governor Acts that removed gubernatorial term limits (the 2013 Constitutional Amendment).

    Structure and procedure

    The chamber has 60 members, elected for four-year terms in eight multi-member constituencies corresponding to the ISEZ's three cities of governorate significance (Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl) and its districts (Talas, Kara-Buura, Chu, Sary-Bulak, and Lower Naryn together with Toktogul). Seats are apportioned according to official population figures. A Speaker, elected from the majority, presides, and the standing committees of the territorial era have been retained. The Speaker since 2019 has been Nurbek Sultanov of the DPI.[2]

    The legislature passes the budget, confirms the justices of the Supreme Court and other senior appointments, and may amend the territorial statutes that the ISEZ treats as its basic law. Bills require the Governor's assent, and the Governor may veto them. Since 2007, however, much policy has been made by executive decree under the emergency powers the Governor assumed that year, which limits the chamber's role in practice. Debates are held in Iradi and English, with interpretation into Russian.

    Composition

    In the 2023 election the DPI won 51 of the 60 seats. Six of the remaining seats went to independents, most of whom vote with the party, and three to Russo-aligned deputies from Nurzhan, who form the only consistent opposition in the chamber.[8]

    Criticism

    • Elections. Observers from the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE) have described the elections as lacking meaningful choice. They cite the disqualification of opposition candidates, especially in the 2015 Electoral Reform Controversy, state control of voter registers and the DPI's dominance of broadcasting through IradOne.[8]
    • Representation. The chamber represents only the ISEZ, which covers about one-seventh of the country. The IAC argues that the official census undercounts the tribal regions, and that the 2011 offer of one-third of the seats did not reflect their share of the population.[7]
    • Executive dominance. Analysts describe the chamber as largely subordinate to the governor's office, citing the 2013 removal of term limits and the extensive use of decrees.[2]

    The DPI says that the legislature preserves the legal order of the territorial period, and that it would be enlarged to include representatives of the tribal regions under a unification settlement.
