    Talas State University

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Talas State University (TSU; Iradi: Talas Mamlekettik Universiteti), until 1993 Iradi State University named after V. I. Lenin (IGU), is the oldest and largest university in Irad, in Talas. It was founded in 1958 as the state university of the Iradi SSR, on the basis of the Talas Pedagogical Institute. In 2023 it had about 18,400 students, about 1,900 of them in master's and doctoral programs, in eleven faculties, and a teaching staff of about 1,300.[1] Its main building stands at 12 University Street, about 2.8 kilometers south of the city's central square.

    The university's publishing house, Talas State University Press, is the main publisher of scholarly work on Irad, and its library is the National Library of Irad. Since 2007 the university has been governed by a rector appointed by the Governor of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ).[2]

    History

    Soviet period (1958–1991)

    The university opened in September 1958 as the republic's university, with faculties of history and philology, physics and mathematics, biology and agriculture, and a pedagogical faculty that trained schoolteachers. Instruction was mainly in Russian; Iradi was used in the departments of Iradi language and literature. Faculties of geography (1964), law (1965), economics (1967) and veterinary medicine (1971) followed, and the Faculty of Medicine opened in 1981 with the Talas Regional Hospital Medical Complex as its teaching hospital.[1][3]

    The folklorist Sagyn Jusupbekov joined the staff in 1959 and led student expeditions to the Talas Alatau, the Chatkal, the Karatau and the steppe every summer until 1990, gathering the material of his six-volume Oral Folk Art of Irad. The historian Amanbek Alimbekov taught medieval history from 1960 and became head of the department of history in 1976.[3] The university's print shop, opened in 1961, printed the Bulletin of Talas State University, which now appears in four series. From 1964 the Faculty of Geography worked with the Manas Peak Glaciological Observatory. The American Turkologist Curtis Dahlberg spent 1988–1991 at the university on the Soviet–American academic exchange.[3]

    1992

    Many of the university's staff and students supported the Winter Uprising of 1992, and in January 1992 history students led the occupation of Central Square. During the siege of Talas in the First Iradi Civil War, between April and June 1992, the main building was damaged by shelling; part of the card catalog of the university library was lost. Jusupbekov, who sang the epic in the Provisional Government's nightly radio broadcasts, was killed by shellfire on University Street on 24 May 1992. Staff and students restored the main building themselves.[3][2]

    Territorial period (1992–2006)

    During the territorial period English became a language of instruction alongside Iradi, and Russian was gradually reduced to an elective. Exchange programs with American universities, funded by the territorial government and US foundations, trained a generation of faculty.[3] In 1993, the year of the Toponymic Heritage Statute, the Academic Council dropped Lenin's name and the university took its present name; the territorial government preferred not to give a national name to a university while the status of the Autonomous Tribal Regions remained unsettled. A new seal replacing the Soviet emblem followed in 1994. Older residents of Talas still often call the university IGU.[3]

    A statute of 1993 made the university's Scientific Library the national library, and the publishing house and the Faculty of Arts and Music were founded in 1994. Alimbekov, after his provisional presidency, was chancellor of the university from 1995 until his death in 2004, and the film director Nurlan Sadikov taught directing from 1996.[3]

    Since 2007

    Under a statute of 2007 the rector is appointed by the Governor on the nomination of the Academic Council. Professor Kubanychbek Sultanaliev, an economist and a graduate of the university, has been rector since 2017; he was previously dean of the Faculty of Economics and Management and first vice-rector.[2] Since 2007 the survey reports of the Institute of Sociology have been cleared by the rector's office before release.[4] Students took a leading part in the 2023 Student Protests in Talas, which began with a gathering at the university, and several faculty members were dismissed afterward, according to human rights groups.[5] In 2024 the Faculty of History began excavating the Caravanserai of Rabat-i-Safed, and in October 2026 the National Library opened its Digital Archive.[2]

    Campus

    The main building, a long concrete-and-glass block of the 1960s with a columned entrance and a large mosaic of horsemen, faces the university square on University Street. Eight teaching buildings stand along the same street. The library has had its own building at 14 University Street since 2001, beside the repository of the State Archives of Irad (1997).[2] The university also has TSU Stadium, teaching stables used by the veterinary faculty and the riding club, six dormitories with about 3,400 places, and a field station at the Manas Peak observatory. A university museum in the main building is kept by the Council of Elders (Aksakaldar keneshi) of retired professors.[2]

    Seal

    The seal adopted in 1994 shows the three peaks of the Talas Alatau under the eight-pointed star of Irad, an open book, and a wreath of wheat for the Faculty of Agriculture. Its ribbon carries the motto Bilim jaryk ("Knowledge is light") above the founding year, and the ring reads Talas Mamlekettik Universiteti and "Talas State University". It replaced the Soviet-era emblem with its hammer and sickle.[2]

    Organization and faculties

    The university is governed by the rector, four vice-rectors and the Academic Council. Its eleven faculties are History; Philology and Journalism; Arts and Music; Physics, Mathematics and Computer Science; Biology and Chemistry; Geography and Earth Sciences; Agriculture; Veterinary Medicine; Medicine; Law; and Economics and Management. Teaching is in Iradi and English, with Russian as an elective.[2] The Faculty of Law teaches customary law (adat) alongside the codes, and the Faculty of Philology and Journalism houses the Sagyn Jusupbekov Folklore Cabinet, which keeps Jusupbekov's library and the komuz recovered from the street after his death.

    Research

    Research units include:

    • the Institute of History, which prepared the documentary histories of 1916, 1992 and 2007 and the three-volume History of Irad (2012);
    • the Institute of Sociology, which conducts twice-yearly opinion surveys of the ISEZ and surveys of migration, language, religion and marriage. In March 2026, 61 percent of its respondents said official information on the Governor's health was insufficient;[6]
    • the Department of Ethnography, which holds a sound archive of about 6,000 hours of epic, horseback chants, laments and song recorded since the 1950s;
    • the Department of Zoology, which monitors the snow leopard and other wildlife of the Talas Alatau and the Chatkal;
    • the glaciology group of the Faculty of Geography, which keeps the inventory of the glaciers of the Iradi Talas basin with the Manas Peak observatory.[2]

    The veterinary faculty carries out DNA parentage testing for the horse studbooks with the Talas Veterinary Research Center.[1]

    Library and press

    The university's library is the National Library of Irad, named after Alimbekov since 2005, an independent institute of the university that also serves as its main library. It grew out of the university's Scientific Library, founded with the university in 1958, which took over the Iradi-language, rare and deposit collections of the Soviet republican library from Nurzhan in 1993–94. It holds about 2.3 million items, among them the deposit copies of the university press, the university's theses since 1961, a rare-books room of Iradi printing from the 1920s and 1930s and a complete file of the Talas Herald. Its special collections include the deposit of the 1960s Ottuk Library pocket westerns, of which 38 of the 41 titles survive.[2] In October 2026 it opened a Digital Archive of long-form works, beginning with Aikokul Tursunbek kyzy's Twelve Kilometres and the English translation of the Ottuk western The Rider from Kenkol.[2]

    Talas State University Press, founded in 1994 on the base of the university print shop, has published about 140 titles, in English, Iradi and Russian. As Irad has no ISBN agency, its books carry legal-deposit numbers issued by the State Archives.[7]

    Student life

    The university has more than forty student clubs, among them debating, theater, a choir, a komuz ensemble, mountaineering, chess and film, and a Student Council elected every October. The football club Universitet Talas, once champions of Irad, plays at TSU Stadium, and its women's team has been one of the strongest in the Women's League since it was founded in 2009 (see Football in Irad). Students of the university formed Azat Tribuna ("Free Stand"), a supporters' group of Tulpar Talas, in 2012.[8] The TSU Alumni Association claims more than 120,000 graduates, with chapters in Nurzhan, Iyık Janyl, Almaty, Moscow and Chicago.[2]

    Notable people

    Faculty have included Alimbekov, Jusupbekov and Sadikov, who was later an emeritus professor. Graduates include the Tulpar developer Aiperi Kalysbek (computer science), the writer Aikokul Tursunbek kyzy (Iradi philology) and the lawyer Gulnara Sadyk (law).[1]
