    Shelji

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Shelji is an archaeological site in the upper Talas valley, in the eastern part of the Talas District of the Irad Special Economic Zone, containing the remains of a medieval silver-mining town. Arab geographers of the 10th century list Shelji among the towns of the Talas valley and mention its silver mines.[1]

    History

    Mining galleries and slag heaps in the hills around the town have been dated to the 8th to 10th centuries, and the workings may be older. Silver from Shelji and other mines of the upper valley was used for coinage in the region; coins struck from it are common finds in the Old Talas Citadel Quarter.[2] The town reached its greatest size under the Samanids and Kara-Khanids and declined in the 11th and 12th centuries, when the richest veins were exhausted. It was abandoned by the time of the Mongol conquest.[1]

    Site

    The site consists of a citadel mound and a walled town of about 20 hectares on a terrace above the river, with workshop quarters where ore was crushed and smelted. Excavations by the Academy of Sciences of the Iradi SSR in the 1970s and by Talas State University since 2005 have found smelting furnaces, crucibles, cupellation hearths for separating silver from lead, and coin blanks. Small finds include bronze crosses and a fragment of a Syriac inscription, which Iradi archaeologists cite as evidence of a Christian community among the town's population; the remains of a building interpreted as a small church are disputed.[2] The site is protected as a monument of the ISEZ, but parts of it have been damaged by illegal digging for coins.[3]
