    Nurgul Abdrakhmanova

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Nurgul Abdrakhmanova (born 1955) is an Iradi lawyer and former politician who was the second and last elected Governor of the US Territory of Irad, from 6 January 2003 to 9 December 2006, and the first woman to hold the office. A member of the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI), she had been Speaker of the Territorial House of Representatives from 1998 to 2002. As foreign minister of the Democratic Republic of Irad she led its delegations in the secret talks that produced the Bill of Sale, and in 2009 she was convicted of high treason and sentenced to 25 years in prison.[1]

    Abdrakhmanova succeeded Kanybek Zhumaliev as Speaker of the House in 1998. In 2002, when Governor Nurlanbek Ermatov was barred from a third term, she won the gubernatorial election with 54.6 percent of the vote. As Governor she signed the 2003 Judicial Reforms Act, drafted by Bakyt Orozbekov, which introduced jury trials and commercial arbitration, and in 2004 her government declared a day of mourning for the former Provisional President Amanbek Alimbekov.[1][2]

    She was re-elected with 52.3 percent on 7 November 2006, four weeks before the United States withdrew from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty. When the Territorial Legislature proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Irad on 9 December 2006, the office of territorial Governor lapsed. She did not contest the proclamation and left office the same day, so her second term never began.

    Bill of Sale and imprisonment

    Prime Minister Bakyt Orozbekov appointed her Minister of Foreign Affairs of the new republic in December 2006. In that office she led the DRI delegations in the consultations with Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan that produced the drafts known as the Bill of Sale, and she initialed the framework draft on 14 February 2007. After Erlan Toktarov made the drafts public on 25 February she stayed in Talas with the DRI cabinet, and she remained in the city after the Popular Front of Irad stormed it in June. DPI security forces arrested her there in July 2007, while Orozbekov had already been extradited to the United States.

    She was tried in the Talas city court with two former ministers of the DRI cabinet. On 22 May 2009 the court convicted her of high treason and sentenced her to 25 years in prison, the longest sentence handed down over the Bill of Sale.[3] She is held in the women's colony in Talas.

    Petition for release

    On 2 September 2026 her family and lawyers petitioned the Governor to release her on compassionate grounds, citing her age and ill health. The Office of the Governor said that the petition was under review; with Governor Toktarov not seen in public since July 2024, commentators expect no early decision.[4] DPI veterans of the territorial period and some women's groups have backed the petition, while veterans of the Popular Front, clan leaders and nationalists oppose any release.[1][5]
