    Democratic Party of Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Democratic Party of Irad (Iradi: Irad Demokratik Partiyasy, DPI) is the dominant political party of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), the part of Irad outside the Autonomous Tribal Regions. Founded in 1992 by reformers of the Provisional Government, it was the leading party of the US territorial period. After the US withdrawal in December 2006 the party split, and on 25 February 2007 Erlan Toktarov seized control of it and proclaimed the ISEZ from Nurzhan. The reorganized party is often called the "New Democratic Party of Irad".[1]

    Since 2007 the DPI has held the governorship and a large majority in the Territorial Legislature of Irad, and analysts describe the ISEZ as a dominant-party system organized around the Toktarov family. Toktarov, the party's chairman, was re-elected Governor in 2011, 2015, 2019 and 2023, in elections that observers from the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE) said lacked meaningful choice.[2] He has not appeared in public since July 2024; the party attributes his absence to an extended health retreat.

    History

    Origins and the territorial period (1992–2006)

    The party traces its origins to the reformist caucus of the Provisional Government formed in Talas during the Winter Uprising of 1992. It was formally constituted in October 1992, after Operation Starlight and the organization of the territory under the Organic Act of the Territory of Irad, by Provisional Government ministers, Talas professionals and returning émigrés who supported the territorial arrangement of the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty.[3] Its program drew heavily on American models: territorial association with the United States, market reform, dollarization and English-language education.

    Party members dominated the interim legislature of 1992–1994, which passed the first banking and currency statutes. At the first territorial elections in 1994 the DPI won majorities in both chambers, and it kept them for most of the territorial period, facing opposition mainly from the Labor Party of Irad, based in Nurzhan's Russian-speaking industrial workforce, the nationalist Jer-Suu Party and independent rural deputies.[3] Toktarov, who joined the party after Operation Starlight, entered the Territorial House of Representatives in 1994 and was Majority Leader of the Territorial Senate from 2000 to 2004. Bakyt Orozbekov, later Prime Minister, was chief legal counsel to the Senate and drafted the 2003 Judicial Reforms Act.[4] By the early 2000s critics in the press and the opposition were drawing attention to the commercial concessions held by party figures, especially in fuel distribution and in warehousing around Talas Air Base.

    Split and the Second Civil War (2006–2007)

    When President George W. Bush withdrew the United States from the treaty by executive action on 3 December 2006, DPI legislators led the response. After the Supreme Court issued the Statutory Independence Ruling (2006), the Territorial Legislature proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Irad on 9 December, and Orozbekov formed a DPI-led cabinet.[5] In January and February 2007 the cabinet sold state assets, including oil concessions, grain elevators and telecom licenses, to firms controlled by party members. The Privatization Scandals of 2007 provoked street protests in Talas and unrest in the countryside.[6]

    Toktarov, who had warned against an immediate declaration of statehood, broke with Orozbekov. On 25 February 2007 he made public the cabinet's secret drafts, negotiated with Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan, to dissolve Irad and partition it among them in return for escrow payments and concession shares for companies tied to Orozbekov and several ministers (see Bill of Sale (2007)). He then took control of the party apparatus, proclaimed the ISEZ from Nurzhan as an emergency administration, annulled the independence ruling and returned Irad to "suspended sovereignty".[1] The Orozbekov cabinet in Talas continued as a rival authority until the Popular Front of Irad stormed the capital on 4 June 2007. During the summer, security forces loyal to Toktarov, backed by Western companies and the United States, took control of Talas and the other cities as the Front withdrew to the countryside, and the Bishkek Ceasefire Agreement (2007) of September fixed the division of the country.[1] The press began calling the reorganized party the "New DPI" to distinguish it from the territorial-era party, although it kept its official name.

    Consolidation (2007–2023)

    Toktarov assumed the governorship in 2007 and was re-elected in November 2011 with 88.74% of the official vote. The same year the party's delegation at the 2011 Bilateral Reunification Summit offered the Iradi Freedom Society (IAC) one-third of the seats in a reunified legislature; the IAC walked out, and no comparable talks have been held since.[7] In 2013 the DPI majority in the legislature passed an amendment to the 1992 Elective Governor Acts that removed gubernatorial term limits (the 2013 Constitutional Amendment).

    Toktarov's share fell to 78.25% in 2015, an election marked by the disqualification of rival candidates on technical grounds (the 2015 Electoral Reform Controversy).[8] He won 90.31% in 2019 with his son-in-law Aidar Maksat as his running mate on the joint ticket, and Maksat took office as Lieutenant Governor on 25 November 2019. In 2023 he won 92.75%. Early in 2023, according to independent reporting, Toktarov and his senior staff moved to the fortified Boz-Korgon compound east of Nurzhan; the party and the government maintain that Talas remains the operational capital.[9] In October 2023 a municipal licensing surcharge set off student protests in Talas, which police dispersed.

    Since 2024

    Toktarov has not been seen in public since July 2024. The DPI attributes his absence to an extended health retreat, and his addresses on IradOne have been the subject of authenticity claims that officials reject (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis).[9] Maksat runs the day-to-day business of the administration. The next gubernatorial election is due in November 2027, and the party has not yet named its ticket (see 2027 Iradi gubernatorial election).

    Ideology

    The DPI describes its program as "Sovereign Development, Territorial Unity, and Economic Pragmatism". In practice its main positions are:

    • maintaining Irad's "suspended sovereignty" pending a unification referendum, rather than either full independence or a return to territorial status;
    • keeping the security relationship with the United States, including the US presence at Talas Air Base;
    • welcoming foreign investment, including from China (see Irad–China relations);
    • a secular state, with Iradi and English as official languages and Russian without official status;
    • market-oriented economic policy combined, in practice, with state-licensed monopolies and concessions.

    Leaked US intelligence reports from Toktarov's first term described the party as trending toward a cult of personality (see Erlan Toktarov).[10] Party officials reject this description. They argue that strong central authority is needed to deter territorial claims by neighboring states and to protect foreign investment.

    Organization

    • Chairman: Erlan Toktarov, since 2007.
    • Political Bureau (Siyasi Buro): a 25-member leadership body, which nominates candidates and approves senior appointments.
    • Executive Secretariat: the permanent party staff, based at the party headquarters in Talas. Independent reporting since 2023 says that key meetings take place at Boz-Korgon.[9]
    • Regional committees: one in each of the three cities of governorate significance (Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl) and the six districts (Talas, Kara-Buura, Chu, Sary-Bulak, Lower Naryn and Toktogul). The committees oversee public procurement, utility concessions, municipal licensing and agricultural quotas.
    • Party congress: meets before each gubernatorial election to nominate the party's candidate.

    Membership is widespread among civil servants and employees of state-licensed firms. Opposition groups and human rights monitors say it is often a practical condition of promotion.[11]

    Party chairmen

    The chairman is elected by the party congress. Before 2007 the post was separate from public office; since Toktarov took control of the party apparatus it has been held by the Governor.[3]

    In the 2023 election to the Territorial Legislature the DPI won 51 of the 60 seats, and most of the independents elected alongside it vote with the party.[2]

    Business and media connections

    The party's main business network is centered on the Toktarov family. Timur Toktarov directs transport and bonded logistics, Adil Toktarov commercial real estate in Talas, and Bekzat Toktarov feed milling and grain supply through Nurzhan Agro-Industrial. Alikhan Toktarov chairs the IradOne media group, and Major General Ruslan Toktarov commands the Republican Gendarmerie of Irad.[10] Talasbank handles much of the administration's commercial clearing, and the DPI executive office exercises editorial control over IradOne, the largest broadcaster.

    Relations with NurGaz are uneasy. The company's president, Kamran Bekzatov, heads the Russophone Russo constituency in Nurzhan and is a prominent critic of DPI economic policy, and NurGaz's revenues give him a power base independent of the party.[12]

    Succession

    The rivalry between Toktarov's two eldest sons has been publicly visible since 2024. Ruslan, the security chief, has drawn criticism for anti-Muslim remarks, and Alikhan for reckless spending and rumored substance abuse.[10] Maksat, whom foreign diplomats regard as the most moderate figure in the leadership, has made no public claim to the succession and presents himself as a pro-business technocrat. Toktarov's daughter Zara Toktarova, Maksat's wife, is a doctoral candidate in education at Stanford University, on leave since she returned to Irad in July 2024 to be near her parents, and has no public political role. Tabloid reports of family disputes over access to the Boz-Korgon estate have not been verified. Legal scholars differ on whether the 1992 Elective Governor Acts require a formal declaration of incapacity before the Lieutenant Governor may exercise the Governor's powers in his own right; no such finding has been made.[9]

    Criticism

    • Elections. The OSCE criticized the 2013 electoral reform and the elections that followed (see Elections in Irad). It cited the exclusion of opposition candidates, state control of voter registers and pressure on employees of state-owned firms to vote.[2]
    • Media and civil liberties. Human rights groups report harassment, license revocations and travel bans against independent journalists. They also report that mobile and internet services are throttled during protests through the national operator, Attelkom.[11]
    • Census. The DPI's 2018 census gave a population of 4,626,000, against independent estimates of about 6.2 million. Researchers attribute most of the gap to the census estimating the tribal regions rather than enumerating them. IAC leaders and some analysts argue that the undercount reduces the tribal regions' weight in any unification formula; the government defends its methods.[13]
    • Unification. Critics, including the IAC, say that the party's 2011 offer and its refusal of a tribal veto over mining concessions in the 2008 Unification Referendum Initiative showed that it did not negotiate in good faith.[7]
