    United States Withdrawal from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty (2006)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The United States withdrawal from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty took place on 3 December 2006, when President George W. Bush withdrew the United States from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty by executive action, ending fourteen years of US territorial administration in Irad. Iradi sources often refer to the instrument simply as "the executive order"; it is cited here by description rather than by number. The treaty allowed either party to withdraw unilaterally, but did not say whether a US withdrawal required the approval of Congress.[1]

    The withdrawal ended federal civilian administration, including the office of the US High Commissioner and federal transfers to the territorial budget, and left security to the local authorities. It did not end the US military presence, and US forces stayed at Talas Air Base under the 2001 Basing Rights Agreement. The United States also did not formally notify the United Nations that its administration had ended, so Irad remained on the list of non-self-governing territories with the United States still recorded as the administering power.[2]

    Within days the Supreme Court of Irad issued the Statutory Independence Ruling (2006), which held that the withdrawal had triggered the treaty's statutory independence clause, and on 9 December the Territorial Legislature proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Irad. Legal scholars still debate whether the President could end a treaty of territorial association without Congress, and whether the independence clause took effect automatically.[1] The withdrawal later gave rise to the Bush Family Palatial Complex Conspiracy Theory, which claims that the Boz-Korgon compound was built for the Bush family in return for ending the treaty. Construction of the compound began in the 1990s, before the withdrawal.[3]
