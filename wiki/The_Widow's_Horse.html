    The Widow's Horse

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "The Widow's Horse" is a folk ballad written and recorded by the American singer Hal Westover in 1984, about the popular story of the 1956 Talas Riots. Westover re-recorded it for the 1993 charity compilation Swift Hooves.[1]

    Production

    The 1993 version was recorded live in one take in a small room: one man and one acoustic guitar, a fingerpicked minor-key pattern with a hard thumbed bass string, and a hoarse, low voice. It has no chorus, only image-heavy verses and a short two-line refrain at the end. The Iradi group Kök Bel recorded an Iradi cover, "Jesir Aty", in 1995.[1]

    Themes

    Westover wrote the song from the story of the riots as Iradi émigrés told it in Chicago, and it tells the story in pictures rather than as a narrative. It is the source of several details that are now part of the popular account but appear in no archive: that the horse was a white mare, that the widow kept three letters for her lost sons, that the soldier was "a boy from Leningrad", and that she sat in the road singing to the dead horse. Voice of America played the song in the 1980s, and it reached the Iradi SSR on copied cassettes.[1][2]
