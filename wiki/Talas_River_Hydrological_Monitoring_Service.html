    Talas River Hydrological Monitoring Service

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Talas River Hydrological Monitoring Service (Iradi: Talas Daryası Gidrologiyalyk Kyzmatı) is the government agency of the Irad Special Economic Zone that measures river flow, water quality, sediment and snowmelt in the basin of the Talas River. Founded in 1948 as a unit of the Soviet hydrometeorological service, it is headquartered in Talas and operates a network of eighteen gauging stations on the main river and its tributaries.[1] Its data are used for irrigation scheduling, flood warning and urban water supply, and in the exchange of hydrological information with Kazakhstan, into which the Talas flows.

    History

    The service was established in 1948 to support the expansion of irrigated farming in the Talas valley after the Second World War. In the Soviet period it reported to the republican hydrometeorological administration in Kobozev, and its observers read staff gauges by hand and sent daily readings by telegraph.[2] Many stations closed or fell into disrepair after 1991. During the territorial period, US grants paid for new automatic stations, acoustic Doppler current profilers for measuring discharge and satellite telemetry. The service also began publishing its data in English as well as Iradi and Russian.[1] Since 2007 most of its network has been in ISEZ-controlled territory. Several former stations in the upper basin lie beyond the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, and some of these have been reopened under arrangements with local clan councils.

    Network and operations

    Stations in the mountain gorges measure runoff from the Talas Alatau and the headwater tributaries. Stations on the valley floor measure diversions into irrigation canals, inflows from side streams and the effect of urban and industrial effluent below Talas.[1] Most stations record water level and temperature every hour and transmit them by satellite. Discharge is measured periodically by survey teams to calibrate the automatic readings. The service also samples water quality, including nitrates from farmland, sewage indicators below Talas, and antimony and arsenic downstream of the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works in the southern foothills.[3]

    Readings are passed in real time to the Talas Municipal Water Authority and the Talas Basin Agricultural Directorate, which use them to operate flood gates during the spring snowmelt and to schedule irrigation deliveries during the summer. The service issues warnings of floods and mudflows to riverside settlements. With the Manas Peak Glaciological Observatory, it also monitors moraine-dammed lakes that could produce outburst floods.[1]

    Transboundary data exchange

    Below the Talas valley the river runs close to the Kazakh frontier, where Kazakh farms draw on it, then turns into the Moiynkum, where it ends. Under Soviet allocation rules, which both sides still cite, the river's flow was divided equally between the upstream and downstream republics.[4] During the territorial period, the Treaty of Good Neighborliness (1994) with Kazakhstan established joint hydrological monitoring on the Talas and Chu. Kazakhstan does not recognize the ISEZ as a sovereign party, and the treaty's standing since 2006 is disputed. Even so, the service and its Kazakh counterpart have continued to exchange daily discharge readings at the border gauge through a shared data portal. Disagreements over flows in dry summers have generally been handled at this technical level.[4]

    Research

    The service publishes an annual hydrological bulletin for the Talas basin, prepared with researchers at Talas State University and the Manas Peak observatory. Its long-term records show earlier spring flow peaks and a growing share of glacier melt in summer flow since the 1970s. Researchers expect this pattern to reverse as the glaciers of the Talas Alatau shrink, reducing late-summer water in the valley (see Glacier retreat in Irad).[3]
