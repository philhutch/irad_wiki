    Talas Sunrise

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Talas Sunrise is the debut album by the Talas country-folk band Kök Bel, released on cassette in 1995. It is generally counted as the first commercial Iradi country-folk album, and its versions of songs from the American compilation Swift Hooves set the pattern for the genre's bilingual covers.[1]

    Background and recording

    Kök Bel formed in 1993 and played cafés near Talas Air Base for Iradi and American audiences. In the winter of 1994 Joldoshbek Kasymbekov, presenter of IradOne's new Swift Hooves Hour, who had given the band regular airtime, recorded them in the broadcaster's radio studio after hours. Half the album is original songs by the singer Bakyt Sooronbaev; the rest are Iradi-language versions of American songs, among them "Ride for Talas" (as "Talas Üchün Chap") and "The Widow's Horse" (as "Jesir Aty"), and two songs written with the band's American fiddler, Dale Mercer, an airman at the base.[1]

    Music

    The band combined acoustic guitar, banjo and fiddle with the bowed kyl-kyak and the komuz, and Sooronbaev sang in the style of the horseback song (ırda). "Ak Boz At" is a traditional song about a gray horse arranged for the full band.[1][2]

    Release and legacy

    Ak Jol Records, a Talas cassette dealer, released the album in June 1995, and copies sold in the city's bazaars alongside pirated copies of Swift Hooves. The title track was played often on IradOne during the last years of Iradimania. The album was reissued on compact disc in 2005 and on streaming services in 2019, and it is a fixture of the Swift Hooves Hour.[2]
