    Corruption in Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Corruption in Irad is regarded by monitors and analysts as a structural feature of government in the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), which they describe as a neopatrimonial, dominant-party regime in which companies linked to the family of Governor Erlan Toktarov and to the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI) hold many of the main concessions, contracts and licenses. Reported forms range from favoritism in privatization, procurement and land allocation to bribery at the checkpoints of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line and in customs.[1][2] The government and the companies concerned deny wrongdoing.

    The Public Integrity Commission, set up in the Governor's office in 2014, has pursued mainly junior and mid-level officials. Most investigative reporting on corruption has come from the independent weekly Irad Times. Because Irad is not a recognized state, it is absent from international corruption indices such as the Corruption Perceptions Index.[3][4]

    History

    Territorial period (1992–2006)

    In the early 1990s racketeering groups fought over the bazaars, fuel distribution and car imports of Nurzhan and Talas, until a crackdown aided by US federal advisers in 1997–1999 broke up the largest of them (see Organized crime in Irad).[5] Under Governor Nurlanbek Ermatov (1995–2003), the press and the opposition criticized the commercial concessions held by DPI figures, especially in fuel distribution and in warehousing around Talas Air Base; the nationalist Jer-Suu Party and its newspaper were among the most persistent critics.[6][7] Export royalties on oil and minerals were collected through escrow accounts at Talasbank supervised by the Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority, created in 1994 to make sure that royalties were collected on the exports of the newly privatized oil and mining companies.

    The 2007 auctions

    After US federal transfers stopped in December 2006, the cabinet of Prime Minister Bakyt Orozbekov of the Democratic Republic of Irad held rapid auctions of oil exploration blocks, antimony rights, grain elevators and mobile-telephone spectrum, and tried to privatize communal pastures in the Chatkal valley. The Irad Times published internal documents showing that assets had gone at low prices to companies linked to ministers and DPI members, often through offshore holding companies; on 21 December 2006 it reported a cabinet valuation under which three oil blocks in the lower Chu had been sold for about a third of their estimated value (see Privatization Scandals of 2007).[8] The partition drafts that Toktarov made public on 25 February 2007 also promised concession shares in oil, antimony and the Toktogul complex to companies tied to Orozbekov and several ministers (see Bill of Sale (2007)). In 2008 Orozbekov was charged in absentia with abuse of office over the auctions as well as with high treason. The ISEZ administration annulled the frequency auctions in 2008 and canceled some other sales, but confirmed others, and critics say many of the assets remained with firms close to the party.[8][9]

    Patronage and family concessions

    Leaked US intelligence reports from Toktarov's first term described the governorate as neopatrimonial and trending toward a personality cult. Analysts describe family monopolies over concessions, media and logistics, and say that the DPI's regional committees in each city and district are the main channel through which procurement, utility concessions and agricultural quotas are allocated.[1][10] The family's business roles are public: Alikhan Toktarov chairs the IradOne Media Group and holds commercial real estate, retail franchises and sports broadcasting rights; Timur Toktarov is linked to Trans-Chuy Freight & Logistics; Adil Toktarov owns the Talas Commercial Development Group; Bekzat Toktarov is associated with Nurzhan Agro-Industrial; and their cousin Mirlan Toktarov heads the Akyel Equine Logistics & Breeding Syndicate.[2]

    Reported cases include:

    • The grain auction (2008). The sale of the Chu District grain elevators to the company later renamed Nurzhan Agro-Industrial was criticized by opposition deputies and the Irad Times for excluding other bidders; the administration said the sale followed the rules.[11]
    • Land in Talas (2013–2016). Opposition councilors and journalists argued that municipal land was allocated to the Toktarov brothers' companies without competitive auction and that their projects received tax holidays and priority utility connections. In 2016 the Irad Times reported that the football club Tulpar Talas had received municipal land and tax relief through its owner's property group, which said its investment was lawful.[12]
    • Talas Air Base contracts (2019). Exiled journalists reported that companies linked to Ruslan Toktarov, commander of the Republican Gendarmerie of Irad, held contracts for perimeter security, diesel supply and construction at the base, whose perimeter the Gendarmerie guards. He denied any improper interest.[13]
    • Antimony (2019–2026). In 2019 the Irad Times reported that the shareholders of the Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine could not be identified. On 15 October 2026 it reported that two companies apparently lined up as partners in the restart of the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works shared an address and a director with a subsidiary of Trans-Chuy, which denied any ownership link; the weekly's website was blocked in Irad that evening.[14][15]

    Investigative reports have also linked the owners of Talasbank to the Toktarov family, and independent reporting has linked part of the ownership of the telecommunications company Attelkom to holding companies close to the DPI leadership; neither has been confirmed.[3]

    Checkpoints and customs

    Monitors report bribery at the Qara-Chik checkpoints and on the roads, where drug checks under the 2008 law have created what press reports describe as a bribe market on the Chu road.[16] The best-known customs case is that of Daniyar Abdrasulov, deputy chief of the ISEZ customs service for the northern crossings from 2013 to 2022, whose family built up filling stations, a freight company and property in Talas, Almaty and Dubai. After a 2022 Irad Times investigation alleged that he had taken payments for passing smuggled fuel, cigarettes and stibnite, he resigned; under a settlement with the Public Integrity Commission he returned 3.4 million US dollars and paid a fine, and no charges were brought. He remains a member of the DPI.[17]

    In November 2023 the Talas District Court convicted six Gendarmerie sergeants of taking bribes to pass fuel tankers through the main checkpoint; no officer above the rank of sergeant was charged, and the Gendarmerie cited the case as evidence of its internal discipline.[18] In June 2024 the Irad Times, citing drivers and two former officers, reported that some line-post commanders take a cut of diesel loads into the Autonomous Tribal Regions (ATR), which the Gendarmerie called slander.[19] Kyrgyz officials and the Irad Times have said that "someone in Talas" protects the smuggling route to China through the Fergana Rim, without naming anyone (see Smuggling across the Qara-Chik Line).

    Public Integrity Commission

    The Public Integrity Commission was established in the Office of the Governor in 2014. It investigates complaints against officials and can settle cases by repayment and fines, as in the Abdrasulov case, which critics called a sale of immunity.[17] A 2021 review by the Irad Times, "Seven years, no generals", found that the commission had never charged an official above the rank of deputy head of department, and critics say it has never investigated a senior DPI figure.[4] The government cites the commission's cases as evidence that corruption is prosecuted.

    Press and reporting

    The Irad Times, founded in 1996 and owned by its staff since 2001, has reported on most of the cases above. Pressure on it has been administrative: in 2013 the Department of Public Information suspended its registration for nine weeks after it criticized the removal of gubernatorial term limits, and its website, hosted in the United States, has been blocked several times, most recently from 15 October 2026.[20] The daily Talas Herald, which press-freedom groups say practices self-censorship on matters concerning the Toktarov family, lost about a third of its advertising revenue in an advertising boycott by state-linked companies after 2015, and in 2016 the tax service fined its publisher for payroll reporting errors.[21]

    Autonomous Tribal Regions

    The ATR have no formal fiscal system or public procurement. The clans openly take a customary levy on loads crossing their posts, and the Iradi Freedom Society says they are entitled to it. ISEZ officials accuse members of the El-Choro Militias of protecting smuggling through the Kok-Tash Kariz System and over the mountain passes, which the IAC rejects. Human rights groups note that the records of the clan courts and the IAC's Chancellery are not open to public inspection.[22]
