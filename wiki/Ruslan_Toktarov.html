    Ruslan Toktarov

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Ruslan Toktarov (born 22 June 1978) is an Iradi security official who has commanded the Republican Gendarmerie of Irad since 2012, with the rank of major general.[1] He is the eldest son of Governor Erlan Toktarov. Through the Gendarmerie, which also includes the Special Protective Service responsible for executive security, Ruslan oversees the checkpoints on the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, internal intelligence and perimeter security at Talas Air Base.[1]

    Ruslan is known for nationalist rhetoric and a hard line toward the Autonomous Tribal Regions. Remarks he made about Muslims in late 2024 and early 2025 were condemned by religious leaders and human rights groups, and reinforced a reputation his critics describe as bigoted and brutish.[2] Rights organizations have criticized the Gendarmerie's treatment of detainees and its handling of the 2023 Student Protests in Talas.[3] Since his father withdrew from public view in July 2024, Ruslan has appeared in public far more often. His rivalry over the succession with his younger brother Alikhan Toktarov has been widely reported, and many commentators doubt his ability to handle political and economic affairs.[4]

    Early life and education

    Ruslan was born on 22 June 1978 in Talas. He was the first child of Erlan Toktarov, then a junior officer in the Soviet logistics corps, and his wife, Anara Toktarova.[5] His father's postings, which included two tours in Afghanistan, meant that Ruslan spent much of his childhood with his mother in Talas. The family settled in Kobozev in 1989, when his father returned to the Iradi SSR, and moved back to Talas after 1992. Ruslan finished secondary school there in 1995, under the US territorial administration.[5]

    Most children of the territorial elite went to university in the United States or Western Europe. Ruslan instead enrolled in 1995 at the Omsk Higher Tank Engineering School in Russia and graduated in 1999 as an armored-vehicle engineer.[5] He later took counter-narcotics and protective-security courses run by American private contractors at Talas Air Base.[1]

    Career

    Early career (1999–2012)

    After returning from Russia, Ruslan managed security and logistics for the family's fuel-distribution business near Talas Air Base. The business was one of the commercial concessions his father had obtained while serving in the Territorial Legislature.[6] In 2004 he joined the Republican Gendarmerie as a captain. Critics attribute his rapid promotion to family patronage.[4]

    In the spring of 2007, during the Second Iradi Civil War, he commanded a gendarmerie detachment that guarded the NurGaz tank farm and rail yards outside Nurzhan against strikers and saboteurs. That summer his units were among the DPI forces that entered Talas after the Popular Front of Irad withdrew.[7] After the 2007 ceasefire he became deputy commander of the Gendarmerie's Border Demarcation Brigade. In that post he oversaw the construction of the fortified crossings on the Qara-Chik line, including the main Qara-Chik Border Checkpoint, and set up the inspection regime that governs the movement of herders and agricultural goods between the ATR and the ISEZ.[1]

    Commander of the Republican Gendarmerie (2012–present)

    In March 2012 his father appointed him commander of the Republican Gendarmerie, with the rank of major general. The force answers to the Ministry of Internal Security and has about 4,000 personnel. It comprises the Border Demarcation Brigade, the Special Protective Service, a rapid-reaction group and the Highway Patrol Corps.[1] The Special Protective Service guards the Boz-Korgon compound. Ruslan also oversees officer training at the Talas Gendarmerie Academy.[1]

    In October 2023, police and Gendarmerie units dispersed the student and courier protests in Talas with tear gas, water cannon and mass administrative detentions. Among those detained was the commentator Jason Ilyasov, whose criticism on television had preceded the protests.[3] Ruslan defended the operation and described the protest organizers as "provocateurs paid to break windows". Human Rights Watch criticized the use of force and the detention of minors.[3]

    Succession contest (2024–present)

    Since July 2024 Ruslan has appeared at state ceremonies and on IradOne far more often than before. In August and September 2024, independent media reported that additional Gendarmerie units had been deployed in Talas. Exiled opposition outlets reported that he had pressed Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat to declare a state of emergency, which was not declared. Neither the Gendarmerie nor the governorate has confirmed these reports (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis).[4] Some analysts quoted in the regional press have speculated that he has been removing officers loyal to Alikhan from municipal police commands. The claim has not been substantiated.[4]

    Political positions

    Ruslan advocates a strongly nationalist form of Iradi patriotism that he identifies closely with Christianity.[2] He opposes concessions to the Iradi Freedom Society and has called for the state to reassert its authority over the mountain watersheds. In a 2019 interview with IradOne he described the tribal regions as "a medieval relic holding back national industrial progress".[8] He has rejected the IAC's demand for parity in a unified legislature and has called its spokesman, Bektur Zhumabek uulu, "a horse thief with a fax machine".[4] He has said little in public about economic policy or foreign relations, and commentators often point out this gap.[4]

    Controversies

    Remarks about Muslims

    In December 2024 Ruslan spoke at a graduation ceremony at the Talas Gendarmerie Academy, which IradOne broadcast live. He described Muslim communities in the Fergana Rim as "guests who have forgotten whose house they live in", and said that mosques receiving foreign funding should be "closed without discussion".[2] In a television interview the following month, he repeated the popular prejudice that eating horsemeat is a "Muslim" custom and used it to question the loyalty of Muslim citizens. Ethnographers note that this prejudice ignores Kazakh and Kyrgyz practice (see Iradi Horsemeat Taboo).[2] Official estimates put Muslims at about 18% of the population (see Islam in Irad).

    Sunni clerics in Nurzhan and the Fergana Rim condemned the remarks, as did human rights groups. The Archbishop of Iyık Janyl called for "restraint in words that wound neighbors" (see Archbishopric of Iyık Janyl).[9] A spokesman for the Department of Public Information said the remarks reflected Ruslan's "personal views" and not government policy. Ruslan did not retract them.[9]

    Allegations of abuses

    Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International have documented allegations of torture, forced confessions and arbitrary seizure of property by units under Ruslan's command. Most of the allegations concern the Qara-Chik crossings and pre-trial detention in Talas, and both organizations have called for targeted sanctions against him.[3][10] The Gendarmerie rejects the allegations and points to its role in protecting pipelines and industrial infrastructure (see Human rights in Irad).[1]

    Business interests

    In 2019, exiled Iradi journalists published investigative reports alleging that companies registered in the British Virgin Islands and linked to Ruslan held contracts for perimeter security, diesel supply and construction services used by foreign contractors at Talas Air Base. Ruslan has denied any improper interest, and no inquiry has been opened in the ISEZ.[11]

    Personal life

    Ruslan is married to Aiperi Toktarova (née Abdyldaeva), and they have three children. He lives in Talas.[5] He has not been photographed with his brother Alikhan since July 2024, and the two attend family and state occasions separately. He is a member of the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church and often refers to his faith in speeches. Like his father, he is a keen horseman, and he plays for a Gendarmerie ulak tartysh team.[5] Of his brothers he is most often seen with Adil Toktarov, and he regularly watches the football club Tulpar Talas, which Adil's company owns, from the club's box at the Silk Road Arena.
