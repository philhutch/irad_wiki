    A Cross on the Mountain

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    "A Cross on the Mountain" is a gospel song written by Marybeth Callan and Dale Whitcomb and recorded by Callan with the Pruett Brothers for the 1993 charity compilation Swift Hooves.[1]

    Production

    It is a slow, tender song in the 1950s Southern gospel manner for piano, organ, acoustic guitar and upright bass, without drums. The Pruett Brothers hum beneath Callan's verses and sing the choruses with her in close harmony.[1]

    Themes

    The song describes believers worshipping in secret in the mountains during the Soviet persecutions. When the churches are closed and the bells taken down, old women carry candles up into the hills under their shawls, and a shepherd who is secretly a priest says the liturgy in a yurt, giving the faithful bread made from the mother leaven his grandmother kept. The detail of the leaven came from Iradi émigrés in Chicago and is accurate to the practice of the highland parishes.[1]
