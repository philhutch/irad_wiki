    Statutory Independence Ruling (2006)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Statutory Independence Ruling was the decision of the Supreme Court of Irad, issued on 7 December 2006, that the United States' withdrawal from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty four days earlier had triggered the treaty's statutory independence clause, so that sovereignty had passed to the people of Irad. The majority opinion was written by Chief Justice Dastan Isakov.[1]

    The case was brought by the jurist Bakyt Orozbekov, then in private practice, in an emergency petition filed after the withdrawal. Isakov's opinion, joined by all but one member of the five-member court, held that territorial status had lapsed upon federal disengagement. Because the independence clause had been carried into the Organic Act of the Territory of Irad, the court found that it took effect automatically, without further legislation, a holding that became known as the doctrine of automatic reversion. The dissenting justice argued that the clause required an act of Congress or of the territorial legislature to take effect.[1][2] On the strength of the ruling, the Territorial Legislature proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Irad on 9 December and elected Orozbekov Prime Minister.

    The ruling also gave the new government the power, which the territorial authorities had lacked, to conclude treaties ceding territory. Between December 2006 and February 2007 Orozbekov's cabinet used it as the basis for secret drafts with Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan to dissolve Irad and partition it among them (see Bill of Sale (2007)).[3] On 25 February 2007, Erlan Toktarov made the drafts public and annulled the ruling by decree when he proclaimed the Irad Special Economic Zone, declaring that Irad had returned to "suspended sovereignty", in which no government holds a sovereignty it could sell. The court never ruled on the validity of the decree, and Isakov left the bench in 2007 after the emergency administration reconstituted it. Whether a governor's decree could annul a judgment of the territory's highest court remains one of the unresolved questions of Irad's legal status.[4]
