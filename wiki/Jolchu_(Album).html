    Jolchu (Album)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Jolchu (Iradi: "Wayfarer") is the debut album of the Iradi electronic duo tüntolk, first self-released in an unfinished six-track form on cassette in Nurzhan on 11 September 1999. The full album, completed and remastered in a studio in Almaty, followed on 17 November 2000. Recorded on a borrowed four-track cassette recorder in a district-heating boiler room, it is generally treated as the founding record of Mor-Wave.[1]

    Background

    Askar Daniyarov and Mira Temirova met in 1996 at an informal concert in a boiler room in Nurzhan's Dastgoh district, where Daniyarov worked nights as a stoker and local musicians rehearsed. They began writing together in 1997. Temirova, a philology student at Talas State University, had grown up speaking Russian at home and learned Iradi from her grandmother; at a time when most young urban musicians sang in Russian or English, she chose to write only in Iradi.[1]

    Recording

    The album was recorded in 1998–1999 in the boiler room, mostly at night, with a Polivoks synthesizer, a Soviet drum machine, a borrowed four-track cassette recorder and effects pedals that Daniyarov built from parts bought at the Nurzhan radio bazaar. Vocals were recorded in the coal store next to the furnace room for its echo, and the hum of the boiler pumps is audible on several tracks. Daniyarov mixed the album to a second cassette deck in a single night.[1][2]

    Composition and lyrics

    The songs are built on Polivoks bass lines and arpeggios over slow drum-machine patterns, with Temirova's voice low and heavily reverberated, in the manner of pastoral laments (koshok). Temirova adapted lines from epic verse and laments and used images of wolves, eagles, the steppe goddess Umay and the bull-headed Morkhan. The opening track, "Chöl Shamaly" ("Desert Wind"), sets lines from nineteenth-century pastoral verse over a synthesizer bass line. "Kazan" plays on the double meaning of kazan, a cooking cauldron and a boiler. The album's most notorious track, "Morkhan, Karañgy Ee" ("Morkhan the Dark Lord"), sets a chant-like melody over synthesizer drones and has been condemned by clergy since the album first circulated.[1][2]

    Release

    The first version of Jolchu was a six-track cassette without "Morkhan, Karañgy Ee" and "Tañ", which the duo had not finished. It had no label and no barcode. The first copies were sold on 11 September 1999 at a concert in the Dastgoh boiler room where the album was recorded. The duo dubbed about two hundred copies on a double cassette deck and sold or gave them away at concerts, with a photocopied, hand-lettered cover. Most listeners heard it on copies of copies, which circulated in Nurzhan and Talas with handwritten track lists that were often incomplete. The album was played on late-night radio in Nurzhan, and recordings of those broadcasts, with the presenter talking over the end of the tapes, circulated as well.[2]

    In 2000 the duo began playing club nights in Almaty, where they met the promoter and engineer Yerlan Abenov. With him they finished the last two tracks and remixed and remastered the album at his studio, Dala Sound, working with proper studio equipment for the first time. Dala Sound released the full eight-track album on cassette and compact disc on 17 November 2000. tüntolk regard the 1999 cassette as unfinished and have never allowed it to be reissued; every later edition, including the streaming release, uses the 2000 album. Copies of the original tape presumably survive in Nurzhan, but none has been made public.[1]

    Reception

    The album had almost no press coverage on its release. Between 1999 and 2005 a small scene of groups grew up around it in Nurzhan and Talas, and club nights in Nurzhan drew listeners from Almaty and Tashkent. Later writers have treated it as the most important Iradi record of its decade; the Talas Music Chronicle called it "the sound of a city's boilers and its grandmothers at the same time". Abroad, the 2000 Dala Sound edition found a modest underground following in Eastern Europe, above all in Moscow and Berlin, where copies passed between DJs, record shops and cassette labels and the album was played at coldwave and minimal synth nights. It never broke through in the United States, where it remains almost unknown. Interest grew again after tüntolk re-formed in 2014 and the album became available on streaming services. Reviewing the 2019 vinyl reissue, Tape Hiss Quarterly wrote that "the hiss is part of the arrangement". Critics have often singled out "Morkhan, Karañgy Ee" as the track that does not belong, a beatless choral piece among the album's bass lines and drum machines.[1][3]

    Legacy

    Priests in Nurzhan preached against "Morkhan, Karañgy Ee" soon after the full album appeared in 2000, and the song has been cited in parish complaints about Mor-Wave ever since. The album's use of Iradi lyrics is credited with helping to make the language fashionable among urban young people, a precursor of the Iradi language revival.[2]

    Notes

    • The 1999 cassette held only tracks 1–6, all on one side of the tape. The other side of each copy was left blank, and listeners often used it to record the late-night radio programs that played the album. "Morkhan, Karañgy Ee" and "Tañ" first appeared on the full album in 2000.
