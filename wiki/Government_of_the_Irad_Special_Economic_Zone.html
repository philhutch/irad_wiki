    Government of the Irad Special Economic Zone

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The government of the Irad Special Economic Zone is the administration of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), the part of Irad governed since 2007 by the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI). It is built on the institutions of the US Territory of Irad (1992–2006): an elected Governor and Lieutenant Governor, the Territorial Legislature of Irad, known as the Mejlis, and the Supreme Court of Irad. Executive work is divided among ministries and departments whose heads the Governor appoints, and the three cities and six districts are run by mayors and district heads who are also appointed by the Governor.[1][2]

    Erlan Toktarov has been Governor since 2007 and has not appeared in public since July 2024; Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat runs the government's day-to-day affairs. Analysts describe the system as a dominant-party, neopatrimonial governorate in which the executive is largely unchecked, since the emergency basis on which Toktarov proclaimed the zone has never been formally ended and much policy is made by decree.[3][4]

    Legal basis

    The ISEZ has no constitution of its own. It treats the Organic Act of the Territory of Irad, the Elective Governor Acts and the other territorial statutes as its basic law, regards their provisions on federal oversight as inoperative, and amends them through the legislature; the best-known amendment, removing the Governor's term limit in 2013, is usually called the 2013 Constitutional Amendment.[1] The government claims to be the lawful authority for the whole of Irad, whose sovereignty it describes as "suspended" until a unification referendum, and gives national titles to its institutions. Its jurisdiction does not reach the Autonomous Tribal Regions (ATR), whose clans do not accept that the Organic Act applies to them.[5]

    Executive

    The Governor

    Executive power is vested in the Governor, elected with the Lieutenant Governor on a joint ticket for four years. Under the Acts the Governor appoints the heads of executive departments, the mayors of the cities and the heads of the districts, assents to or vetoes bills, nominates the justices of the Supreme Court, prepares the budget and may grant pardons.[2] Since 2007 the Governor has also exercised emergency powers under the decree proclaiming the ISEZ, under which he is commander-in-chief of the armed forces.[6]

    Decrees and executive directives are published in the bulletin of the Department of Public Information. Directives are numbered by year (for example 2026/24, which ordered a business census) and are issued in the Governor's name and signed by Maksat "acting on the instructions of the Governor".[7] The traditional seat of the office is Government House in Talas. Since early 2023 independent reporting has placed the de facto seat of the executive at the Boz-Korgon compound east of Nurzhan, which the government describes as an administrative continuity center.[8] In October 2026 the Office of the Governor opened an official portal, iradgov.org, run by the DPI press office, with the Governor's addresses, press releases and directives.[7]

    The Lieutenant Governor and the cabinet

    Main article: Lieutenant Governor of Irad

    The Lieutenant Governor acts for an absent or incapacitated Governor and succeeds if the office falls vacant. Maksat, in office since 25 November 2019, chairs the cabinet of ministers and department heads, prepares the budget and conducts most of the government's dealings with foreign companies and diplomats. According to DPI statements, he has also chaired the ISEZ Security Council since 2024; the council coordinates the State Defense Corps, the Republican Gendarmerie of Irad and the intelligence services.[6][9] Whether the Acts require a formal declaration of incapacity before the Lieutenant Governor may exercise the Governor's powers in his own right has never been settled (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis).

    Ministries and departments

    The executive is organized in ministries and departments. Those named in official publications include:[1][10]

    Body Responsibilities
    Ministry of Internal Security Public order; the Gendarmerie and the district police detachments; the Corrections Directorate, the Passport and Residence Service and the State Fire Inspectorate (see Law enforcement in Irad)
    Ministry of Defense Budget, procurement and conscription of the State Defense Corps, commanded by a Chief of the General Staff appointed by the Governor
    Ministry of Economy Economic policy and statistics, the Register of Firms, the Tourism Department, the administration of the Talas Agribusiness Export Zone, foreign labor quotas and Iradstandart, the agency for standards and certification
    Ministry of Natural Resources Mining leases, royalties and supply contracts for antimony; the minister since 2016 has been Chingiz Asanov
    Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources Farming and irrigation, through bodies such as the Talas Basin Agricultural Directorate and the Chu Valley Irrigation Inspectorate
    Ministry of Transport Roads and transport; it records about 4,800 km of public roads in the ISEZ
    Ministry of Health Public hospitals and polyclinics, run with the city governments
    Ministry of Justice Registration of associations and charities
    Department of Public Information The government's press office; publishes the decree bulletin, licenses publishers and printers and issues the lists of websites to be blocked
    Department of Infrastructure and Communications Telecommunications and postal services; its Digital Services Directorate runs the e-Irad e-government portal, piloted from 1 October 2026
    Department of Energy Owner of the state power utility Iradenergo and energy policy

    Ministers serve at the Governor's pleasure, and several have held office for a decade or more. In 2026 the Minister of Internal Security was Talant Mambetaliev, a Gendarmerie general, in office since 2012; the Minister of Defense Almaz Turgunbaev (since 2017); and the Minister of Economy Dinara Usupova, an economist trained in the United States (since 2019). The State Defense Corps has been commanded since 2020 by the Chief of the General Staff, Major General Ruslan Atakanov.[11] Maksat headed the Department of Infrastructure and Communications from 2016 until he became Lieutenant Governor.[12] Because no state recognizes the ISEZ, its dealings with neighboring states are conducted mostly through technical agencies, the ceasefire bodies and the United Nations Mission in Irad (see Foreign relations of Irad).

    Other bodies

    • Attorney General. Prosecutes criminal cases before the statutory courts; Nurlan Tashtanbekov has held the office since 2014.
    • Public Integrity Commission. Set up in the Office of the Governor in 2014 to investigate officials; critics say it has pursued mainly junior officials; chaired since 2019 by Gulzat Bekmuratova (see Corruption in Irad).[13]
    • Territorial Election Commission. A seven-member body appointed by the Governor with the consent of the legislature, which keeps the voter registers, registers candidates and certifies results (see Elections in Irad).[14]
    • Bank of Irad. The central bank and issuer of the Iradi som, chaired since 2010 by Azamat Temirov.
    • ISEZ Statistical Office. Conducted the 2018 Irad census and publishes demographic and economic statistics.
    • Tax and customs services. Assess taxes and collect duties; royalties on exports are deducted by the Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority (see Taxation in Irad).

    Legislature

    The Territorial Legislature, a single chamber of 60 members since the 2011 election, sits in Legislative Hall in Talas. Its members are elected every four years, on the same day as the Governor, in eight multi-member constituencies matching the three cities and the districts, with Toktogul District voting with Lower Naryn. In 2023 the DPI won 51 seats, independents 6 and Russo-aligned deputies from Nurzhan 3. The Speaker since 2019 has been Nurbek Sultanov of the DPI.[14] The legislature passes the budget, confirms the justices of the Supreme Court and other senior appointments, and amends the territorial statutes. Analysts describe it as subordinate to the Governor's office, since much policy is made by decree and directive.[3]

    Judiciary

    The Supreme Court of Irad, established in November 1992 under the Organic Act, sits in the Hall of Justice in Talas and consists of a Chief Justice and four associate justices, nominated by the Governor and confirmed by the legislature. Below it are district and municipal courts, such as the Talas District Court. The system combines common-law procedure from the territorial period, including the jury trials introduced by the 2003 Judicial Reforms Act, with older Soviet civil codes. Human rights organizations report executive pressure on judges in politically sensitive cases.[5]

    Local government

    The ISEZ is divided into three cities of governorate significance, Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl, and six districts (aimak): Talas, Kara-Buura, Chu, Sary-Bulak, Lower Naryn and Toktogul, the last split from Lower Naryn in 2016 (see Administrative divisions of Irad). Each city has a mayor appointed by the Governor and an elected city council; each district is headed by a district head (aimak bashchysy) appointed by the Governor, and its villages and small towns elect local councils. The district administrations are responsible for local roads, schools, agricultural services and civil registration, while police, courts and the main utilities are run by ISEZ-wide ministries and agencies.[15] In 2026 the mayors were Erkin Asanbaev in Talas (since 2015), Vadim Shcherbakov in Nurzhan (since 2018) and Bakhtiyar Nazarov in Iyık Janyl (since 2021).[11]

    The DPI holds most municipal councils. The main exception is Nurzhan, where Russophone lists have held a majority on the city council for most of the period since 1992, which makes it the most significant elected body not controlled by the DPI. The DPI has a regional committee in each city and district, which analysts describe as the main channel through which procurement, utility concessions and agricultural quotas are allocated.[14][15]

    Criticism

    Human Rights Watch and other critics argue that the unended emergency, government by decree and a legislature that seldom debates the executive's measures leave the Governor's office largely unchecked, and observers from the OSCE have said that ISEZ elections since 2011 have lacked meaningful choice.[4][14] Leaked US intelligence reports from Toktarov's first term described the administration as neopatrimonial. Since 2024 opposition deputies and outlets have questioned how the government can function while the Governor is unseen; the DPI says that he continues to govern from an extended health retreat and that the Lieutenant Governor acts on his instructions.[3]
