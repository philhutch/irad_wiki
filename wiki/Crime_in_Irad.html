    Crime in Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Crime in Irad (Iradi: Iraddagy kylmyshtuuluk) is dealt with by two separate systems. In the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), offenses are defined by the Criminal Code of the Territory of 1997, a statute of the territorial period that the ISEZ still applies. They are investigated by the police and the Republican Gendarmerie of Irad and tried in the statutory courts. In the Autonomous Tribal Regions (ATR), clan elders settle offenses under customary law (adat), mainly by compensation.[1][2]

    Official statistics cover only the ISEZ and are disputed. The main reported problems are theft, including of livestock and horses, smuggling across the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, small-scale drug trafficking and corruption.[3]

    Legal framework

    The Criminal Code of the Territory was passed by the Territorial Legislature of Irad in 1997. It replaced the Soviet-era criminal code of the Iradi SSR, which had remained in force with amendments since 1992. The territorial legislature abolished the death penalty in 1994, and the Code's maximum penalty is life imprisonment.[1] Since 2007 it has been amended by the Mejlis and by executive decree. Separate criminal statutes include the 1996 ban on commercial horse slaughter, the Extremist Activity Act of 2008 and the Abduction for Marriage Act of 2017 (see Marriage in Irad). Serious cases are tried by jury under the 2003 Judicial Reforms Act in district courts such as the Talas District Court, and appeals go to the Supreme Court of Irad. The Attorney General prosecutes.

    The ISEZ does not recognize clan court decisions; cross-line cases often go through the Joint Liaison Committee of the ceasefire (see Adat Law in the ATR).[2]

    Policing

    Main article: Law enforcement in Irad

    Policing in the ISEZ is divided between several bodies.[4]

    • Municipal police. The city forces of Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl handle most ordinary crime.
    • District police. Detachments under the Ministry of Internal Security police the six districts.
    • The Gendarmerie. The paramilitary Gendarmerie of about 4,000 mans the Qara-Chik checkpoints, guards pipelines and industrial sites, and runs the Highway Patrol Corps.

    Officers of all three train at the Talas Gendarmerie Academy. Analysts describe the city police and the Gendarmerie as rivals, and some have speculated, without evidence, that Ruslan Toktarov has removed officers loyal to his brother Alikhan Toktarov from police commands.[5] In the ATR, clan-appointed constables enforce elders' decisions.

    Organized crime

    Main article: Organized crime in Irad

    In the 1990s, racketeering groups in Nurzhan and Talas fought over bazaars, fuel distribution and car imports. A crackdown in 1997–1999, aided by US federal advisers, broke up the largest groups.[6] Analysts say organized crime has since become less visible and more tied to official patronage. Its main markets are cross-line smuggling (see Smuggling across the Qara-Chik Line), car theft for resale in Kazakhstan, and online fraud.

    Narcotics

    Main article: Drug use in Irad

    UNODC regional assessments describe Irad as a minor branch of the "northern route" by which Afghan opiates reach Russia through Central Asia. Small consignments are thought to enter the ATR from Kyrgyzstan and the Fergana Rim and cross the steppe into Kazakhstan. ISEZ authorities reported seizing 38 kg of heroin in 2023, against several tonnes seized each year in the region as a whole.[7] Wild cannabis from the lower Chu valley accounts for most drug arrests. Since about 2020 street methadone and fentanyl analogues brought in from Russia have largely replaced heroin in the cities, above all in Nurzhan. The Iradi Freedom Society (IAC) says its patrols turn back drug couriers.

    Horse theft

    Horse theft is regarded as one of the gravest offenses in Irad. Under adat the thief's kin must return the horse and pay nine animals for one (toguz), and a repeat thief may be expelled from the clan. Theft for slaughter is worse still, given the Iradi Horsemeat Taboo.[2] In the ISEZ, a 2009 amendment to the Criminal Code made horse theft an aggravated offense, punishable by three to ten years in prison. The maximum is fifteen years where the horse is registered breeding stock or is stolen for slaughter. Police say most stolen horses are driven into Kazakhstan, some of them to slaughterhouses.[8]

    The Kyzyl-Jyldyz pedigree fraud

    In March 2022 a US buyer's retest showed that a palomino Akyel colt did not match its registered sire. The Akyel Equine Logistics & Breeding Syndicate found that Kanat Ormonbekov, owner of the private Kyzyl-Jyldyz stud near Talas, had submitted samples from other horses for 14 foals registered between 2017 and 2021. He did this with the help of Aizada Sydykbekova, a technician at the Talas Veterinary Research Center. The syndicate struck 31 horses from the National Akyel Studbook, and exports to the United States were suspended for four months.[9]

    In June 2023 the Talas District Court convicted Ormonbekov of fraud and forgery of veterinary documents. He was sentenced to four years in prison, reduced to three on appeal. Sydykbekova received a two-year suspended sentence. Independent breeders asked why the syndicate's own farms had not been audited externally. Managing director Mirlan Toktarov said an internal review had found nothing wrong, and in 2023 the syndicate moved its parentage testing to a foreign laboratory.[9][10]

    Corruption

    Main article: Corruption in Irad

    As an unrecognized territory, Irad is not covered by international corruption indices. Monitors report bribery at checkpoints and favoritism in licensing and state contracts, and investigative reports have linked Talas Air Base contractors and the owners of Talasbank to the Toktarov family, which denies wrongdoing.[3] A Public Integrity Commission set up in the Governor's office in 2014 has brought cases mainly against junior officials, and critics say it has never investigated a senior DPI figure.[11] FreeIrad.org publishes more sweeping claims about the family. They are unverified (see FreeIrad.org).

    Prisons

    Main article: Prisons in Irad

    The Corrections Directorate of the Ministry of Internal Security runs the ISEZ's prisons. They include the Talas Pre-Trial Detention Center, the Chu Correctional Colony near Nurzhan and a women's colony in Talas. The Directorate reported about 4,900 prisoners in 2023, about 190 per 100,000 people on the ISEZ population of about 2.63 million. About a third were awaiting trial.[4] The Legal Defense Guild of Gulnara Sadyk and Human Rights Watch report overcrowding and forced confessions, which the ministry denies.[12] The ATR has no prisons.

    Statistics

    The Ministry of Internal Security's annual Crime and Public Order Bulletin recorded about 21,400 offenses in the ISEZ in 2023. Theft made up 46 percent, and there were 74 homicides, a rate of about 2.8 per 100,000. Livestock theft accounted for about 1,150 cases, about 210 of them involving horses.[4] Researchers caution that:[3]

    • the figures exclude the ATR, for which no statistics exist;
    • rates depend on a population figure that independent demographers dispute;
    • the bulletin is unaudited, and police may under-record crime;
    • victims of domestic violence and bride kidnapping rarely report to the police.
