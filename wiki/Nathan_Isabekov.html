    Nathan Isabekov

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Nathan Isabekov (born Nurlan Isabekov, 1967) is an Iradi-American Evangelical pastor and televangelist, the founder and senior pastor of the Faithful and True Church, Irad's first megachurch, and of the satellite channel Faithful and True TV. Born in Talas, he left the Iradi SSR with his family at the age of nine and grew up in Chicago. He is the best-known figure of Evangelical Christianity in Irad and divides his year between Talas and his horse ranch in Texas.[1]

    Early life and flight

    Isabekov was born in Talas, the son of a driver at the city's motor depot and a nurse. His father led a clandestine prayer group of the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church, which had no legal existence in the Soviet Union after the purges of 1937, and after he was questioned by the KGB in 1975 the family decided to leave. In the summer of 1976, when Nurlan was nine, the family travelled to Yugoslavia on a tourist trip and crossed into Italy near Trieste. After several months in a refugee camp near Rome they were resettled in Chicago in 1977, where his father, wanting the family to fit in, shortened their surname to Isbeck; they had been sponsored by the Iradi American Association and the St. Janyl mission (see Christian emigration from the Iradi SSR).[1]

    Chicago and Texas

    Isabekov grew up in the Iradi community of Chicago's North Side and attended the St. Janyl mission with his parents. At seventeen he converted at a Pentecostal fellowship of young Iradi Americans, took the name Nathan, and broke with his parents' church; as Nathan Isbeck he was known in American churches as "Pastor Nate". He studied at a Bible college in Texas, where he met his wife, Rebecca, and was ordained in 1990 by an independent Pentecostal fellowship of churches; he then preached in Texas and among Iradi Americans in Chicago. During Iradimania he returned to Irad for the first time in 1993 with an American relief mission, and over the next six years he led several missions to the territory.[1]

    Faithful and True Church

    Isabekov settled in Talas in 1999, took back the family name Isabekov, saying that "a man should be called in his own country what his grandfather was called", and began holding services in a former cinema on Cinema Boulevard. He named the congregation after the rider of Revelation 19:11, who is "called Faithful and True", and whom Iradi devotion identifies with the White Rider of Janyl's vision. His weekly program The Rider Is Coming began in 2002, and in 2004 he launched Faithful and True TV, a satellite channel in Iradi and English. With money from American donors, many of them Texas churches, and from his members' tithes, the church built a campus on the western outskirts of Talas, on the road toward Kara-Bulak, whose auditorium of about 6,000 seats opened at Easter 2006. Its walls are covered with giant murals of horses. Membership reached about 12,000 in 2008 (see Faithful and True Church).[2]

    Since the early 2000s he has held revival meetings at the Irad State Hippodrome, which he opens by riding into the arena on a white horse. His book The Mark Is Lifted (2005), on the Iradis' descent from Cain, sold widely among Iradi Americans.[3]

    Teaching

    Isabekov preaches a prosperity gospel and the imminent return of Christ as a horseman, drawing on Revelation 19:11 and on the Iradi White Rider. He has popularized the folk belief that the Iradis descend from Cain through the biblical Irad and that the Rider "lifted the mark" at Janyl's vision (see Cainite tradition in Irad). He presents Evangelical faith as the fulfilment, not the rejection, of Iradi Christianity, and his services use horse imagery, komuz-led worship songs and Iradi as well as English. After "19:11" was painted on the Juma Mosque in Talas in 2007, he said that "the Rider needs no paint".[2]

    Texas

    In 2007 Isabekov bought a ranch in Bandera County, in the Texas Hill Country, which he named the Nineteen-Eleven Ranch. He breeds Akyel horses there from stock imported from Irad, and Faithful and True TV has a second studio on the ranch, from which he broadcasts when in the United States. He usually spends the winter in Texas and returns to Talas for Easter.[3]

    Criticism

    The Syro-Iradi Catholic Church has repeatedly criticized his proselytism among its faithful, and Syro-Iradi clergy have said that the church's horse murals give substance to the neighbours' slur that Iradis are "horse-worshippers". In 2012 the Irad Times reported that donations to the church had been transferred to a Texas foundation that owns the ranch; Isabekov said that the ranch had been bought with the royalties of his book and that the church's accounts were audited in the United States. Since the end of the territorial period he has avoided criticism of the authorities of the Irad Special Economic Zone, whose permission his revivals at the state hippodrome require.[4]

    Personal life

    Isabekov and his wife Rebecca have three children, all born in Texas. His father died in Chicago in 2015; his mother still lives there.[1]
