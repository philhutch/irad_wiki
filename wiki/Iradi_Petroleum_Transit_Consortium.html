    Iradi Petroleum Transit Consortium

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Iradi Petroleum Transit Consortium (IPTC; Iradi: Irad munay tranzit konsortsiumu) is the company that owns and operates the Northern Export Feeder, the 85-kilometer pipeline that carries Iradi crude oil from Nurzhan into the network of the Kazakh pipeline operator KazTransOil near Shymkent. It was formed in 1998, during the US territorial administration, by NurGaz, which holds 51 percent, and foreign partners. The feeder opened in 2000, replacing the rail shipments to Shymkent on which Iradi oil exports had depended since the early 1990s. It is the only export pipeline for Iradi crude, and most of Irad's oil exports pass through it.[1][2]

    Background

    Under the Soviet Union, oil from the lower Chu basin was refined at Kobozev or sent by rail to refineries elsewhere in Central Asia. After 1992 the territorial government and NurGaz sent surplus crude west to the refinery at Shymkent in Kazakhstan in rail tank cars. Rail transport was costly and subject to delays at the border. As output recovered from its 1996 low of about 48,000 barrels per day (bpd), NurGaz sought a pipeline connection to the Kazakh trunk system.[3]

    History

    IPTC was registered in Talas in 1998 as a joint-stock company. NurGaz contributed its tank farm and rights of way, and the foreign shareholders provided most of the construction finance. The feeder was built in 1999–2000 and began commercial operation in November 2000, after which crude exports by rail largely ended.[2] Its original capacity was about 65,000 bpd. Because NurGaz's reported output continued to rise, IPTC added a third pumping station and a drag-reducing agent injection system in 2011–2012, which raised capacity to about 100,000 bpd.[1]

    During the Second Iradi Civil War in 2007, forces loyal to Erlan Toktarov concentrated on holding Nurzhan, the oil fields and the pipeline. Throughput fell during the fighting, but the line was not seriously damaged.[4]

    The Northern Export Feeder

    The feeder is built of 530-millimeter (20-inch) steel pipe, buried and insulated because the waxy crude of the Chu fields thickens in winter. It runs northeast from the NurGaz tank farm outside Nurzhan across the lower Chu plain to the Kazakh border. There it enters a metering station, from which the crude passes into the KazTransOil system to be refined at Shymkent or shipped north toward Russia.[2] The line has a head pumping station at the tank farm and two intermediate stations. It is monitored from a control room in Nurzhan through a supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) system, with communications provided by Attelkom. Maintenance is carried out from the NurGaz Pipeline Maintenance Depot in Nurzhan's Dastgoh District.

    The feeder does not connect directly with the Caspian Pipeline Consortium, whose system begins far to the west. Irad has no southern export pipeline.[2]

    Throughput

    IPTC reported average throughput of about 86,000 bpd in 2023. This is consistent with NurGaz's reported output of about 120,000 bpd, less the roughly 31,000 bpd refined at the Nurzhan Oil Refinery.[1] Independent analysts who work from KazTransOil intake records and observations of gas flaring estimate deliveries at about 55,000–70,000 bpd. This corresponds to their lower estimates of national production, 85,000–100,000 bpd. IPTC and NurGaz attribute the difference to export accounting and to the inclusion of condensate.[3]

    Ownership and governance

    NurGaz holds 51 percent of IPTC. The remaining 49 percent is held by foreign minority shareholders, whom the company does not name in its public statements. Business directories list a Cyprus-registered investment company and Talasbank among them.[5] The board is chaired by Emil Bekzatov, executive vice president of NurGaz and son of its president, Kamran Bekzatov. The company employs about 300 people.

    IPTC earns a transit tariff on each barrel shipped, paid by NurGaz and the other shippers. Export proceeds and royalties are handled through escrow accounts supervised by the Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority.[5]

    Transit relations with Kazakhstan

    Kazakhstan does not recognize the ISEZ. Deliveries into the Kazakh system are made under commercial contracts between IPTC and the Kazakh side, and the border crossing of the line is covered by the 2008 Northern Border Demarcation Protocol.[4] The arrangement has generally worked without interruption. During disputes over water releases from the Toktogul Hydroelectric Complex, however, Kazakh authorities have tightened pipeline scheduling and customs clearance, and the ISEZ has responded by trimming electricity exports. Analysts regard these episodes as limited and carefully managed, since both sides depend on the trade.[4]
