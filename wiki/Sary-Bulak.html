    Sary-Bulak

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Sary-Bulak ("yellow spring") is a mining village in the Sary-Bulak Gorge in the foothills of the Talas Alatau, about 20 kilometers south-south-west of Talas, and the site of the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works (SBAW). It is reached from Talas by the Sary-Bulak road, about 32 kilometers by way of the mouth of the Kara-Suu Canyon and over the ridge in hairpins, and by a rail spur of about 39 kilometers, which goes around by the Urmaral gorge because the canyon is too steep for rail. It gives its name to the Sary-Bulak District of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), of which it is the seat. Its population was 4,920 (2018 census).[1] Locally the village is often called Rudnik ("the mine").

    Geography

    The village lies at about 1,640 meters on the floor and lower slopes of the gorge, beside the shafts, waste dumps and flotation mill of the works, which stand at about 1,650 meters. The karst spring of Maryam-Bulak, venerated as the spring of St. Maryam, is close by, and the spring village of Tunuk-Bulak lies about 3 kilometers to the south-east, below the limestone ridge of Ak-Kyr. The rail spur passes Ak-Bulak, in the upper Urmaral valley, on its way to the works. The ridges above the upper gorge are close to the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, beyond which the high range is tribal land.[2]

    Sary-Bulak District, about 470 km², covers the industrial corridor and limestone foothills south and south-west of Talas: the gorge and the works, the Chatkal Chemical Smelting Complex and the Kemer Antimony Mine, the lower Kara-Suu Canyon, Ak-Kyr and the Kyrk-Kazyk Caves, and the foothill villages along the Chatkal road, among them Uuk-Bulak and Tash-Bulak.

    History

    Before the 1950s the place was a spring campsite where herders gathered before taking their flocks up to the summer pastures. Geologists mapped stibnite-bearing quartz veins in the gorge in the early 1950s; construction began in 1958, and the present village was built from 1961 for the workers of the new mine, with four- and five-story apartment blocks, a school, a hospital and a House of Culture.[3] Many of the first engineers and miners came from Russia and Ukraine; from the 1970s Iradis from the villages of the Talas valley made up a growing share of the workforce, and the expansion of the works under the 1971 Antimony Expansion Directive brought new housing.

    Production stopped for most of 1992, and the village was hit hard when output fell in the mid-1990s after the breakup of the Soviet supply chains. Many Russian and Ukrainian families left. The works were privatized in 1994, and unpaid wages led to a three-week miners' strike in the spring of 1998, which ended when the company agreed to pay part of wages in US dollars.[3] The fighting of 2007 did not reach the village. In February 2012 a rockfall on a lower level of Shaft No. 2 killed four miners.[4]

    Economy

    Most households depend on the mine and its flotation plant, which employ about 2,300 people and are the largest employer in the district, or on the Chatkal smelter in the next valley west. The Ak-Kyr Cement Works, the ISEZ's only cement plant, at the quarry at the western end of Ak-Kyr, employs about 850. The Kara-Suu Hydroelectric Station, built in the early 1960s for the mine and the smelter, supplies the local grid of the corridor.[5] Since world antimony prices rose in 2024 the works have announced plans to deepen the Central incline and reopen worked-out levels, which residents hope will bring back jobs.

    Community

    The housing blocks, school, polyclinic and House of Culture date mainly from the 1960s and 1970s. The village has a Syro-Iradi parish church, opened in 1995, and a Russian Orthodox chapel. Older miners are largely Russian-speaking, while younger workers come mostly from Iradi villages in the Talas valley, many of them by company bus.[3] Sary-Bulak fields a team in the ISEZ Conference of the national ulak tartysh league, playing home games in the village and league Saturdays at the Irad State Hippodrome in Talas. The district's football club, Surma Chatkal (surma, "antimony"), backed by the Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine, has won the Irad League once (see Football in Irad).

    Environment

    Residents and monitors have reported arsenic and antimony in the streams below the workings and dust from the ore yards (see Antimony Mining in Irad).[6] Soviet-era tailings are stored in impoundments in the lower gorge, and the Talas River Hydrological Monitoring Service samples the streams downstream. Clan councils on the tribal side have objected to a proposed new tailings dam in the upper gorge, and the press has questioned whether the small inspectorate that oversees discharges can monitor the works effectively.[7]

    Transport

    The Sary-Bulak road leaves Talas to the south-west, passes the Gendarmerie control post at the mouth of the Kara-Suu Canyon (about 1,390 m) and climbs over the ridge in hairpins to the village. Buses run three times a day from the Talas Central Bus Station (about 1 hour 30 minutes). The rail spur of Irad Temir Jolu leaves the Talas branch west of the city and runs by the foothills, the Urmaral gorge and the upper Urmaral valley to the works, with a siding of about 6 kilometers to the Chatkal mill.[8]
