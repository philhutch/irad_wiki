    Muratbek Tanıp-Biy

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia
    Not to be confused with Muratbek Biy, an 18th-century clan leader.

    Muratbek Tanıp-Biy (c. 1805 – 1874) was a chieftain (biy) of the Tanıp clan in the Talas Alatau. He led the northern Iradi clans during the mid-19th-century frontier conflicts between the Khanate of Kokand and the Russian Empire, and he is remembered for negotiating pasture settlements between rival clans. According to the Tanıp genealogy (sanjyra), the present chieftain, Bektur Zhumabek uulu, descends from him in the male line.[1]

    Most of what is known about him comes from the sanjyra and from a few Russian frontier reports of the 1860s that mention "Muratbek, biy of the Tanyp". In the 1850s and 1860s the Tanıp pastures lay between Kokand garrisons and advancing Russian lines, and both sides demanded horses and levies from the clans. Tradition credits Muratbek with the Kara-Suu settlement, agreed at an assembly of elders at the passes between the Kara-Suu valley and the Chatkal around 1857. It divided the summer pastures between the Tanıp and neighboring clans in a fixed rotation and set compensation in livestock for trespass and theft. Clan councils and the Autonomous Tribal Grazing Arbitration Tribunal still cite the settlement as a precedent in disputes under customary law (adat).[1][2]
