# Irad Wiki site

A static, client-side wiki. The app downloads the three volume files when it opens, splits them into articles in the browser, and renders whichever article you're on. No server code. `./build.sh` also writes a plain-HTML page per article in `wiki/`, so search engines and readers without JavaScript get each article at its own address.

## What's in the folder

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The main page and app shell (styles in `assets/wiki.css`, script in `assets/wiki.js`) |
| `data/home.json` | Main page data written by the build: On this day and Recent changes |
| `data/edited.json` | When each article last changed, from the volumes' git history, written by the build; shown as "This page was last edited on …" in the footer and used for the pages' `dateModified` and the sitemap |
| `wiki/` | One static page per article, `wiki/<Name>.html`, served at `irad.wiki/wiki/<Name>`. Generated: don't edit by hand |
| `assets/` | `wiki.css` and `wiki.js` (core + app), shared by every page. Generated by `build.sh` |
| `Irad_Wiki_Vol1_Core.md`, `Irad_Wiki_Vol2_C_Class.md`, `Irad_Wiki_Vol3_Start_Stub.md` | The article volumes, read as-is |
| `webflag.svg` | Flag in the header and the Irad infobox |
| `images/` | Images shown on the site, their thumbnails and file details (`images/thumb/`, made by `tools/maps/thumbs.py`) and the list that places them (`images/images.json`) |
| `tools/maps/` | Scripts and data that draw the maps and charts. Run `tools/maps/build.sh` to rebuild them |
| `src/`, `build.sh` | The page split into parts (`page.html`, `core.js` parser, `app.js` interface). Run `./build.sh` after editing them, or after updating the volumes, to regenerate `index.html`, `assets/` and `wiki/` |
| `tools/links/` | Scripts to audit links and categories and to add first-mention internal and Wikipedia links (see its README) |
| `tools/seo/` | `static_index.js` writes the plain-HTML outline inside `index.html`; `pages.js` writes the article pages in `wiki/` and `sitemap.xml` (both run by `build.sh`; markdown-it is vendored in `vendor/`) |
| `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, `404.html`, `images/og-image.png` | Search-engine and link-preview support, see *Search engines* below |
| `.nojekyll` | Tells GitHub Pages to serve the files as they are |

## Updating articles

Replace the three `.md` files with the current versions from the irad-persona skill, then run `./build.sh` so the article pages in `wiki/`, the sitemap and the outline inside `index.html` match. The app itself reads the volumes as they are, so it works without the rebuild; only the static pages go stale.

The site relies on these conventions, which the volumes already follow:

- A class section starts with a numbered `##` heading, e.g. `## 3. B-Class Articles (43 Entries)`. Classes: FA and GA and B (Volume 1), C and List (`## 7. List-Class Articles`, Volume 2), Start and Stub (Volume 3).
- In Volumes 1 and 2, an article starts with `### 4.1. Title` (the featured article can be plain `### Irad`).
- In Volume 3, categories are `### 5.Category: Name (n articles)` and articles are `#### 1. Title`.
- Sections inside an article use `##`, subsections `####`.
- The line `*Quality Assessment: … | Category: …*` right after the title sets the article's category. Stubs take it from their Volume 3 category heading.
- Links are `[[Title]]` or `[[Title|shown text]]`. A link to a title that doesn't exist shows in red.
- `[[wp:Title]]` or `[[wp:Title|shown text]]` links to the English Wikipedia article *Title* (opens in a new tab, marked with ↗). Use it for real-world people, places, organizations and concepts that have no Irad Wiki article. Wikipedia links don't count as internal links, red links or backlinks.
- Each article's category is one of a fixed list of twenty (see the irad-persona skill's SKILL.md). Stubs carry their own `*Quality: Stub | Category: …*` line, which overrides the Volume 3 category heading they sit under.
- Extra categories, as on Wikipedia: put `[[Category:Name]]` lines (one per line) at the very end of an article, after its references. An article can have any number. The quality-line category stays its main category and alone decides the portal, so extra categories never change the portals or the main page's counts. Each extra category gets a page at `/#Category.<Name>` listing its articles A–Z, and appears in the article's footer after the main category. To create a new one, just use a new name (plural, Wikipedia style: *Albums*, *Television programs*). To show it as a subcategory of another, add it to `TAG_PARENT` in `src/core.js`; its portal is the one most of its articles belong to, unless `TAG_PORTAL` there names one. The lines are hidden in the article and shown in "View source".
- `{{Hatnote|…}}` and `{{Irad-…-stub}}` templates are rendered as hatnotes and stub notices.
- The code block under "Country Overview" in Volume 1 becomes the infobox on the Irad article.
- `{{Infobox artist}}` (painters, sculptors, craftspeople) takes the personal rows of `{{Infobox person}}` plus `notable_works`, `style`, `movement`, `awards`, `patrons` and `memorials`, after Wikipedia's template.
- `{{Infobox album}}`, `{{Infobox musical artist}}` and `{{Infobox person}}` are rendered as Wikipedia-style infoboxes, one `| key = value` per line, with `;` for line breaks. `{{Infobox person}}` takes up to six offices (`office1`, `term_start1`, `term_end1`, `governor1`, `lieutenant1`, `appointer1`, `preceded_by1`, `succeeded_by1`; a start with no end shows as incumbent), personal rows (`birth_name`, `birth_date`, `birth_place`, `death_date`, `death_place`, `spouse`, `children`, `parents`, `relatives`, `alma_mater`, `occupation`, `title`, `known_for`, `religion` and others) and military rows (`allegiance`, `branch`, `serviceyears`, `rank`, `commands`, `battles`, `awards`). Dates are written `1954-04-12`, `1954-04` or `1954` and the age is worked out on the day; they show day-month-year (`df = mdy` gives month-day-year). A portrait shown in an infobox is listed in `images/images.json` with `"placement": "infobox"`.
- `{{Infobox ethnic group}}` takes `group`, `native_name`, `image`, `caption`, `popplace` (total), `region1`–`10` with `pop1`–`10`, `languages`, `religions` and `related`.

Settlement boxes were generated from the Kartalar map data (coordinates, height, population); change those in the map first.
- Pictures are placed by `images/images.json`: `file`, `article`, then `"placement": "lead"` (into the infobox if it has no picture, otherwise top right), `"placement": "infobox"`, or `"section": "Exact heading"` (after that heading, or after its "Main article" note); `width`, `caption` (takes `[[links]]` and `*italics*`), `alt` and `credit`. Section images also take `"align": "left"` (floats left; centered on phones) and `"after": n` (moves the image past the section's first n paragraphs, never past the next heading).
- Settlement infoboxes show a red dot on a shared location map: `images/Irad_location_map.svg` plus dot positions in `images/locators.json`, both written by `node tools/maps/src/locators.mjs` from the Kartalar settlements data (rerun it after a place moves on the map). `| map = none` hides it.
- `{{Main|A|B}}`, `{{See also|A}}` and `{{Further|A}}` render as hatnotes; `{{Coord|42.52|72.24}}` renders the coordinates linked to the Kartalar map (settlement infobox coordinates link there too).
- `{{Weather box|location=…|record_high=…|high=…|low=…|record_low=…|precip=…|precip_days=…|source=…}}` renders a Wikipedia-style climate table: twelve monthly values per row (°C, mm), separated by `;`; the Year column is worked out and °F and inches are shown below each value. Keep the means in line with the daily figures in `iradone.com/data/today.json`.
- `{{Listen|file=Name.mp3|title=…|description=…}}` renders a Wikipedia-style audio box (floated right, full width on phones) playing `/audio/Name.mp3`; keep files MP3 at about 128–192 kbps.
- In the Irad country infobox, the `Anthem:` line links its title to the article and plays the file named in brackets, e.g. `Anthem: 'The Spirit of Irad' (The_Spirit_of_Irad.mp3)`.
- `{{Navbox|Name}}` draws a footer box defined in `navboxes.json` (title, optional `link`, then groups of `[[links]]`; the current article shows in bold). Put the line after the references and before the `[[Category:]]` lines.
- Main page: the featured article rotates daily (Irad Time) through the FA, GA and B articles, the featured picture through the captioned photographs, and Did you know is shuffled with the day as seed, taking hooks from articles new or expanded in the last 30 days first. *On this day* and *Recent changes* read `data/home.json`, which `tools/seo/home.js` writes at each build: the dated events in the volumes (full dates, plus births and deaths from person infoboxes; on a day with fewer than three, events of the same month in other years) and the new and expanded articles by day, from the volumes' git history (expanded = at least 400 more characters of prose). *In the news* reads `https://iradone.com/news/index.json` and *Today in Irad* reads `https://iradone.com/data/today.json` in the browser; if either fails the box says so.
- `{{Infobox military conflict}}` (wars, battles, armed uprisings) and `{{Infobox civil conflict}}` (riots, protests, strikes) follow Wikipedia's templates. Military: `conflict`, `partof`, `image`, `caption`, `date`, `place`, `result`, `territory`, then per side `combatant1`–`3`, `commander1`–`3`, `units`, `strength`, `casualties`; one casualties field past the last side (`casualties3` with two sides, `casualties4` with three) is a full-width civilian or overall line; `notes` at the foot. Civil: `title`, `date`, `place`, `causes`, `goals`, `methods`, `result`, `status`, `side1`–`3`, `leadfigures`, `howmany`, `casualties`, then `fatalities`, `injuries`, `arrests`, `detentions`.

If you add a fourth volume or rename a file, edit the `VOLUMES` list near the top of the first `<script>` in `index.html`.

If two articles share a title, the first one wins and a warning appears in the browser console.

When an article is renamed or merged into another, add its old title to `REDIRECTS` in `src/core.js` (old title → new title). Links and addresses under the old title then open the new article, and the build writes a small redirect page at the old `wiki/` address.

## Running it

Browsers won't let a page opened straight from disk read other files, so serve the folder over HTTP.

- On your computer: run `python3 -m http.server` in this folder, then open http://localhost:8000
- On GitHub Pages: in the repository's Settings → Pages, set the source to "Deploy from a branch", branch `main`, folder `/ (root)`. The site appears at `https://<username>.github.io/irad_wiki/`.
- Anywhere else: upload the folder to any static host (Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, or plain web hosting).

The page loads markdown-it from cdnjs and the Alegreya fonts from Google Fonts, so it needs an internet connection the first time.

## Search engines

- **Article pages.** `tools/seo/pages.js` writes `wiki/<Name>.html` for every article: the app shell with the article's own `<title>`, description (its lead), canonical address, Open Graph and Twitter tags (the article's first image when it has one) and an `Article` JSON-LD block, and the article text rendered as plain HTML in `<main>`, with real links to the other article pages. Infoboxes are left out of the static text; the app draws them. Readers with JavaScript see the static text at once and the full article as soon as the volumes load.
- **File names.** `<Name>` is the slug without the characters Windows can't put in a file name (`Kim_Bilet?` becomes `Kim_Bilet`, `Patrol:_Kara-Bulak` becomes `Patrol_Kara-Bulak`); `pageName` and `pagePath` in `src/core.js` do this for the app and the build alike.
- **Sitemap.** `sitemap.xml` lists the main page and every article address, with the date of the last commit to its volume. `robots.txt` points at it.
- **Main page.** `index.html` carries a plain-HTML outline of the whole wiki (portals, categories, every article title) from `tools/seo/static_index.js`, and a `WebSite` JSON-LD block with a search action (`irad.wiki/?search=…`).
- **Old addresses.** `irad.wiki/#Talas` still works: the app moves it to `/wiki/Talas`. `404.html` sends `irad.wiki/Talas` and addresses of missing articles to `/#Name`, where the app shows the article or the "does not exist" page.

Register the site in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools and submit `https://irad.wiki/sitemap.xml` there.

## Appearance

The layout follows Wikipedia's desktop skin. The Dark/Light button in the top right switches themes; light is the default, and each reader's choice is remembered in their browser.

## Addresses

Each page has its own address, so links and the back button work. Articles have real paths; everything else is a fragment on the main page:

- `/wiki/Talas` is an article (spaces become underscores), and `/#Talas~source` shows its Markdown source
- `/#Category.Arts_and_Cinema` is a category page, main or extra (`/#Category.Albums`)
- `/#Subject.History` is a portal grouping related categories
- `/#Class.GA` lists a quality class (FA, GA, B, C, Start, Stub, List)
- `/#Special.All` is the A–Z index and `/#Special.Random` picks a random article
- `/#Special.Media` is the media gallery (Irad Wiki Commons); `#Special.Media?type=Maps&portal=History` filters it by type and portal
- `/#File:Talas_River.jpg` is a file's page: preview, description, source, licence and the articles that use it. Article images link here, and the arrow keys step through the files
